Micro and nano payments — 2025, revisited

First published 2 April 2025 on docs.diniscruz.ai by Dinis Cruz and ChatGPT Deep Research · 10,813 words · original PDF · LinkedIn

Source licence: CC0 1.0 Universal (docs.diniscruz.ai). This page: CC BY 4.0. Curation: synthesised — this is newsroom.sgit.ai's own summary and framing of the original, not a verbatim republication; the full 10,813-word text has not been reproduced here. Republished-as-pointer 23 August 2026.

The largest single treatment of news monetisation anywhere in either corpus, and the earliest by over a year: published 2 April 2025, fifteen months before the x402 payment rail this argument depended on actually existed. Read the original in full at the link above; this page states what it argued and pairs it, deliberately, with what has since become real.

What the original argued

The piece made the case for micro- and nano-scale payments as the mechanism that could let news monetise at the granularity of a single fact or a single article read, rather than only at the scale of a subscription or a full-page advertisement. Written well before any production-grade rail existed to carry payments that small economically, it argued the case on the economics and the reader behaviour, not on any specific implementation available at the time.

The pairing that demonstrates the site's own thesis

This page is deliberately paired with the 2026 payment-rails page. The original argument is unedited at its source — nothing about it has been rewritten to look more prescient in hindsight. What is new is that x402 now exists, settles in ~200ms, and charges zero protocol fees: the mechanism the 2025 argument needed has since arrived. That is this site's own central argument about correction-propagation, applied to its own history rather than to somebody else's mistake: the original stays where it is: the newer knowledge attaches alongside it rather than overwriting it.

Why this page is not a verbatim republication

Per this site's provenance contract, a page must state its curation honestly. This site's brief pack recorded the original's title, date, authors, word count and theme in full, but not its complete text. Rather than fabricate a verbatim reproduction from that metadata, this page states plainly that it is a synthesis and points to the real original for the full argument. Synthesised is not a lesser status; it is a different claim about the text — and a false claim to verbatim reproduction would be exactly the provenance failure this site exists to argue against.

For an agent

This page is a synthesis, not the source. For the complete original text, fetch the canonical URL directly rather than treating this page's summary as a substitute for it.
