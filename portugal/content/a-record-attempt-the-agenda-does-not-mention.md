## What the press says is happening on Wednesday

On 29 July 2026 two Portuguese outlets published the same story on the same day. AICEP
Portugal Global, the state trade and investment agency, and Portugal Business News both
reported that Startup Summit Lisbon would host a **48-hour non-stop startup pitch marathon**
as an official attempt on a Guinness World Records title, with almost a thousand founders
taking part, remotely or in person at Unicorn Factory Lisboa's Beato Innovation District, on
**16–17 September 2026**.

Both pages are frozen in this repository with their hashes. Neither is quoted here; the links
are in the table at the foot of this page and the wording is theirs.

## What the event's own agenda says

The event's agenda page, frozen on 13 September, lists a Welcome Drinks on the evening of the
16th, a Morning Run at 07:30 on the 17th, an Opening Keynote at 09:30, four named sessions
across two stages, lunch, investor office hours and a Founder Social.

It does not list a pitch marathon. It does not mention a record attempt. The word Guinness does
not appear on it.

> The page describes itself as a summarised view and says more sessions, speakers and exact
> timings are being announced weekly.

So the honest description is not "the agenda contradicts the press". It is: **the two
pieces of coverage describe a headline event, and the event's own published programme, as of
four days before it, does not carry it.** Those are compatible. A summarised agenda can omit
things. A record attempt run alongside the summit rather than inside it might live on a
different page. This publication does not know which, and says so.

## One page, two dates

There is a smaller wrinkle inside one of the two reports. Portugal Business News' summary line
dates the marathon to 17 September; the body of the same article dates it to 16–17 September.
Both are on one page, frozen with one hash. AICEP says 16–17 throughout.

That is the kind of thing a reader never notices, because nobody reads a summary line and a
body paragraph against each other. A graph layer notices, because it extracts a date from
each and finds two.

## Why this is the third story and not the first

The first story on this site was about a list that changed. The second was about two names
for the same three stages. This one is about the gap between what the press says will
happen and what the programme says will happen.

All three have the same shape, and it is deliberate: **publish the record, never the
verdict, and no adjective about a named party.** None of them says the event is disorganised.
None says the press got it wrong. Each says: here are two published things, here is where
they differ, here are the frozen copies, decide for yourself.

It is also the first story that stands on sources this publication did not get from the
event itself — which matters, because until now every source in the register had one
publisher. Two Portuguese outlets and a state agency are now in it, and the graph has a
`Coverage` type with a `covers` edge whose inverse is `covered_by`, because a path from a
news article to the event it is about should read as a sentence in either direction.
