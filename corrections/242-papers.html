242 papers

The 10,000-hours case is one claim, followed by hand. This is the same failure mode measured at network scale: one biomedical belief, traced through its entire citation network.

242papers citing the beliefmeasured
675citations across those papersmeasured
>220,000supporting citation pathsmeasured

Four named mechanisms of distortion

MechanismWhat it does
Citation biasPapers selectively cite sources that support the claim already being made, rather than a representative sample of the evidence
AmplificationEach new citation adds apparent weight without adding independent evidence — a chain of papers citing each other reads, from outside, as many independent confirmations
InventionA hypothesis is converted into a stated fact purely by the act of being cited as one, with no new evidence produced at any step
Citation diversionA later commentary's addition: a citation is used to support a claim the cited work does not actually make, redirecting the reader's trust toward a conclusion the original never reached

The distortions did not stay academic. They extended into grant applications — funding decisions made partly on the strength of a citation network that, traced back, terminates in nothing. The underlying source data is publicly citable: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19622839.

Why this is the site's argument, not a side note

A single claim like the 10,000-hours figure is a story a reader can hold in their head. A 242-paper citation network is not — nobody reads 675 citations to check whether they actually support what they are cited for. That is exactly the case a graph is built for: typed citation edges mean the amplification and the invention are visible in the structure itself, not something a reader has to reconstruct by hand from 242 separate PDFs.

For an agent

Citation count and citation independence are different measurements. A claim cited 675 times by 242 papers that mostly cite each other is not 675 times more supported than a claim cited once by an independent source — see independence, not count. Before treating a heavily-cited claim as well-established, check whether the citations are independent or amplifying.
