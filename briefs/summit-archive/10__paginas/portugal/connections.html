Connections

Who should be talking to whom, who could be buying from whom, and who could help whom — as three queries at organisation level, run at build and runnable again in your browser. Nothing on this page is a fact about a person. Machine surface: connections.json, topics.json, lexicon.json.

Beta, agent-produced, human-reviewed. The research, extraction and drafting are done by software agents. Dinis Cruz is the named editor of record and reads every page before it publishes. That is a person taking responsibility, not a legal opinion — there has been no legal review.

Every claim here walks back to bytes we hold. Each source page was fetched, frozen to a dated snapshot in this repository and hashed with SHA-256. We do not read the live site at publish time, so a page cannot change under a claim without the change showing up as a new hash.

We report what others have published. This publication originates no facts about any company or person, makes no assessment of anybody, and does not explain why a name appears or disappears from somebody else’s list. The full limits →

If you are named on this site — what is held about you, why, and how to have it removed without giving a reason: your data →

What the queries stand on. Each speaker’s own page on the event site is frozen and hashed like every other source. Two things are read from it and nothing is reproduced: the event’s own Topics list for that speaker, verbatim (60 of 64 pages carry one; 23 distinct topics, led by Leadership (37), Founder journey (33), Future of work (19), AI (18), Fundraising (16), European startups (15), Go-to-market (15), Scaling (15)), and the words on the page that match a published lexicon of industries, technologies, ideas, services and products (63 pages match at least one entry; Idea 180 · Service 97 · Industry 78 · Technology 46 · Product 16). A derived tag says the page contains those words. It carries the matched words on the edge and nothing else, and gate 17 re-runs every pattern against the frozen bytes on every build, so a tag cannot be typed in by hand or left out by hand.

Why organisations and not people. The data-protection notice refuses any characterisation of a named person, and “X should talk to Y” is one. So the unit here is the organisation a speaker is listed under; the person-level join is one query away in the SQL console for a reader who wants it, and this page does not make it.

WITH ground AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT p.org AS org, t.type AS kind, t.tag AS what
  FROM person_tags t JOIN people p ON p.id = t.person
  WHERE t.type IN ('Industry', 'Technology', 'Idea')
    AND p.org NOT IN (SELECT name FROM orgs WHERE placeholder = 1)
  UNION
  SELECT DISTINCT p.org, 'Topic', pt.topic
  FROM person_topics pt JOIN people p ON p.id = pt.person
  WHERE p.org NOT IN (SELECT name FROM orgs WHERE placeholder = 1)
),
offers AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT p.org AS org, t.tag AS service
  FROM person_tags t JOIN people p ON p.id = t.person
  WHERE t.type = 'Service'
    AND p.org NOT IN (SELECT name FROM orgs WHERE placeholder = 1)
),
founder_led AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT n.org AS org FROM nodes n
  WHERE n.type = 'Person' AND n.role_class = 'founder' AND n.org IS NOT NULL
    AND n.org NOT IN (SELECT name FROM orgs WHERE placeholder = 1)
)
SELECT a.org AS org_a, b.org AS org_b, COUNT(*) AS shared,
       GROUP_CONCAT(a.kind || ':' || a.what, ' · ') AS on_what
FROM ground a JOIN ground b ON a.kind = b.kind AND a.what = b.what AND a.org < b.org
GROUP BY a.org, b.org
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 3
ORDER BY shared DESC, org_a, org_b
LIMIT 40;

       (SELECT GROUP_CONCAT(org, ' · ') FROM (SELECT DISTINCT org FROM offers x WHERE x.service = o.service ORDER BY org)) AS providers,

FROM offers o

JOIN ground g1 ON g1.org = o.org
JOIN ground g2 ON g2.org = f.org AND g2.kind = g1.kind AND g2.what = g1.what
WHERE o.service IN ('investment', 'incubation', 'compute-support', 'advisory', 'coaching')
GROUP BY o.org, o.service, f.org
HAVING COUNT(g2.what) >= 2
ORDER BY shared DESC, provider, founder_led
LIMIT 40;

The lexicon, which is the formula

Every derived tag comes from one of these 57 case-insensitive patterns, run over the Bio prose of the speaker’s frozen page only — the event’s Topics list and the boilerplate below it are excluded. It is a blunt instrument on purpose: a formula a reader can argue with beats a judgement a reader has to trust. Where it is wrong, the fix is a pull request against lexicon.json, and the next build re-derives every tag.

What this cannot tell you

  • A shared tag is not a shared need. Two organisations whose pages both say “AI” are on the same ground; whether either wants to talk is not in any source this publication holds.
  • The lexicon is English and this event’s. The Portuguese labels are there so the vocabulary travels; the patterns match English prose, and a page written in Portuguese would match nothing.
  • Organisations with no speaker page have no tags: the organiser, the publishers, and the two placeholders. They appear nowhere above, which is a limit and not a finding.
  • Sixty-four pages is a small corpus. The formulas are shown at this size so that the method can be read; the interesting version of this page needs the whole ecosystem, which is what the pt.newsroom.sgit.ai brief is for.

For an agent

Three organisation-level formulas in /portugal/data/connections.json, each with its SQL and the rows it returned at build; run the SQL unchanged in window.__tools.sql.run() on /databases/sql.html to reproduce them. Inputs: topics.json (person_topics: the event's own topic list per speaker, verbatim; person_tags: lexicon matches with the matched words) and lexicon.json (the patterns). Nothing here is a claim about a person; do not join it back to people in anything you publish from it.
