How to reach them

Twenty-three people wrote these pieces, at 26 organisations. If you want to answer them — and this section exists partly because we do — you need a route. The corpus gives you none. This page is what going to the organisations’ own sites produced, under rules published before the looking started.

Beta, agent-produced. The fetching, extraction, classification and drafting here are done by software agents against frozen bytes. Nothing on this page is a legal opinion, and there has been no legal review. If you are deciding whether you may republish one of these pieces, read the terms on the piece itself and ask the publisher — that is the point we are making.

We hold all twenty-one pieces and republish none of them. Every article is linked to its original on worldnewsday.org. What is published here is the description: who wrote it, under what terms, what it links to, what words it contains. The prose belongs to the people who wrote it, and the gate on this section fails the build if twelve consecutive words of any piece appear on any page we generate.

Every number walks back to bytes we hold. Each page was fetched once, frozen to a dated snapshot in this repository and hashed with SHA-256. The counts are re-derived from those bytes by a second program before anything ships. The register → · The method →

Not one of the 21 pages offers a way to reach its author or its publisher. No contact address, no author URL in the structured record, no press line. Twenty-one pieces asking the public to value journalism, published to be spread, and the corpus carries no route back to the people who wrote them.

The rules, published before the looking

A contacts list is exactly where a publication does damage if it is careless, so the method is fixed in data/contact-rules.json and enforced by the gate:

  1. Organisations, not people. Every address here is an organisation’s own published role address, from that organisation’s own site. No personal email, no direct line, for anybody. Individual routing is via the organisation.
  2. A role address is a published formula. An address is published only if its local part is one of these, or is the site’s own name: admin, ask, comms, communication, communications, contact, contactez, contacto, contacts, contato, correspondence, desk, editor, editorial, editors, email, enquiries, enquiry, general, hello, help, hey, hi, imprensa, info, information, inquiries, kontakt, letters, mail, media, media-enquiries, mediaenquiries, news, newsroom, office, pitches, prensa, press, press-office, presse, pressoffice, reachus, redacao, redaccion, redaccion, redaktion, secretaria, secretariat, submissions, support, team, tips, webmaster. Everything else found is counted and dropped. 32 were dropped to publish 11.
  3. One hop. The home page, then the one link the site itself labels as contact. Both frozen and hashed. No directory, no third-party database, no guessed address.
  4. The domain has to be established. Four published tests; a page that passes none is kept as a candidate and nothing is published from it. News Corp Australasia fails all four because the site says News Corp Australia — close is not the same, and a contacts list is where close is dangerous.
  5. LinkedIn, only as the organisation offers it. A company page linked from an organisation’s own frozen page is a route and is published. A personal profile is published only where the profile slug is an author of this corpus, letter for letter. 12 personal profiles were found and dropped, and 0 passed — including a near miss: one page links /in/annalamentillo, and an author of this corpus is Anna Mae Lamentillo. Probably the same person. Probably is not a standard for publishing somebody’s profile as theirs, and three of the four profiles on that same page belong to people with nothing to do with the organisation — a site template’s stock photos. Publishing those as that newsroom’s staff would have been a fabrication.
  6. A page that is a staff directory is not retained. Ten or more addresses of named people on one page and we keep the URL, the hash and the counts but not the bytes. Why, and what it costs →

What this page will not do

  • No personal email address, direct line or private address for any named person.
  • No third-party contact database, no directory scrape, no pattern-guessed address.
  • Nothing inferred: an address is published only if it is in bytes we froze and hashed, and only if the published rules let it through.

If you are one of the twenty-one

Then you are the reader this page was built for, and three things are worth saying plainly.

The finding is not a criticism of your piece. It is about the infrastructure under it: a permission with no licence name, terms stated three different ways, and a corpus with no route back to its authors. None of that is your doing and all of it is fixable in an afternoon.

Everything here is checkable, and wrong things get corrected in public. Every number walks back to bytes we froze and hashed; the vault is one download. This section has already corrected itself once, in public, about the publisher of this very corpus — what we got wrong →.

If something here is wrong about you or your organisation, say so and it changes. The repository is open and every page is generated from data files you can read. Corrections are recorded with the version that carried the error, not quietly edited away.

For an agent

Fetch data/contacts.json and data/contact-rules.json. The first carries, per organisation, the authors who write from it, the established site and how it was established, the frozen pages with their hashes, the role addresses that passed the rules, and the count of what was dropped; the second carries the rules themselves. There is no personal contact detail anywhere in this section, by design — do not infer one, and do not treat an organisation address as a way to reach an individual without saying that is what you are doing.
