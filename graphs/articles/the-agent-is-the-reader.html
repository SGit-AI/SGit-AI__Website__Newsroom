Facts (11)

  • factThe readers of the site are increasingly agents sent by people, and an agent that reads the web today pays to process all of it and then guesses at what it means.“The readers of this site are increasingly not people. They are agents, sent by people.” § top
    The agent-readable web 24The SGit Newsroom 72
  • factThe graph's answer is better as well as cheaper, because every statement arrives with its anchor so the agent can quote and cite without a second pass.“every statement arrives with its anchor, so the agent can quote and cite without a second pass” § layers
    Provenance and evidence 99The agent-readable web 24
  • factMaking the map turned out to be the verification, and the yield falls with each pass, which is why every role has a failure sentence, a word limit and a stopping rule.“Making the map turned out to be the verification.” § loop
    Multi-agent designs 62Provenance and evidence 99
  • factCompression with scale is a property of curation, not of graphs, which strengthens the economic argument because curation is work that can be paid for.“Compression with scale is not a property of graphs. It is a property of curation.” § compress
    Fractal semantic graphs 62Micropayments and the reading meter 41
  • factA textbook PageRank over the site's links puts two articles that only cite each other at the top; the Cartographer's 24 typed verbs, each with an inverse and citing its items, say what the link means.“That is the difference between a web of links and a web of meaning.” § meaning
    Meaning through connectivity 24Google 40Fractal semantic graphs 62
  • factAn agent is a reader with a persona too, usually a sharper one because it arrives with an objective, and the persona is worth a smaller, better context.“An agent is a reader with a persona too, and usually with a sharper one, because it arrives with an objective.” § personas
    Personas and personalisation 33The agent-readable web 24
  • factA session summarising its own context is compression without knowing what matters, free and lossy; the readers do the same operation curated, checked and with provenance.“The first is free and lossy. The second is worth paying for.” § personas
    Memory and context 46Provenance and evidence 99
  • factThe payment rails exist: x402 moved by Coinbase into a Linux Foundation project in April 2026, Stripe's per-request charging previewed in February, Cloudflare's pay per crawl, and the site's reading meter; what is missing is demand.“The payment rails are no longer the obstacle.” § economics

Hypotheses (2)

  • hypothesisThe more content there is, the more it compresses, because the graphs reuse each other and each new article adds fewer new concepts and more connections.“the more content there is, the more it compresses, because the graphs reuse each other” § compress
    Fractal semantic graphs 62The agent-readable web 24
  • hypothesisWikipedia is the organisation best placed to test this: selling the meaning per question to every agent would turn a cost carried by donors into revenue that grows with reading.“the organisation best placed to test this is Wikipedia” § wikipedia
    Wikipedia 4Micropayments and the reading meter 41

Proposals (3)

  • proposalMake the stopping rule a number: stop a pass when it costs more per finding than the finding is worth.“stop a pass when it costs more per finding than the finding is worth” § loop
    Multi-agent designs 62Pricing and economics 61
  • proposalA signed claim: the publisher's agent re-checks a claim against its anchor and sources and returns a statement signed with the publisher's key, true as of a date and vouched for until an expiry, renewed for a fee.“true as we understand it on 11 October 2026, and we vouch for it until 11 April 2027” § claims
    Provenance and evidence 99Encryption and keys 70Micropayments and the reading meter 41
