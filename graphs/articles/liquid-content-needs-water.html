Hypotheses (5)

  • hypothesisIf content is to be liquid it needs water in it, and the water is not the format but the reporting: the notes, the calls, the documents, the half-recorded interviews, the hypotheses, the facts with the doubts still attached.“If content is to be liquid, it needs water in it, and the water is not the format. It is the reporting” § top
    Story vaults and liquid content 15Journalism and the news business 21
  • hypothesisThe guide's call for a complete reinvention of newsroom norms is a known trap; the method already exists in decades of journalistic practice, and the owner would bring back the experienced journalists and give them the tools.“The more I think about this, the more I think we already have the method.” § top
    Journalism and the news business 21Story vaults and liquid content 15Publisher 17
  • hypothesisThe right picture is not AI writing everything with the journalist feeding it facts; writing the article is how the journalist discovers the narrative, a feedback loop between gathering evidence and writing.“Part of the skill of a journalist is writing the article, because writing it is how they discover the narrative.” § top
    Journalism and the news business 21Story vaults and liquid content 15
  • hypothesisWith a graph that records who contributed which claim it becomes economic to bring in a historian, a librarian, a data scientist or a lawyer for an hour or a story and pay them for their time and the credibility they lend, which accumulates.“it becomes economic to bring them in for an hour, a question or a story, and to pay them” § top
    Story vaults and liquid content 15Micropayments and the reading meter 41Provenance and evidence 99
  • hypothesisPersonalisation should be neither static nor statistical: it is the intersection of two graphs, the reader's, holding role, company, sector, time, depth and language, and the story's.“It is the intersection of two graphs, the reader's and the story's.” § top
    Personas and personalisation 33Story vaults and liquid content 15

Proposals (3)

  • proposalLiquefying the notebook means capturing what the journalist already produces in the way they produce it: handwritten notes photographed and transcribed, calls recorded and linked, claims extracted into a graph the journalist can see and correct.“Liquefying it does not mean asking the journalist to fill in forms.” § top
    Story vaults and liquid content 15Publisher 17
  • proposalA graph that names every claim and source adds ways of being paid that do not depend on attention: pennies per use, an agent paying per question, institutions licensing the evidence, and a share of every use walking back to the journalist and contributors.“a share of every use walking back to the journalist and to the contributors whose evidence the claim stands on” § top
    Micropayments and the reading meter 41Story vaults and liquid content 15Pricing and economics 61
  • proposalStart with one journalist: pick one topic and its reporter, capture their notebook as a graph they can correct, build them a desk, bring in one paid expert, publish their article and one other projection, measure, then liquefy the next story.“I would end with one journalist. Pick one topic and the reporter who knows it best.” § top
    Story vaults and liquid content 15Complexity and shipping 60Publisher 17

Definitions (1)

  • definitionThe guide defines liquid content as content that primarily consists of datafied components, or atomic objects, such as quotes, events or dates, rather than information already synthesised into an article, podcast or video segment.“today publishers mould content like clay into a finished product and let it dry, and liquid content keeps the clay wet” § top
