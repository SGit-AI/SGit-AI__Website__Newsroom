Facts (18)

  • factInnovation needs constraints and the loop of trying, failing and shipping, and the constraint that shaped sgit most was the encryption.“the constraint that shaped sgit most was the encryption” § mistake

  • factThe research is more careful than the slogan: a review by Acar, Tarakci and van Knippenberg found an inverted U-shaped effect of constraints on creativity and innovation, so some constraint helps and too much hurts.“an inverted U-shaped effect of constraints on creativity and innovation” § mistake
    The value of constraints
  • factEncryption on the client moves the security problem from protecting all the data, every copy, backup, log and replica, to protecting the keys, which is a much smaller problem.“the problem moves to protecting the keys, which is a much smaller problem” § keys
    Encryption and keys 70Client-side and on-device 56
  • factThe sovereignty line stops being where the data is stored and becomes who holds the keys, so data can sit with a provider or in a country with sovereignty gaps and you can still move it.“The sovereignty line stops being where the data is stored and becomes who holds the keys.” § keys
    Privacy and sovereignty 45Encryption and keys 70
  • factThe European Data Protection Board's recommendations on transfers take exactly this case and conclude that strong encryption with keys retained solely under the exporter's control is an effective supplementary measure.“the EDPB considers that the encryption performed provides an effective supplementary measure.” § keys
    European Data Protection Board 3Regulation and law 32The European Union 19Privacy and sovereignty 45
  • factThe memo said that with the data encrypted you do not have to worry about GDPR; that is too strong, and the article corrects it.“That is too strong, and the article should say so.” § gdpr
    GDPR 8Regulation and law 32Corrections must propagate 47
  • factFor whoever holds the keys, encrypted personal data is still personal data: the UK regulator says not to think encrypted data is not subject to the UK GDPR, storing it is processing, and the controller's duties stay.“and that storing it is still processing. The controller's duties stay.” § gdpr
    ICO 3GDPR 8The United Kingdom 19
  • factThe European Court of Justice held in September 2025 that pseudonymised data is not personal data in all cases and for every person, which may help a store that never has the keys, but that store still processes data under a contract.“but that store still processes data on someone's behalf, under a contract” § gdpr
    Court of Justice of the European Union 2GDPR 8Regulation and law 32
  • factThe biggest gain is engineering: a server that cannot read the data removes both the possibility and the temptation of processing on it, so there is no server code, no complexity, no deployment, nothing to patch.“once nothing can run on the server, there is no server code: no complexity there, no deployment, nothing to patch” § server
    The value of constraintsClient-side and on-device 56Deployment and self-hosting 21

  • factThe author knows the temptation because he has fallen for it: a developer makes a change in the cheapest place, which looks like the server, until it has to be maintained.“I know the temptation well, because I have fallen for it.” § server
    The value of constraintsDinis Cruz 126
  • factSome of sgit's most powerful features exist because the constraint had to be solved in version control: clone --path holds only the folders asked for, and with immutable encrypted objects a read is a plain GET any static host can serve.“With the objects immutable and encrypted, a read is a plain GET, so any static host can serve a vault.” § server
    sgit 91Vault design 48Deployment and self-hosting 21The value of constraints
  • factOpen source removes the temptation to lock the customer in; the best product is not the one you use because you have to but the one that hurts when you stop.“The best product is not the one you use because you have to; it is the one that hurts when you stop.” § open

  • factThe same is true of design: take things away until it hurts and stop there, because past that point it gets worse; sometimes that is one button and sometimes fifty.“take things away until it hurts, and stop there, because past that point it gets worse” § open
    The value of constraintsThe code you do not ship 18
  • factYou cannot open source half of it: a community edition that turns proprietary as soon as you use it for real is an evaluation version with a better name, and brings back every dependency open source was meant to remove.“A community edition that turns proprietary as soon as you use it for real is an evaluation version with a better name.” § all
    Open source and open licences 53

    Elastic 2HashiCorp 3Redis 2Open source and open licences 53Open Source Initiative 2

Data points (1)

  • data-pointThe GDPR lists encryption as a security measure in Article 32, and under Article 34(3)(a) a breach need not be communicated to the people affected if the data was made unintelligible, provided the key was not lost with it. 34 GDPR article whose paragraph 3(a) lifts the duty to notify people when the data was unintelligible“provided the key was not lost with it” § gdpr
    GDPR 8Encryption and keys 70Regulation and law 32

Hypotheses (3)

  • hypothesisNobody will buy sgit because it is encrypted; if it succeeds it will be for better features, better workflows and a better solution, and leading with encryption was the author's own mistake.“I do not think anyone will buy sgit because it is encrypted.” § mistake
    sgit 91Encryption and keys 70Strategy and positioning 26
  • hypothesisThe curse of most startups is customers getting them to build features and integrations only they need until the roadmap belongs to everyone else; open source answers with the code, integrate it yourself or pay us for that thing.“here is the code; integrate it yourself, or pay us to do that specific thing” § open
    Startups and early access 34Open source and open licences 53
  • hypothesisWorking in the open with no private code to protect and no lock-in to defend is more efficient, and is part of how very small teams can now build and maintain software that companies with billions cannot.“Working in the open, with no private code to protect and no lock-in to defend, is simply more efficient” § all
    Open source and open licences 53Startups and early access 34

Definitions (1)

  • definitionOpen source is the second constraint: there is no private code, so the code is available everywhere and easy to deploy, run and change, including by the customer, which solves many sovereignty problems on its own.“The constraint is that there is no private code.” § open
    Open source and open licences 53The value of constraintsPrivacy and sovereignty 45
