Facts (13)

    Privacy and sovereignty 45Europe 6Strategy and positioning 26
  • factBuilding frontier capacity takes years and capital that only benefits those who already have the infrastructure, and most real work does not need a frontier model.“Very few use cases need the frontier” § ball
    Strategy and positioning 26Pricing and economics 61
  • factMost organisations cannot move, not between clouds and often not within the same hyperscaler, because the infrastructure was hard-coded, created by clicks and never deployed in a second place.“The infrastructure was hard-coded, created by clicks, built up over generations of changes, and never deployed in a second place.” § move
    Deployment and self-hosting 21Incidents and resilience 25
  • factThe gap is an engineering gap, not a policy gap, and a supplier disruption that stops a business is critical whether or not it is national critical infrastructure.“That is an engineering gap, not a policy gap” § move

    Wardley maps 24Deployment and self-hosting 21

Hypotheses (2)

  • hypothesisThe engineering benefits of being able to move, reproducible deployments and understood dependencies, will pay for much of the cost even if the alternative runs a little more expensively.“end up paying for much of it, even if the main path stays optimised on the incumbent” § possible
    Pricing and economics 61Deployment and self-hosting 21
  • hypothesisEurope's gap is not startups or talent but a commercial open source model that connects many small players into a bigger play.“The gap is positioning, and it is a commercial open source model that Europe never quite developed” § open

Proposals (4)

  • proposalDraw the Wardley map and ask of each dot you do not control: take it away, what happens; follow the edges up to see the blast radius.“take it away, what happens?” § dot
    Wardley maps 24Blast radius 36Threat modelling 15
  • proposalA maturity model for every dot: from 0, it stops, through the data can leave, it runs somewhere once, it runs for the minimum viable company, to 4, it moves when you decide.“A maturity model for every dot you do not control.” § zero
    Minimum viable companyWardley maps 24
  • proposalRun the twins in disposable QA environments all the time, so the business proves every week it can run without the dots it does not control and finds problems on a Tuesday, not in an outage.“Every time something breaks, you have found it on a Tuesday, not during an outage.” § qa
    Connectors and twins 25Complexity and shipping 60Incidents and resilience 25
  • proposalSome twins should be provided centrally, by governments, industry bodies or the larger organisations in a supply chain, as a road: built once, open, used by everyone.“built once, open, used by everyone, and not a central project that becomes another dependency” § central
    Supply chains 22Open source and open licences 53Deployment and self-hosting 21

Definitions (2)

  • definitionSovereignty is the ability to move: agility, almost liquidity, moving a workload, data and an agent's actions from one provider to another when you decide.“sovereignty is mostly agility, almost liquidity: the ability to move a workload and keep operating” § move
    Privacy and sovereignty 45Deployment and self-hosting 21
  • definitionThe minimum viable company, from the resilience world, is the smallest version of the business that can keep serving customers while the rest recovers.“the smallest version of the business that can keep serving customers while the rest recovers” § zero
