  • factThe term open source was proposed by Christine Peterson in February 1998 after Netscape released its browser's source; the OSI was founded that month by Bruce Perens and Eric Raymond with a definition from the Debian guidelines.“It was proposed by Christine Peterson at a meeting in February 1998, after Netscape decided to release its browser's source” § history
    Christine PetersonNetscapeOpen Source Initiative 2Bruce PerensEric S. Raymond 2Debian

    LinuxLinus TorvaldsGNU General Public LicenseCorrections must propagate 47

  • factA small group of maintainers decides what goes into a project; contributions are reviewed, tested, signed off and often refused, and anybody can fork their own copy but nobody can change yours.“Anybody can fork a project and change their own copy; nobody can change yours without you.” § control
    Open source and open licences 53Provenance and evidence 99
  • factThe control over an open source project is real; it is just visible, which is a strength, because you can see exactly who decides and what they accepted.“The control is real. It is just visible, which is a strength” § control
    Open source and open licences 53
  • factApart from a very small minority of popular projects, almost every open source project is run by a small number of people, which is the normal shape of it, not a failure.“That is not a failure of open source; it is the normal shape of it.” § contribute

  • factOpen source is neither dramatically more nor less secure than proprietary software, because openness is not the metric; the metric is whether somebody had the time, and was paid, to review it.“Open source is not dramatically more secure or less secure than proprietary software, because openness is not the metric.” § security

    Open Source Initiative 2Open source and open licences 53

  • factEven open source has to be hosted somewhere and distributed through somebody, which is itself a point of control; the idea of a free-for-all without any control is not true.“even open source has to be hosted somewhere and distributed through somebody, which is itself a point of control” § fud
    Open source and open licences 53Supply chains 22
  • factThe power of open source has nothing to do with contributions: it is that you are not locked in, and can read it, run it, fix it and move it if the company behind it is sold, changes licence or disappears.“The power is that you are not locked in.” § power
    No lock-inOpen source and open licences 53
  • factWith open source you get the whole development and technology workflow: the history, the tests, the build and the reasoning in the commits, as valuable as the code.“the history, the tests, the build, the reasoning in the commits, all of which are as valuable as the code” § power
    No lock-inThe record 57

Definitions (1)

  • definitionOpen source is a copyright licence: the holder uses the right to control copying to grant copying, use, change and sharing on stated terms; it is not the absence of copyright.“It is a copyright licence. It is not the absence of copyright.” § history
