Hypotheses (4)

  • hypothesisDiversity of models is half of it; the other half is diversity of context, because a second model that has read the same poisoned email as the first is not independent however different its weights.“A second model that has read the same poisoned email as the first is not independent, however different its weights are.” § seven
    Prompt injection 26Human in the loop 59Memory and context 46
  • hypothesisA brake is only as good as the answers to who holds it, how long it takes, what it breaks and what it cannot reverse; containment without recoverability is a brake on a car with no reverse gear.“Containment without recoverability is a brake on a car with no reverse gear.” § seven
    Incidents and resilience 25One-way door 18
  • hypothesisFive things his list leaves out decide whether the principles work: the mandate, the deployment rather than the model, a named owner and an expiry, recoverability, and speed.“Read against our work, five things are missing, and each of them decides whether the seven work in practice.” § gaps
    Authority outside the modelBehaviour policy 91Risk acceptance 37
  • hypothesisHis last line is that the most trustworthy system lets us trust the model least; the other half is that once you know how little you have to trust it, you can let it do a great deal more.“once you know exactly how little you have to trust it, you can let it do a great deal more.” § start

Proposals (3)

  • proposalThe mandate should be locked under change control before the agent runs, so an auditor compares the footprint with a commitment, not a story, and the second reader's sources should come from somewhere the drafter cannot write.“so that an auditor compares the footprint with a commitment, not with a story.” § seven
    Footprint 22Provenance and evidence 99Behaviour policy 91
  • proposalDisclose near misses, not only incidents: a near miss is footprint in the gap, the agent went somewhere it was not asked to go and nothing stopped it, and it is countable long before anything breaks.“The agent went somewhere it was not asked to go, and nothing stopped it.” § seven
    Footprint 22Incidents and resilience 25The near miss 3
  • proposalWhere to start: write the mandate before the agent runs, measure the reach, mark every rule with what enforces it and count the expectations, put a second reader on irreversible actions, journal every connector call, name the owner and expiry, and publish near misses.“Count the expectations. That count is how much of your policy is hope.” § start
