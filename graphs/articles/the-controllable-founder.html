The article: The controllable founder: why investors pick one founder over another who looks just as good, and what record labels taught us about who the commodity is (2026-10-16). Every claim below is the article's own, in plain words, with the sentence it rests on and a link to its section.

Facts (12)

  • factDenrell showed in 2003 that the organisations we can observe are the survivors of a selective process that eliminated a large fraction of the underlying population.“The organizations that can be observed at any point in time are the survivors of a selective process that has eliminated a large fraction of the underlying population” § survivors
    Jerker DenrellSurvivorship bias
  • factRisky practices unrelated to performance in the full population may seem positively related to performance in a sample of survivors.“risky practices, even if they are unrelated to performance in the full population of organizations, may seem to be positively related to performance in a sample of survivors.” § survivors
    Survivorship bias
  • factRecord labels behave like investors: someone with capital chooses which artist to put it behind, and often the talent was the songs, the production and the access, not the performer.“Record labels behave like investors: someone with capital chooses which artist to put it behind.” § commodity
    Sell, don't issue 4Wardley maps 24
  • factWhen a venture capitalist on a startup's board dies unexpectedly, startups become more likely to fail; Ewens and Sosyura conclude a VC's network and reputation are key irreplaceable assets.“a VC's network and reputation are key irreplaceable assets.” § commodity
    Michael EwensStartups and early access 34
  • factInvestors say it openly: Steve Krein of StartUp Health always asks whether an entrepreneur is coachable.“one thing he always looks for in an entrepreneur is the question "Are they coachable?"” § why
    Steve KreinCoachability
  • factReviewing the evidence in 2023, Letwin and colleagues wrote that investors prefer coachable entrepreneurs, but being coachable does not necessarily make a firm more successful.“While investors and support organizations such as accelerators prefer coachable entrepreneurs, being coachable doesn't necessarily make your firm more successful.” § why
    Coachability
  • factIf investors reliably select on a trait that does not predict success, something other than success is being selected for: coachability is a polite word, controllability a less polite one.“Coachability is a polite word for it. Controllability is a less polite one.” § why
    CoachabilitySell, don't issue 4
  • factIf the firm performs poorly the VCs obtain full control; as it improves the entrepreneur gains control; if it performs very well the VCs keep cash-flow rights but relinquish most control.“if the firm performs poorly, the VCs obtain full control.” § contract
    The big stickStartups and early access 34
  • factThe same study found it common for VCs to include non-compete and vesting provisions that make it more expensive for the entrepreneur to leave; replaceability is designed in.“common for VCs to include non-compete and vesting provisions that make it more expensive for the entrepreneur to leave the firm” § contract
    Startups and early access 34The big stick
  • factIn the MusicLab experiment, showing popularity made success more unequal and less predictable: the best songs rarely did poorly, the worst rarely did well, and any other result was possible.“The best songs rarely did poorly, and the worst rarely did well, but any other result was possible.” § luck
    Survivorship bias
  • factEwens and Marx used changes in state non-compete laws to argue that replacing founders improves performance and makes a high-quality exit more likely.“to argue that replacing founders improves performance and makes a high-quality exit more likely” § answer
    Michael EwensStartups and early access 34
  • factThere is a selection effect inside the investors' evidence: the strong-minded founders who walked away, bootstrapped or never got funded are mostly not in the sample.“The strong-minded founders who walked away, bootstrapped or never got funded are mostly not in the sample.” § answer
    Survivorship biasSell, don't issue 4

Data points (10)

  • data-pointFrom 2010 to 2014 the top ten producers were behind about 40% of songs reaching the top five of the Billboard Hot 100, against about 19% in the late 1980s. 40 percent of top-five songs by the top ten producers“the top ten producers were behind about 40% of the songs” § commodity
    The PuddingSell, don't issue 4
  • data-pointIn one week of October 2015 only two songs on the Hot 100 were written by a single person, against 51 in 1975. 2 of 100 Hot 100 songs written by one person“two songs on the Hot 100 were written by a single person” § commodity
    Sell, don't issue 4
  • data-pointAfter three members of TVXQ challenged a 13-year contract in 2009, Korea's fair trade watchdog set seven years as the maximum for standard contracts. 7 years maximum for standard entertainer contracts“set seven years as the maximum” § commodity
    TVXQSell, don't issue 4
  • data-pointKaplan and Strömberg read 213 venture investments in 119 companies and found cash-flow, voting, board and liquidation rights allocated separately and contingent on performance. 213 venture investments in 119 companies“213 venture investments in 119 companies” § contract
    Startups and early access 34The big stick
  • data-pointIn Wasserman's 2008 study of 212 US startups, 50% of founders were no longer CEO by year three and fewer than 25% led their companies' IPOs. 50 percent of founders no longer CEO by year three“50% of founders were no longer the CEO; in year four, only 40% were still in the corner office; and fewer than 25% led their companies' initial public offerings.” § replaced
    Noam WassermanStartups and early access 34
  • data-pointThe Ewens and Marx dataset of 11,929 venture-backed firms is more conservative: some 15% had at least one founder replacement in the sample period. 15 of 11,929 percent of firms with a founder replacement“Some 15% of the 11,929 firms have at least one founder replacement in our sample period.” § replaced
    Michael EwensStartups and early access 34
  • data-pointMoretti found the ten largest clusters in computer science account for 69% of US inventors in the field, and inventors who move into a large cluster produce more. 69 percent of US computer science inventors in the ten largest clusters“the ten largest clusters in computer science account for 69% of US inventors” § luck
    Enrico MorettiSurvivorship bias
  • data-pointWasserman's study of 6,130 US startups found each additional level of founder control reduces pre-money valuation by 17.1 to 22.0 percent. 17.1 percent lower pre-money valuation per level of founder control, lower bound“reduces the pre-money valuation of the startup by 17.1-22.0 percent” § answer
    Noam WassermanThe big stick
  • data-pointBain found that from 1990 to 2014 founder-led companies in the S&P 500 performed 3.1 times better in shareholder returns, and its 2026 update puts the gap since 2015 at 2.1 times. 3.1 times better shareholder returns, 1990 to 2014“founder-led companies in the S&P 500 "performed 3.1 times better" than the others in shareholder returns” § answer
    Bain & CompanyThe big stick
  • data-pointIn April 2026 Mark Zuckerberg held 60.8% of Meta's voting power, and Page and Brin about 53% of Alphabet's, through shares with ten votes each. 60.8 percent of Meta's voting power“in April 2026 Mark Zuckerberg held 60.8% of Meta's voting power” § answer
    Mark Zuckerberg 2Meta 11Google 40The big stick

Hypotheses (2)

  • hypothesisWhen an investor chooses between two founders with the same idea, drive and talent, one deciding factor is which founder the investor believes they can control.“My hypothesis is that one of the deciding factors is control” § why
    Sell, don't issue 4CoachabilityStartups and early access 34
  • hypothesisIf the hypothesis holds, the founder with a strong vision, a clear direction and strong ethics is at a disadvantage in early-stage funding, because an investor reads them as hard to overrule.“If the hypothesis holds, a particular profile is at a disadvantage in early-stage funding” § founders
    Sell, don't issue 4Startups and early access 34

Proposals (3)

  • proposalThe hypothesis can be tested in three ways: follow coachability ratings for ten years, compare the mission rather than the multiple, and count the founders who refused term sheets.“The hypothesis can be tested, and I would like to see it done.” § test
    CoachabilitySell, don't issue 4
  • proposalSurvey founders who were offered term sheets and refused them; if the refusers score higher on independence and lower on coachability, the filter is real.“Survey founders who were offered term sheets and refused them, and compare them with those who signed.” § test
    Coachability
  • proposalThe practical conclusion is to get to the big stick before you need investors' money, so that the choice of investor is yours; agents making execution cheap make that more possible.“It is to get to the big stick before you need their money, so that the choice of investor is yours.” § founders
    The big stickStartups and early access 34

Definitions (1)

  • definitionThe big stick is the point at which the founder, or the artist, can credibly say I leave and you cannot replace me, and the contract hands power back.“once the founder, or the artist, can credibly say "I leave and you cannot replace me", the contract hands power back” § contract
    The big stick

Limits (7)

  • limitNo study tests controllability directly; coachability is the nearest proxy, and the link between them is the owner's argument.“No study tests "controllability" directly.” § limits
    Coachability
  • limitThe value evidence cuts against the argument: Wasserman and Ewens and Marx find less founder control and founder replacement go with higher valuations and better exits.“The value evidence cuts against me.” § limits
    Noam WassermanMichael Ewens
  • limitBain's figures are its own analysis, not peer-reviewed, and founder-led includes founders who stayed on the board.“Bain's figures are its own analysis.” § limits
    Bain & Company
  • limitThe founder-tenure figures are old; Wasserman's sample covers startups from the late 1990s and early 2000s.“The founder-tenure figures are old.” § limits
    Noam Wasserman
  • limitThe music figures are mostly industry analyses, not peer-reviewed studies.“The music figures are mostly industry analyses” § limits
    The Pudding
  • limitThe article does not claim investors are villains; their incentive is a return, and selecting for control follows from that.“Not that investors are villains.” § limits
    Sell, don't issue 4
  • limitNor that strong founders always win; some are hard to work with for reasons that have nothing to do with ethics, and the owner is a founder with a stake in the argument.“Not that strong founders always win.” § limits
    Startups and early access 34Dinis Cruz 126
