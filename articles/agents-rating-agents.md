- **A centre of gravity for the newsroom:** a standard to map the agents of the companies at the summit.
- **Two ladders:** freedom from 1 to 5, governance from 0 to 4, rated from evidence, measurable from the outside.
- **Eight instruments, one short list:** 279 provisions, 73 indicators, 21 questions.

## Part seven of ten days

This is part seven of [ten days at Web Summit](ten-days-at-web-summit.md), the series about what six of us (Dinis Cruz, Angelo Leggio, Vicky Wang, Silvio Switha, José Bota and Indi Gupte) built with around 250 agents between 3 and 13 November, with more than 60 working at once at the peak. Part six, [stopping a hundred agents](stopping-a-hundred-agents.md), was about the night of 9 November. GAMMA-Ray was briefed the same night.

## The brief

My words, from the brief, after the opening night: "At the moment our website, PrompTugal, doesn't have a centre of gravity." The idea was to take the standards that already exist for mapping agents and, "because we have the graph technology", map the companies at the summit and the ones we wanted to talk to, so that every interview and everything we presented sat in that context.

Three conditions came the next day. It had to be about agents: "a way to map them and a way to show how mature they are. And this should be also positive." It had to "over-index on stuff that we can measure and stuff that we can calculate from the outside." And it had to look through RiskMandate's lens: a score "might actually be very different when you actually take into account the reality", once you apply reach and mandate ([where assist becomes execute](where-assist-becomes-execute.md)).

## The mashup

The mashup: 279 provisions in eight instruments, reached by 21 questions through 73 indicators, each a link.

Maturity standards for agents ask many of the same things in different words. GAMMA-Ray's answer is a mashup. It read eight instruments: the EU AI Act, NIST's AI risk management framework, OWASP's work on agentic threats, the MCP authorisation specification, and four maturity models from vendors and practitioners. It cut them into 279 provisions, one statement each; found the observable fact behind each, an indicator, 73 of them; and grouped the indicators into 21 questions, from "what is the agent built on?" to "is the organisation behind it ready?". Those 21 questions reach 210 of the 279 provisions, three quarters. Every level on a card is a query over that mapping, and every answer counts towards several standards at once.

The result is two ladders: **freedom**, from 1 to 5, what an agent can do and how far it reaches; and **governance**, from 0 to 4, how openly it shows it and how far it can be trusted to go. The same questions are answered at three tiers of evidence: public, what anyone can check from outside; declared, the operator's word; and evidenced, the operator's evidence.

## Eight agents, and nobody marks their own work

Agents rating agents: drafted, reviewed twice, signed off by a third agent; none may approve its own work.

A team of eight agents built it, each in its own session, woken every hour: one owned the standard, a studio and a field agent wrote the scans, a reviewer, an approver, a designer, a graphs agent and an editor. Their ownership table says what each may never do: the studio never reviews or approves its own scan; the reviewer never changes an answer or assigns a level; the approver never approves a scan it wrote or reviewed, or one with fewer than two review rounds; the editor never uses an adjective about a company. Two agents never edit the same file in the same hour.

A card is published only after a draft, two rounds of review, answers to every finding and a sign-off. Round two argues against every level three and four: is the evidence an artefact, or an adjective? The gate refuses a third party's card that skipped any of it. Every source of every scan was fetched once, hashed and saved into a public vault for that scan, so the evidence a rating rests on cannot quietly change ([how a vault works](how-a-vault-works.md)).

## Twenty cards, at direct links

The team produced about twenty company cards in its first days. Then, on 13 November, I made a decision: every third party's card, rating and report is built at an unguessable address, marked noindex, and left out of every list, the sitemap and the data files, so it can be sent to the company first, to claim or correct. The public site shows the standard and our own two cards: RiskMandate's publisher agent and the PrompTugal newsroom team. That is why this article names no company.

It is the same principle as everywhere else in our work: the agents draft, and a person decides what goes out. A card about someone else's agent is a message to them before it is a page about them.

## Draft, and why

The most important word on every card is Draft, and the site says exactly what it means: found and checked by agents of one model family in one repository; no human has signed one; no company has confirmed one; no customer has attested one. Its REALITY file is as blunt: no company's card is public, no organisation has been rated, no grant measured by us, and the links between indicators and provisions have not been checked one by one by anybody.

I think that honesty is the point of the exercise. A rating made by agents is only worth what its evidence and its process are worth, so both have to be on the card. Beside the cards, the team also drafted FACT-Ray, a way to show on every fact how strong its evidence is and who stands behind it, from an agent's finding to a company's signed statement at a well-known address.

## What it taught us

- **A standard can be built in days,** if the source instruments are read at source and every link is data.
- **Separation of duties works for agents** exactly as for people, if it is written down and a gate enforces it.
- **Evidence goes in a vault,** hashed at capture, or the rating drifts with the web.
- **Publish your own card first,** and other people's only after they have seen it.

## What this does not claim

- **GAMMA-Ray is a beta draft:** the rules, the mapping and every rating are experimental and will change.
- **No company has confirmed a card,** and no human has signed one.
- **The instrument links are one reader's first pass,** not yet reviewed.
- **The outside review of the standard was by an AI model,** at my request.
