## The question

John Saroff, chief executive of Chartbeat, describes a moment most of us know. Someone in his alumni group shared a great article from a local news organisation; "I clicked, hit a paywall, and felt disappointed." He was curious about the article and the publication, and could not get to know either.

His question for publishers follows from it: "when someone arrives through a trusted recommendation, should the goal be to ask them to pay right away or to start building a relationship?" And he gives a number that makes it urgent. Across Chartbeat's network, dark social, visits from texts, messaging apps and other sources without referral data, "grew from 7.1% to 11.3% of pageviews over the past two years." He points to The New York Times giving subscribers unlimited gift links as "a compelling example of meeting readers where discovery happens", and ends: "A share can be more than a pageview. It can start a reader relationship and create an opportunity to earn the next visit."

I agree with every line. So here is what the other side of that question looks like when it is built, because this newsroom has been running it since October.

The card in John Saroff's post: dark social's share of pageviews across Chartbeat's network, 2024 and 2026.

## No paywall, and credit to start

What a reader finds on the other side of a shared link here: no paywall, credit to start, a meter for what is read.

Anyone who arrives here, from a WhatsApp chat, an alumni group or a LinkedIn post, can read everything. There is no paywall and no registration. What there is, is a meter:

- **Every reader starts with £5 of credit,** and no account. The balance lives in their own browser.
- **Every page has a price of a few pence,** and the reader pays for the share of it they actually read, measured section by section in their browser. A page they only scroll through costs a tenth of its price.
- **A page that was not worth it** can be marked so at its foot, and it is not charged.
- **Credit can go below zero,** and no page is ever blocked. When it runs low, a reader tops up £5, in one payment, on the payment provider's page, after the value: no subscription, nothing renews.

The reasoning is in [paying after the value](paying-after-the-value.md) and in [I just want to buy the article](i-just-want-to-buy-the-article.md), my own version of John's disappointment; how it is built is in [the reading account, as built](the-reading-account-as-built.md).

## Every link is a gift link

Gift links are a good answer inside a subscription model: they let a subscriber open a door that is otherwise closed. Without a paywall there is no door to open. Every link anyone shares, subscriber or not, is already a gift link, and it works in every chat, group and email, including the ones no publisher can see.

That matters for dark social in particular. We cannot see those conversations either. But we do not need to, because the reader who arrives through one gets the whole article, and pays, if they pay, for what they read.

## The relationship grows on the reader's side

What a reader finds next: a front page from their own reading, a way to ask for one designed, and a top-up.

The relationship John describes, earning the next visit, is built here from the reader's side rather than ours:

- **Your newsroom:** a front page for each of the reader's personas, worked out in their browser from what they read, with nothing sent anywhere.
- **Send us your reading:** if they choose, they send what they read, encrypted in their browser so that only our agent can open it, and get back a front page designed for them.
- **A newsletter,** an issue a week or so, for those who want it.
- **Their reading account can move with them,** kept in their own encrypted vault, as [the vault crosses Chrome profiles](the-vault-crosses-chrome-profiles.md) describes.

The publisher learns what the reader chooses to share, and the reader keeps everything else. That is a relationship a reader can trust, which is the point of arriving through someone they trust.

## What publishers could take from it

- **Make the first visit free and complete,** and charge for what is read after it, not for access before it.
- **Let a reader decline a charge** for a page that was not worth it; it costs little and buys trust.
- **Keep the reader's history on the reader's side,** and ask for it, rather than collect it.
- **Treat every share as a gift link,** because from the reader's side, it is.

## What this does not claim

- **We are small.** A newsroom of one editor and agents is not a local paper with a newsroom to pay; the meter is an experiment running in public, with modest numbers.
- **We have no dark social data of our own;** the figures are Chartbeat's, as the post states them.
- **The meter is client-side:** a new browser starts again with £5, as the account page says, and we accept that.
- **My interest:** I build the tools this newsroom runs on.
