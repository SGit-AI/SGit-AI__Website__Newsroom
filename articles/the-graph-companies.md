## Graph companies

Something I have been looking at while reading my book: some of the biggest and most successful companies in the world, especially the ones that do not sell physical resources, are graph companies. Nodes and edges. Connecting A to B to C to D.

Look at them:

- **Google** began with a graph of links. Its founders' 1998 paper describes a search engine that "makes heavy use of the structure present in hypertext" ([Brin and Page](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse454/15wi/papers/google.html)). In 2012 it added the Knowledge Graph, which "understands real-world entities and their relationships to one another: things, not strings", starting with more than 500 million objects and 3.5 billion facts ([Google, 16 May 2012](https://blog.google/products/search/introducing-knowledge-graph-things-not/)).
- **Facebook** is a social graph; Mark Zuckerberg used the term when Facebook opened to developers in 2007. Its own researchers measured it in 2011: 721 million active users and 69 billion friendships, with almost everyone in one connected piece ([Ugander and others](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.4503)). Meta now counts 3.6 billion people a day across its apps ([Meta, July 2026](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/)).
- **LinkedIn** calls itself the Economic Graph, "a digital representation of the global economy", with more than a billion members ([LinkedIn](https://economicgraphchallenge.linkedin.com)). Microsoft paid $26.2 billion for it in 2016.
- **Amazon** is a gigantic graph of products, sellers and supply chain. Its scientists have published how they build a product knowledge graph automatically, across thousands of product types ([AutoKnow, 2020](https://www.amazon.science/publications/autoknow-self-driving-knowledge-collection-for-products-of-thousands-of-types)). Last year Amazon's sales were $716.9 billion.
- **Visa and Mastercard** are networks. Visa processed 257.5 billion transactions in its last financial year, over 4.9 billion payment credentials ([Visa 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316125000089/v-20250930.htm)).

By market value on 19 October 2026, Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta are four of the ten most valuable companies in the world, worth about $12.8 trillion together ([companiesmarketcap.com](https://companiesmarketcap.com/)). And there is an argument for some of the others: a chip that trains models is a machine for computing graphs.

## Logistics is a graph

Any logistics company is the graph that connects the dots. It is the graph that enables the profitability, that tells you what to do at that moment in time. UPS's route optimisation system, ORION, is a graph problem, and UPS said it would save about 100 million miles and 10 million gallons of fuel a year once fully deployed ([UPS, 2015](https://app.3blmedia.com/news/ups-accelerates-use-routing-optimization-software-reduce-100-million-miles-driven)).

## Ontologies are a business

Then there are the companies that are very successful because of their ontologies and their taxonomies. Palantir is the poster child these days. It describes its Ontology as "an operational layer for the organization" which, "in many settings, ... serves as a digital twin of the organization" ([Palantir](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/overview/)). Its revenue grew 56% in 2025, to $4.5 billion.

It is not alone. LSEG publishes PermID, permanent identifiers for organisations, instruments and people, as open linked data ([PermID](https://permid.org/about)). Neo4j, a graph database company, passed $200 million of annual recurring revenue in 2024 ([Neo4j](https://neo4j.com/press-releases/neo4j-revenue-milestone-2024/)). ServiceNow agreed to buy data.world in 2025, and described its core as "a semantic layer or a knowledge graph" ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchitoperations/news/366623687/ServiceNow-digs-deeper-into-data-for-AI-with-Dataworld-buy)). And a lot of consulting projects are, at heart, bringing ontologies and taxonomies into an organisation.

## Everything is a graph

A lot of software is just connecting data. A spreadsheet is a graph with a couple of dimensions. A relational database, the model [Codd described in 1970](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/362384.362685), is a graph, probably not in the most efficient way. A language model is a graph: the frameworks that train them record every operation as one ([PyTorch](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/notes/autograd.html)).

Law is a gigantic graph. Legal research has been a citation graph since Shepard's Citations started in 1873, and researchers have measured the importance of precedents by analysing the network of 26,681 US Supreme Court majority opinions ([Fowler and others, 2007](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/network-analysis-and-the-law-measuring-the-legal-importance-of-precedents-at-the-us-supreme-court/D9B2F13BE2594E1527780AAFDD6BF164)). Contracts are graphs. Governments would be a lot more efficient if they thought in graphs and connected one piece of the puzzle to another, and so would most businesses.

## The answer becomes obvious

There is a very cool thing in design: once you have the right questions and the right information, the answer is obvious. It is the end of a whodunit. All the clues were there; what changes at the end is that the right connections are made, the irrelevant ones fall away, and only one answer is left.

The answer becomes obvious. On the left, a graph with every node and edge: people, places, events, evidence, and no answer in sight. In the middle, the irrelevant nodes removed, the relevant connections kept. On the right, what is left: a short path from the question to the answer, which now looks obvious. The same move works for a supply chain, a contract, a regulation, or a search result.

That is what a graph does once it has the right connections. You remove the nodes that are not relevant, you focus on the ones that are, and the answer becomes obvious. It is why search worked, why a feed knows what to show you, and why a logistics company knows which van goes where.

## Why graphs never scaled

So you can argue that graphs have already won. But they won for a few. For everyone else, they did not scale, and I think there are three reasons.

Why graphs never scaled, and how they can now. Before: building a graph took a lot of technology; whoever built it used it to lock in the customer and keep competitors out; there was no movement to share schemas or the code; so each graph stayed inside one company, and the advantage stayed with the few. Now: fractal semantic graphs, where each part keeps its own vocabulary and nobody has to share a schema; open source and open schemas; agents that build and maintain the graph; so graphs can be built by anyone, for any domain.

First, creating a graph never scaled. It required a lot of technology, and a lot of people to build and maintain it.

Second, lock-in. Many of the players were motivated, consciously or not, to lock the customer in. There was no big movement to share schemas, and none to share the code that got created. So whichever company got there first and built the graph gained a competitive advantage, and then used the graph to keep competitors out.

Third, some of the nodes in those graphs were treated as commodities when they should not have been, the customer among them, which is what [when the customer becomes the commodity](when-the-customer-becomes-the-commodity.md) is about. The value in graphs is enormous; it is just not evenly distributed, or not very effectively.

The contrast is striking. Analysts put the whole market for knowledge graph software at about a billion dollars in 2024 ([MarketsandMarkets](https://prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/knowledge-graph-market-worth-6-938-4-million-by-2030--exclusive-report-by-marketsandmarkets-302347820.html), an estimate). The companies built on graphs are worth trillions. The graphs were the moat, not the product.

## Now they can scale

I think that is changing. [Fractal semantic graphs](introducing-fractal-semantic-graphs.md) are built on the premise that everything connects to everything and nobody has to share a schema: each part keeps its own vocabulary, and the bridges between them are edges somebody declares. Agents are very good at building and maintaining graphs. And open source and open schemas, which already exist for the web in [schema.org](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2011/06/introducing-schemaorg-search-engines) and for European public services in the [EU's core vocabularies](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/semic-support-centre/solution/core-vocabularies), mean a graph does not have to be a moat.

So the business case is this. The biggest companies, the most successful ones, and an enormous amount of money are in graphs. What has been missing is a way for everyone else to build them. That is the opportunity for the next generation of founders.

## What this does not claim

- **"Graph company" is my lens.** These companies also run ads, cloud services, devices and stores.
- **Market values move daily.** These are from 19 October 2026.
- **Correlation, not cause.** Graphs are at the heart of these companies; they are not the only reason for their value.
- **The market-size figures are estimates.** Analysts define the market differently.
- **UPS's savings are its own figures.**
