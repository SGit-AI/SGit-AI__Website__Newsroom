Fifteen minutes of an expert: how agents make a short review by someone with twenty years in a field worth acting on, so that an expert's quarter of an hour, a few pen notes and a voice memo on a website, a deck or a book, becomes changes with their source, why that is one of the professions that never scaled and now can, my own book as the case, and a career website reviewed by a CISO as the first market

By Dinis Cruz · 2026-11-19 · article v1.0.0 · newsroom v0.14.180 · expertsscaling-expertisereviewscareersagenticworkflowarticle

Abstract: I keep making the case that AI is about scaling professions that never scaled, and one of them is this: how do you get value from ten, fifteen minutes, an hour of an expert's time? I think we are now close. Take how I am reviewing my book. I print it, and on paper, even on a plane, I write all over it: notes, references, comments of a kind I would never have made before. And I notice myself commenting more, and more confidently, because I know the notes will be acted on. Before, every five minutes of review cost half an hour to two hours of work to apply, so the review mostly did not happen. Now the agents do that work. Apply it to someone looking for a job in cybersecurity. I am a CISO; I have hired and run large teams. Fifteen minutes from me on their website, a few marks and a voice memo, turned by agents into changes with their source, is worth a great deal to them, and costs me fifteen minutes. The same holds for any field, and for a website, a deck, a book or a PDF. The career websites we are building are where it starts.

In short

  • An expert's short time never scaled: the work to apply a review was too large.
  • Agents now do that work, so fifteen minutes of review becomes changes with their source.
  • The expert comments more, and more confidently, knowing the notes will be acted on.
  • My own book is the case; a career website reviewed by a CISO is the first market.
  • It works for any field, and for a website, a deck, a book or a PDF.

The profession that never scaled

In the book I argue that much of the opportunity is in jobs that never scaled (the jobs that never scaled). One is the simplest of all: getting value from a short piece of an expert's time. Ten minutes, fifteen, an hour. The value is enormous, because those minutes sit on twenty years of experience. But it rarely happened, because a review only helps if someone acts on it, and acting on it was the expensive part.

My book, as the case

I am reviewing my book the way I reviewed its first release candidate (143 notes in a day): printed, with a pen, even on a plane on the way over. I write all over it: notes, references, comments, a massive amount of them, and of a kind I would never have written before.

Two things changed. Before, every five minutes of review would have cost half an hour, an hour, two hours of work to put into the book, so most of it would never have been done, and so I would not have written it. Now the agents do that work: they read the marks, find the passage, make the change and keep the note as its source. And I notice myself commenting differently: more, and more confidently, because I know it will be acted on.

A CISO reviewing a career website

Now take someone trying to get a job in cybersecurity. I am a CISO; I have hired people and run large teams. When I was hiring, I asked candidates to present themselves, and I tried hard to give them something back from the interview; they got a lot from it. Imagine I spend fifteen minutes on their career website: a few marks, a few typed lines, a voice memo saying what I would look for, what I would cut, what would make me call them. The agents turn it into changes, each with my note as its source, for them to accept or not.

For them, that is very valuable. For me, it is fifteen minutes. That is the trade that was never possible before.

The loop

  1. The work: a website, a deck, a book, a PDF, on screen or printed.
  2. The expert: fifteen minutes; pen notes, a few typed lines, a voice memo.
  3. The agents: transcribe, read the marks, and turn each note into a change with its source.
  4. The person: every change to review, why it was made, and who said it.

Every field has its experts, and every one of them could give fifteen minutes like this: to a graduate, a founder's deck, a new author, a small business's website. The expert's time is the scarce part; everything around it is now the agents'.

Why someone would pay for it

Because it is very valuable, and it is now cheap to deliver. The person gets a review from someone they could never otherwise reach, applied for them; the expert gives a quarter of an hour and sees it used; and a platform that matches them, carries the work and keeps the record can take a fair share. It is also how the career websites get better than anything a person can make alone.

What this does not claim

  • No paid expert review exists yet; this maps the workflow.
  • The book review is real and ongoing; the CISO review of a career site is an illustration.
  • An expert's review is advice, for the person to accept or not.

Where this comes from
