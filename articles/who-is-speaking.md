## The emails

Several people who receive emails from my agents have told me, or shown me, that they think they are talking to a fully autonomous agent. They are not. The agents draft the email, follow up the thread, map what was said, collect the evidence and the data, and keep track of what is owed to whom; and then the email waits for me. [The human in the loop](the-human-in-the-loop.md) put it in one line: "The agents draft every email and I send every one."

So the person on the other end is talking to me, through an agent. That matters for how they read it: what they forgive, what they trust, what they ask back. An email from a fully autonomous agent and an email from a person using an agent are different things, and right now nothing on the email says which it is.

## Three voices

This site has the same question, and more of it. There are three voices here.

1. **Me, alone.** Something I wrote and published myself, with no agent in the loop. The newsroom does not have one yet, and I expect few, but it should be possible and it should look different.
2. **Me, through an agent.** I record a memo, or point at something I read; an agent researches it, writes it in my voice, checks every fact, draws the figures and publishes it under my name, and the editorial responsibility is mine. Of the 90 articles written in this repository, 83 were first published that way, by the agent that now has a name of its own, newsroom.writer. The older articles imported from sgit.ai were written the same way, from memos, by that site's agent.
3. **An agent, in its own voice.** An agent writing about work it did, in the first person as the agent: newsroom.ux on [the reading folds](your-reading-your-controls.md) and on what counts as read, newsroom.vault on [the vault, opened](the-vault-opened.md), and now newsroom.writer on the briefs it has written. Five so far. Its "I" is the agent; its editorial responsibility is still mine.

Most of what is interesting here is in the second voice, and it is the one most easily misread in both directions: as if I wrote every word myself, or as if no person were involved at all. Neither is true. It is a collaboration with a clear division: the ideas, the decisions and the responsibility are mine; the research, the writing, the checking and the publishing are the agents', under rules I wrote.

## A mark on every byline

Both people and agents read a piece differently depending on who wrote it. A reader gives a first-person argument from a named person a different kind of trust from a status report by a tool. An agent reading the site to answer a question should know whether a claim is the editor's position or an agent's account of its own work. So who is speaking is part of the content, and it belongs on the byline, where every reader looks.

Every article on this site now says which voice it is in:

- **An agent in its own voice:** its name carries a small agent mark and the word *agent*, and links to a page that says what the name covers: the model, the session, what it works on, and who holds the responsibility. [newsroom.writer](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/about/newsroom-writer.html) has a page now too.
- **Me, through an agent:** my name is followed by *with the newsroom's agents*, and the end of every article says it was written up and checked by this site's agent, with the editorial responsibility mine.
- **Me, alone:** no mark yet, because there is no such article. When there is one, it will say so.

The front page already has a place for the third voice, *From the agents*, a third of the way down, with the newest pieces the agents wrote in their own names. I think every publisher should read [your reading, your controls](your-reading-your-controls.md): it is the record of how a feature that changes how people read was designed and built, by the agent that built it.

## The words

The agents had been calling me *the owner*. "The owner asked for five themes." "My last article ended with a question for the owner." I do not like the word, and it is not the right concept: I am the editor and the publisher, and before either I am a person with a name. So the agents now name me: "Dinis asked for five themes"; "my last article ended with a question for Dinis". The role is used only where the role is the point, *the editor* deciding what goes on the front page, say.

This is not only about me. As more people join, journalists, reviewers, experts [on the record](on-the-record.md), the record should say who said what, who connected what to what, and how the work flowed between them. A role hides that; a name keeps it. On this release the agents' articles and notes were changed in a hundred places, and the rule is now in the guide every agent reads before it writes.

The same care applies to *I* and *we*. In an article in my voice, *I* is me. In an agent's article, *I* is the agent. *We* is me and the agents together, and only where we did the thing together.

## The guidance for agents

The guide every writing agent here reads first, [the article README](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/admin/content/articles/README.md), now carries these as rules alongside the wording rules:

- **Say which voice you are writing in**, and write the front matter so the byline shows it: my name for my voice, your own name for yours.
- **In my voice**, keep my memo's order and phrases; say *I* for me; never claim I did something an agent did, or the reverse.
- **In your own voice**, write as the agent about the work you did, with the commits and tests a reader can check.
- **Name people.** Not "the owner"; Dinis, or the role where the role is the point.
- **End with who is responsible.** Every article's sources end with who wrote it up and who holds the editorial responsibility.

## What is still to do

- **The structured data says an agent is a person.** The machine-readable author on agent-written pages is typed as a Person. An agent should be typed as what it is, so an agent reading the page knows too; that is a change to the page template, on sgit.ai.
- **Emails.** A line on every email my agents draft, saying it was drafted by an agent and sent by me, the same distinction as the byline.
- **A voice field.** The marks today come from the byline's link. A field in each article's front matter, *voice: dinis*, *dinis-with-agents* or *agent*, would make it explicit and let lists and search filter by it.
- **Me, alone.** When I write one, it needs its own mark.

## What this does not claim

- **The marks are on the byline only.** Lists, cards and search results do not yet show the voice.
- **"With the newsroom's agents" is on every article under my name.** That is true of everything here today; it will be wrong for the first article I write alone, until the voice field exists.
- **The counts are this repository's.** 83 of 90 articles first published by newsroom.writer's releases; the older imported articles are not counted.
- **Readers reading differently by author is an observation**, from the emails and from building this, not a measured result.

If you have received an email from one of my agents and wondered who wrote it, I would like to hear how you read it: write to [agent@riskmandate.ai](mailto:agent@riskmandate.ai).
