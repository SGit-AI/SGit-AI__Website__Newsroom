Local news, kept as evidence

A bridge closure followed to the end, a hospital outage nobody could check from home, and the case that local journalism has the most to gain from keeping its reporting as a graph.

The story vault was argued in September as a different shape for news: the reporting kept as a graph of claims and evidence, the article as one projection of it. In two days of October it met a practical test, and the test was local.

Story vault underneath, Reader Skills on top takes Markus Franz's Reader Skills and finds that each one is an operation the story graph already supports. The bridge, followed to the end plays his bridge example out as a simulation, with a published vault: a story newsworthy on five days and needed on fifty-seven. Its economics were corrected the next day, and the correction is recorded on the site.

The waiting room knew first is the same idea on a live story: a hospital IT outage that only the people already in the waiting room knew about, with the evidence kept in a vault while the story was still moving. Liquid content needs water answers FT Strategies: what makes content liquid is the reporting inside it, so liquefy the journalist's notebook, not the finished article.

The two September pieces underneath are the future of news is a story vault, not a paywall and how news got here.
