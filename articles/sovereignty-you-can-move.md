## A direct competitor

We have been describing the website cell as something new. It is, in how it is made. But in what it replaces, it is plain: it is a website, and the people who would buy it today buy Wix, Squarespace, a WordPress host or, increasingly, an AI builder like Fliplet ([rent or own](fliplet-and-the-website-cell.md)). So we should position it as a direct competitor, and compete on the one thing those platforms cannot offer without giving up their business ([no lock-in](no-lock-in.md)).

## Where it is hosted is not sovereignty

Most of the talk about digital sovereignty is about location: data kept in an EU data centre, a supplier incorporated at home. That matters, and it is not enough. A site hosted in Dublin on a platform whose terms say you cannot take it with you is not yours, wherever the servers are. Fliplet keeps accounts in Dublin by default; its standard terms still say the service and what you build in it are Fliplet's, and that it offers no way to transfer or copy your applications.

## What sovereignty requires

On our open source site I set it out in four steps ([sovereignty requires open source](https://open-source.sgit.ai/views/sovereignty.html)):

- **Only open source makes independence possible:** "if you suddenly get cut off from the service, at least you have a chance".
- **The schemas matter as much as the code:** "the data schemas are as important as the code ... you need to be able to control it, to own it". An export in a format the vendor chose is access, not control.
- **Without ownership you are one SLA away from losing access,** and whoever has jurisdiction over the supplier has it over you.
- **Company nationality is not sovereignty:** "A European company gets acquired by a US company and the sovereignty moves, you lose it."

That last one is not abstract. Squarespace was taken private by an investment firm in 2024. A company that is British today can have owners, investors and a jurisdiction elsewhere tomorrow, and its terms with them.

## The website cell passes the tests

- **Can you move it?** Yes: static files in your own GitHub repository, with their history, hostable anywhere, from GitHub Pages for free to any web host ([one commit, one site, one owner](one-commit-one-site-one-owner.md)).
- **Do you own the schema?** Yes: the structure of the site and its content are files in your repository, in open formats, changed by your own Claude.
- **Who sets the terms?** You: no platform, no subscription, and open-source code that nobody can withdraw ([governance from zero](governance-from-zero.md)).
- **What if we are bought?** Nothing changes for you: you already hold everything, and every site is expected to move into its owner's ownership within three months.

## Privacy-first, European, multilingual

A sovereign website: yours to move, privacy-first, European-aligned, multilingual, and open for others to build on.

The rest follows from the same design. No cookies unless the owner adds them, and no tracking by us ([static against the platforms](static-against-the-platforms.md)). Built in Europe, on open standards, and deployable in any region the owner chooses. Multilingual from the first day: agenticcells.ai itself is in ten languages ([you bring the business, we build the site](you-bring-the-business.md)). That is a proper sovereign website, not a sovereign label on a rented one.

## A business others can build on

Because it is open, the website cell is also a base for other businesses: partners who sell and support it in their own country and language, agencies, newsrooms, councils ([partners sell, we build](partners-sell-we-build.md)). The agents that build and maintain these sites work under behaviour policies from RiskMandate, which is where the alignment with our other company lies: sovereign sites, built by agents whose reach is written down and checked. I think there is a large business here on its own.

## What this does not claim

- **Sovereignty is a set of possibilities, not a guarantee:** owning the files means you can move; it does not move them for you.
- **The platforms' positions are from their pages and standard terms;** negotiated contracts may differ.
- **The three-month ownership expectation is a soft requirement** for now.
