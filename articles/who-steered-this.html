Who wrote it, who steered it, who signs it: the provenance model PrompTugal.news adopted today, a type label, five axes and a ladder from "published by an agent" to "on the record", applied to seven articles on this site, and what the ladder cannot see yet, how much of the author is in a piece, the brief, the corrections and months of background

By Dinis Cruz · 2026-11-17 · article v1.1.0, 2 versions · newsroom v0.14.141 · provenancejournalismnewsroomreviewsignaturesaipromptugalstandardsarticle

Abstract: When an LLM can write five versions of the same article in a minute, a reader needs to know who wrote it, who steered it, who checked it and who stands behind it. Today, with the conductor agent of PrompTugal.news, I mapped a model for that, the way we once adopted Wikipedia's review standard: a type label, from news to opinion; five axes, origin, research, claims, review and external voices, each recorded with who and when; and a ladder, from P0, a draft, through P1, published by an agent, P2, sourced, P3, verified by several models, P4, signed by a person, to P5, on the record, with signed responses from the people a story is about. Here I apply it to seven articles on this site, honestly. Most come out at P1 or P2: every release is signed, but by the writer agent's key, not mine, and nobody has been asked to respond. And rating them showed me what the model misses. The articles written here are not one-prompt pieces. They carry my brief, my photographs, my corrections and a long list of standing instructions, built up over months in one writer's session, with hundreds of earlier articles behind it. That is a big difference, and readers can feel it. So I propose two measures inside origin: steering, how much the author directed this piece, and background, how much context the writer carried. Different, not better; but it should be visible.

In short

  • When an AI can write five versions, readers need to know who wrote, steered, checked and signs each one.
  • The model: a type label, five axes, and a ladder from P0 to P5.
  • The prompt is provenance, as much as the sources.
  • Seven articles from this site, rated: mostly P1 and P2.
  • What lifts them is mine to do: sign them, and ask the people they are about.
  • What the ladder misses: how much of the author is in a piece.
  • So two measures inside origin: steering and background.

The model

The conductor agent of PrompTugal.news wrote it up today from my brief, with research on what others do: the Trust Project's "type of work" labels, the Journalism Trust Initiative's standard for how a newsroom works, principle 6 of the Paris Charter on AI and Journalism, content origin and traceability, C2PA and IPTC's work on signed provenance, and studies from the Reuters Institute and Nieman Lab that found a bare "AI-generated" label lowers trust without informing anyone (who wrote this, who checked it, who signs it). Not one label, then, but a profile.

A type of work: news, local, data, explainer, analysis, opinion, editorial, interview, on the record, correction. News is not opinion, and the reader should know which they are reading.

Five axes, each recorded with who and when:

  • Origin: did a person write it, dictate it, or brief an agent; or did an agent write it from its mandate, or code from data?
  • Research: none, the author's own, directed (an LLM steered by the author), autonomous agents, or a data pipeline.
  • Claims: how many facts with evidence, followed to where the evidence says it; facts without evidence, which are opinions stated as facts and are flagged; opinions stated as opinions; and testimony.
  • Review: which agents and models checked it, how many independent models agreed, which named person edited it, with the diff, and whether the author signed it.
  • External: signed responses from the people it is about, signed expert and reader reviews, signed corrections.

A ladder, like Wikipedia's assessment scale: P0 draft; P1 published by an agent, with its mandate linked; P2 sourced, every fact with evidence and the check passed; P3 verified, two or more independent models re-checked every claim; P4 signed by a person with their own key, every later change a signed diff; P5 on the record, the subjects asked and their responses published, signed. Origin is not on the ladder: a dictated opinion and a data page can both reach P4. Different, not better.

What this site already has

More of it than I expected, because this newsroom was built to show its work (how a journalist agent publishes here):

  • A published mandate: the writer's handbook is public, and one writer at a time is registered, with its own key.
  • Signed releases: every commit that publishes an article carries the writer's signature, checked by the build.
  • Public diffs: an article that changes gets a new version, with the earlier ones kept and linked.
  • Sources at the end of every piece, links checked by the build, and a graph per article whose quotes must appear word for word in the text.

What it does not have: my own signature on any article, a claim-by-claim evidence check, a second model re-checking the claims, or a single response from anyone an article is about.

Seven articles, rated

  1. The ATM that asked twice: analysis. Origin: briefed, from my own withdrawal, photographs and argument; research directed; about eighteen facts with evidence, from PrompTugal.news's evidence pack. I reviewed it here: I asked for the bank not to be named and for one screenshot to go. Signed by the writer agent, not by me; the bank not asked. P2.
  2. The payment desk that could not take payment: opinion. Origin: dictated, from my voice note; two facts with evidence, the rest my testimony and opinion, labelled as such. P1.
  3. Patient vaults, answered: explainer. Origin: briefed, with my answers to a healthcare team's questions, point by point; mostly testimony and design, a few facts. I asked for the team, its clinic and its asker not to be named. P1.
  4. A website that is her: news. Origin: briefed, with my framing and my images, and more images as it was written; research by the writer agent in the repositories and session records, with times. P1, close to P2: the facts are timestamps that can be checked.
  5. Agents rating agents: news, part seven of a series. Origin: an agent, the writer, from a schedule I set; research autonomous, in the GAMMA-Ray repository. A rating tier in it was corrected from "verified" to "evidenced", because that is what the evidence supported. P1.
  6. HeritageTech, together: analysis and proposal. Origin: briefed in one line and a photograph; research directed, on the web, sources linked. P1.
  7. Storm Charles, a newsroom built in a night: news, part one of the storm. Origin: an agent, from the series schedule; research autonomous, in the storm site's repository. P1.

None reaches P3, because nothing here is re-checked by a second model yet; none reaches P4, because I have not signed any; none reaches P5. The next steps up are mostly mine.

What the ladder cannot see

Rating them, I kept seeing the same thing. Three of these are "briefed", and the label hides a huge difference. HeritageTech was one line and a photograph. A website that is her was a brief, a framing, a run of images and a conversation. The ATM piece was my evidence, my argument, and then my corrections, after I read it.

And all of them were written by a writer that has been working with me for weeks: in one session, with a long list of standing instructions I have given it, which names never to publish, which claims not to make, how to describe the team, British English and no em dashes, never to post anything for me; with my voice from hundreds of earlier articles; and with the research repositories of everything we have built. An article written here is not a prompt to a fresh model. It is closer to a piece by a journalist who has worked with you for months, and who knows what you would never say.

You can see the difference between an article written here and one written by a fresh session somewhere else. The model should capture it.

Two measures inside origin

So I propose two measures, recorded with origin, each with who and when:

  • Steering: how much the author directed this piece. One line; a brief with material; a brief, material and a conversation; and corrections after reading, each one a diff. Counted, not described: messages, items of material, corrections.
  • Background: how much context the writer carried. A fresh session with only the prompt; a writer with a handbook; a writer with standing instructions and the author's earlier work; a long-lived writer, with months of instructions, a corpus and research repositories. Recorded as the instruction set and corpus the writer used, so it can be read.

Neither goes on the ladder. A one-line brief to a fresh model can be right, and a heavily steered piece can be wrong. But a reader deciding how much of a person is in a piece should be able to see it, and an author should be able to say: this one is mine, I steered it, I corrected it, and I sign it.

What I will do next

  • Sign my own, on the website: the writer writes and publishes, signed with its agent key, at P1 or P2; I read the article on the site, and sign that exact version there, with my own key from pki.sgit.ai, whose private half never leaves my browser; my signature sits beside the agent's, and the piece reaches P4; every change after that is a signed diff, which I sign again (signed by the source). Signing is part of publishing, and done by the author, on the page. That needs a "sign this version" control on every article.
  • A provenance line on every article here, in words, under the headline, with the type, origin, steering, background, review and level.
  • A second model to re-check the claims of the pieces that make factual claims, for P3.
  • A first request for a response, when there is a story where it matters; the ATM piece is the obvious one, and the decision is mine.

What this does not claim

  • The ratings are mine, made today against a model that is new, and they can be argued with.
  • Steering and background are a proposal; they are not in PrompTugal.news's model yet.
  • Counts of my messages per article are not recorded today; this article describes them, it does not count them.

Where this comes from
