Joining Algarve Evolution: The Cyber Boardroom takes the startup membership, and what we can share with the region, our workflows and our technology, all of it open source

By Dinis Cruz · 2026-10-31 · article v1.0.0 · newsroom v0.14.23 · algarveportugalopen-sourcestartupsecosystemcollaborationarticle

Abstract: After a day at the Vibathon in Faro, we are joining Algarve Evolution. The Cyber Boardroom Limited, the company that publishes this newsroom, will take its Start membership, €150 a year, the category for startups from idea to growth, and I am talking with António Guerreiro, its executive director, about how we can work together. This article is about what we can bring. We are not offering a priced service here; the opportunity is to share what we do, our workflows and our technology, because all of it is open source and anyone in the region can use it with us or without us. Algarve Evolution works on three pillars, and each meets something we already run. For education and its buildathons, the loop I described today for turning a checklist into an app, explorer coding, and the way this newsroom turns a memo into a release. For startups and smaller companies, from its incubator to INOVA Algarve 3.0 on AI for tourism businesses, the villager services that release what founders build without changing it, the email and CRM workflow that runs on a £20 model account, and the behaviour policies and harness that keep agents inside their limits. For the ecosystem, from the regional digitalisation platform to its map of 24 digital communities, a newsroom that publishes under standards, vaults and knowledge graphs for a map that keeps itself current, and the open-source tools under all of it. In return we get what the Start membership offers, and more valuable, a community of founders with real problems, who are the best test of whether any of this is useful.

In short

  • We are joining Algarve Evolution. The Cyber Boardroom takes the Start membership, €150 a year.
  • I am talking with António Guerreiro, its executive director, about how we work together.
  • What we bring is what we already do: our workflows and our technology, all of it open source.
  • For education: the loop for turning a checklist into an app, explorer coding, and how a memo becomes a release.
  • For startups and SMEs: villager services, an email workflow on a model account, and policies that contain agents.
  • For the ecosystem: a newsroom with standards, vaults and graphs for the community map, and the tools beneath them.
  • What we get is a community with real problems, the best test of whether any of it is useful.

Why we are joining

I spent today at the Vibathon Algarve 2026, Algarve Evolution's sold-out day of building with AI at the University of the Algarve, and wrote up who Algarve Evolution are: a movement that began in 2018 as a small group of local entrepreneurs, now 106 active members, with a vision of the Algarve as a top southern European innovation hub and an economy beyond tourism.

That vision is close to ours. We build for small teams and founders: tools that run on the client side, that the user owns, that are open source and cheap to run. Some of our team live in the Algarve, I have a long history with the region, and the conversations I had today, two of which became from a checklist to an app you calibrate and villager services for explorer coders, showed me how much the people in that room could do with what we have built.

So The Cyber Boardroom Limited, the company that publishes this newsroom, will join on the Start membership, €150 a year, the category Algarve Evolution describes as "For startups from idea stage to active growth." I am talking with António Guerreiro, its executive director, about how we can work together.

What we bring: what we already do

We are not arriving with a priced service. The opportunity is simpler: to share what we are doing, our workflows and our technology, because all of it is open source. Anyone in the region can read it, use it and change it, with us or without us. Where it helps, we can show how; where someone wants it done for them, that is a separate conversation.

Algarve Evolution organises its work in three pillars, and each meets something we already run.

Education

Its education work runs from the university's entrepreneurship programme on the Penha, Gambelas and Portimão campuses to "The Company" with Junior Achievement Portugal, and buildathons like today's. What we can share:

  • The loop for turning a checklist into an app, written so it can be sent as a link: build the smallest version in half an hour, use it, log every run, calibrate.
  • Explorer coding: what building with an AI is good for, and the handover before anyone depends on it.
  • The newsroom's own pipeline, how a memo becomes a release, as a worked example of agents doing real work under rules.
  • A follow-up for every buildathon app, so a project from a day like today has a next step instead of stopping when the day ends.

Startups and smaller companies

Its startup work runs from its incubator, with the Founder2Founder growth programme, to INOVA Algarve 3.0, which helps the region's tourism businesses understand AI and robotics. What we can share:

  • Villager services: releasing what a founder built without changing what it does, deployed, locked down, tested, private where it needs to be, sold as finite work, never rent.
  • The inbox team as a recipe: the email and CRM workflow that runs on a model account of about £20 a month and a mailbox of about £7, which a small tourism business could copy.
  • Behaviour policies and a harness for agents: Agent Behaviour Policies say what an agent may do, and SHarness is our early work on enforcing it, for any business that lets an agent near its customers.
  • Websites you own: a site the business owns and changes by talking to it, not one it rents.

The ecosystem

Its ecosystem work runs from the digitalisation and ICT platform it leads for CCDR Algarve to the Algarve Tech Hub Summit and the Algarve Digital Innovators network, which has mapped 24 digital communities. What we can share:

  • A newsroom that can cover the region, published under standards, with corrections in the open, so founders and events get written up with their sources.
  • Vaults and knowledge graphs: a map of communities, people and companies that its members can keep current themselves, kept in an encrypted, versioned vault and published as a graph.
  • The tools beneath it, all open source: sgit and its vaults, the PKI and passkeys, the behaviour policies and SHarness, under licences anyone can use.
  • A bridge to Web Summit, where our team goes from 9 to 12 November, with meetups in the Algarve before it.

What we hope to get

The Start membership brings what Algarve Evolution lists for it: a place in the members' directory and on its website, its events and meetups, preferential access to a startup validation cohort, mentorship events and early-stage investor events.

The more valuable part is the community. Everything we build is tested best by people with real problems and no patience for tools that get in the way, and a room like today's was full of them. If what we share helps them, we will know quickly; if it does not, we will know that too.

What this does not claim

  • The membership is in progress, and the conversation with António Guerreiro is ongoing; nothing beyond the membership is agreed.
  • Everything here is an offer to share, not a priced service or a commitment by Algarve Evolution.
  • Algarve Evolution's programmes and figures are from its own site, read on 31 October 2026.
  • Some of what we share is early. SHarness and the passkey vault say so in their own articles.
  • My interest: I build the tools described, and The Cyber Boardroom is my company.
