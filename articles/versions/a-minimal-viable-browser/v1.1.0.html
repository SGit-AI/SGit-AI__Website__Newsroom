A minimal viable browser: what changed in v1.1.0

15 unchanged paragraphs, under In short {#in-short}, The biggest program we run {#biggest}

The cost of that shows up in the bug counts. In May, Mozilla wrote that it fixed 423 security bugs in Firefox in April 2026, against roughly 20 to 30 a month through 2025. 271 of them were found with an Anthropic model and fixed in Firefox 150: 180 rated high, 80 moderate, 11 low. ManyA number of them were sandbox escapes, written assuming the content process was already compromised and going after the privileged parent process through IPC, IndexedDB and image decoding. Chrome is seeing the same: one update on 6 October 2026 fixed 247 vulnerabilities. Counted from NVD by the organisation that filed them, Google's Chrome team filed 202 CVEs in 2025 and 3,246 in 2026 up to 17 October; Mozilla's filed 213 and 675 (Mozilla's also cover Thunderbird and other products). This is the code you do not ship argument at its largest: models now find what was always there, and every feature in the engine is a place to look.

7 unchanged paragraphs, under What a minimal viable browser looks like {#shape}

Firefox has had pieces of this, and dropped some of them. It had a site-specific browser, behind a hidden preference, and removed it in Firefox 8686](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1682593) in 2021, citingciting known bugs and weak user research. It has a kiosk mode, started with --kiosk. It has had site isolation on for every desktop user since Firefox 95, which is the process boundary a component model needs. And Servo, the engine Mozilla started in Rust, is now an embeddable library, published as 0.1.0 in April 2026 under Linux Foundation Europe.

2 unchanged paragraphs, under A browser for one agent {#agent}

The vendors say plainly that this will not be fixed. OpenAI wrote in December 2025 that prompt injection, "much like scams and social engineering on the web, is unlikely to ever be fully 'solved'" (TechCrunch). GoogleGoogle's callsChrome indirectsecurity promptteam injectionwrote in December 2025 that "the primary new threat facing all agentic browsers".browsers is indirect prompt injection"](https://blog.google/security/architecting-security-for-agentic/). Gartner told most organisations to block AI browsers for now.

5 unchanged paragraphs, under Every extension in its own box {#extensions}

The pattern in the incidents is not malicious code at install. It is a trusted extension that changes. Koi's ShadyPandaShadyPanda reportreport, as The Hacker News relayed it, covers 145 extensions with about 4.3 million installs,installs across 145 extensions (20 for Chrome and 125 for Edge, Koi's count of one phase), some of them "Featured" or "Verified", that were legitimate for years and then were weaponised through auto-update. Urban VPN Proxy, with a Featured badge, began harvesting every prompt and reply on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot from a July 2025 update, with no off switch. Firefox is not immune: Koi's later DarkSpectre attribution includes Firefox add-ons.
