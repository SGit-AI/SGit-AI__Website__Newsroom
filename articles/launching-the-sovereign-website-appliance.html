Launching the sovereign website appliance: the plan for a Kickstarter campaign that sells a sovereign website on a configured Android tablet or a Raspberry Pi, five tiers from a £50 supporter to £1,000, the two Pro tiers running local AI models that can build a website on the device itself, the hardware we would buy in the UK today, a European tablet, the UK as the base goal with countries and iOS as stretch goals, and why we launch alone

By Dinis Cruz · 2026-11-19 · article v1.1.0, 3 versions · newsroom v0.14.177 · sovereign-websitecrowdfundingkickstarterandroidraspberry-pigo-to-marketarticle

Abstract: This is the plan we arrived at, after an afternoon of turns recorded in the previous piece. We launch Sovereign Website on Kickstarter as an appliance: a sovereign website on a device its owner holds, an Android tablet or a Raspberry Pi, configured with the site, its editing and its monitoring. On the Pro tiers the device also runs a local AI model, so it can build and change a website, and run other tasks, with nothing leaving the house. That is proper sovereignty, and some people will want it for the setup alone. Five tiers. £50, the supporter: the website, set up and handed over, no hardware. £400 for a Raspberry Pi 5 with 8GB, about £230 of hardware today, and £500 for a tablet at about £250, such as a Lenovo Idea Tab at £209.99: we build the website for you, and the device runs it. £900 for a Raspberry Pi 5 with 16GB and 256GB, about £400, and £1,000 for a tablet at about £500, such as a Samsung Galaxy Tab S10 FE at £509: these run local models, and the website can be built on the device itself. The German-made Volla tablet with the de-Googled /e/OS is a European alternative. The Pi is the cheaper way in at each level; what is left of each pledge after the hardware pays for the setup, the site, the team inside it, support, shipping and the platform's fees. The UK is the base goal; Portugal is the first stretch goal; backers vote on the countries after it, and on iOS. All-or-nothing: if we do not reach the goal, nobody pays and we build nothing. And we launch on our own, with Android and open source, and talk to device makers once it works.

In short

  • On Kickstarter, as an appliance: a sovereign website on a device you hold.
  • Android first, or a Raspberry Pi; open source all the way down.
  • The Pro tiers run local AI models, and can build a website on the device itself.
  • Five tiers: £50 supporter; £400 Pi; £500 tablet; £900 Pi Pro; £1,000 tablet Pro.
  • The Pi is the cheaper way in at each level; the rest of each pledge pays for the setup.
  • The UK is the base goal, Portugal the first stretch goal, then countries and iOS by vote.
  • We launch alone, and partner once the flow works.
  • The appliances launch officially once the Kickstarter finishes.

The product

A sovereign website is a website its owner owns and can move (what is a sovereign website), built by agents and handed over with a team inside it. The appliance puts all of it on a device the owner holds:

  • An Android tablet, configured with the site, a way to change it by talking, monitoring of the live site, the AI app of their choice (they sign in with their own Claude or ChatGPT account), and a backup to an encrypted vault. The site can also run on the tablet itself, as an installable web app, so it does not depend on any app store.
  • Or a Raspberry Pi, a small computer that serves the site from home, deployed from the same repository by the same pipeline that deploys it to GitHub Pages.
  • On the Pro tiers, an AI model on the device itself, so the device can build and change the site, and run other tasks, without sending anything out.

On Kickstarter this is a physical product with a working prototype, which is what Kickstarter is for. The site on its own, without a device, stays on sale directly on sovereignwebsite.ai.

Android, and why not an iPad first

Android is open source and gives us full control of the device: sgit, the site and the agents' tools can all run on it. The team already had a signed Android tablet build running four hours after its first commit at Web Summit (native apps by agents), and I have the Android tablet I bought for Web Summit to make the prototype and film it. An iPad would mean the App Store and its review, a part we do not control, so iOS is a stretch goal, and backers can tell us if they want one.

The tiers

  • £50, Supporter. Your sovereign website, set up and handed over, with no hardware. A real thing, not a donation, and the cheapest way in.
  • £400, Pi. Your website, and a Raspberry Pi 5 with 8GB, in a starter kit with case, cooling, power and storage, set up with your site. We build the website for you; the Pi runs and serves it.
  • £500, Tablet. Your website, and an Android tablet at about £250, set up the same way: we build the site, the tablet runs it.
  • £900, Pi Pro, with local AI. Your website, and a Raspberry Pi 5 with 16GB and 256GB, running a local model: it can build and change websites itself.
  • £1,000, Tablet Pro, with local AI. Your website, and a tablet at about £500 with the memory and chip to run a local model, so the site can be built on the tablet itself.
  • Add-ons, at any tier: your own app made from your site, about £500; your own product configured to run on the device, about £1,500.

What is left of each pledge after the hardware pays for the setup, the site, the team inside it, support, shipping, and the platform's fees, which come to about 8 to 10% of a pledge. At today's prices that is about £135 on the £400 Pi, about £205 on the £500 tablet, and about £400 to £420 on the two Pro tiers, before shipping: the entry tiers are thin on purpose, and the Pro tiers carry the margin. We buy the hardware after the campaign, the best for that price at the time, so the models named below are examples, not promises.

No T-shirt. It only adds complexity.

What the hardware costs today

Examples from UK retailers on 19 November 2026, which will change before we ship:

  • Raspberry Pi, standard: an iRasptek starter kit for the Raspberry Pi 5 with 8GB and a 128GB card, case, cooler and power supply, £229.99 on Amazon UK. At The Pi Hut, the official Pi 5 starter kit with 8GB is £224.50.
  • Raspberry Pi, Pro: the same iRasptek kit with 16GB, £380.99 with a 128GB card and £397.99 with 256GB, on Amazon UK.
  • Tablet, about £250: the Lenovo Idea Tab, 11 inches, 8GB and 128GB, with a pen, £209.99 at John Lewis; or the Samsung Galaxy Tab A11+, from £199 to £259 by option, also at John Lewis.
  • Tablet, about £500: the Samsung Galaxy Tab S10 FE, 12GB and 256GB, with Galaxy AI, £509 at John Lewis. Two others to compare at the time, whose current prices we could not confirm: Lenovo's Yoga Tab, with a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 and 12GB, and the OnePlus Pad 3, with a Snapdragon 8 Elite and 12GB, launched at £529.
  • A European tablet: the Volla Tablet, made by Volla in Germany, sold by Murena with /e/OS, a version of Android without Google: 12.6 inches, 12GB and 512GB, €569.99 in Murena's current offer, normally €649.99. Its chip is old and slow for AI on the device, so it would be a European alternative at the Tablet Pro price without the local model: a sovereignty choice, not an AI one.

Memory prices have risen sharply this year, and Raspberry Pi prices with them, which is one more reason the devices are chosen at the time we buy them.

Local AI on the Pro tiers, and sites made on the device

The difference between the standard and the Pro tiers is a local model. On the £400 Pi and the £500 tablet, we build the website for you with our own setup, and the device runs it; they could run a small model, but not well enough to be worth promising. On the £900 Pi Pro and the £1,000 Tablet Pro, the device runs a local model of its own. I have already tried a model running on my Web Summit tablet, in the browser; it was slow, but it worked, and for this it does not need to be fast. A Raspberry Pi 5 with 16GB should run useful open-weight models, more slowly; that is what we will test first.

That changes what the device is. It is not only where the site lives; it is where the site can be made. On the Pro tiers, point the build page from watch your website being built at the device's own model, and a website is built on the device itself, with no key, no cloud and nothing leaving the house. The same model can run other tasks, and connect to other things the owner chooses. Some people will buy the appliance for that alone, because that is proper sovereignty: a computer they own, running a model they own, doing work for them under a behaviour policy they can read.

So the campaign should show it working: websites built entirely on a Pi Pro and on a Tablet Pro, published as examples, with how long each took.

A website served from home

A sovereign website served from a box its owner holds is sovereignty you can put on a shelf. There are two ways to reach it from the internet. A dynamic DNS name and port forwarding on the home router keep everything in the owner's control, but fail when the internet provider shares one address between customers, and reveal the home's address. A tunnel service works everywhere, but the common ones are proprietary, which is exactly the kind of part in the middle a sovereign website avoids, so a self-hosted tunnel to a small server the owner controls is the better version.

So the honest design is that the Pi is a host, not the only host: the site stays on GitHub Pages or any static host, and the Pi serves it from home and keeps a full copy. The site then runs in more than one place, one of them a machine its owner holds, which is the seventh principle: proven, not claimed.

The goal and the stretch goals

  • The base goal is the UK, in pounds and in English, with every page in our other languages from day one. It should be a goal we are confident of reaching early; the stretch goals carry it further.
  • Portugal is the first stretch goal: going there properly, with local partners, demo sites, domain advice for .pt names, and a launch there.
  • Then countries by vote, each one a place we go to, shown on a map that lights up as backers unlock it.
  • iOS is a stretch goal too.

The appliances, the Raspberry Pi sovereign website among them, launch officially once the Kickstarter finishes: nothing is sold as an appliance before then. Stretch goals are revealed as the campaign goes. It is all-or-nothing: backers are charged only if the goal is met when the campaign ends, and the money arrives once, after it closes. If we do not reach it, nobody pays and we produce nothing.

Value first

Every backer can see their own website before anything ships: five designs, made by agents and sealed in a vault they open with a QR code, a link or a zip (your website, from a vault). The campaign video shows a real tablet, the one from Web Summit, running a real site.

Launch alone, partner later

We do not need a device maker or an operating system to launch with us. We launch with Android out of the box, open source, and our own flow working end to end. Then we talk to the companies that make tablets, small computers and open operating systems, Europe's first, as a project that is already funded and already shipping, which is a much better conversation. That is the open source strategy: anyone can build on it, and we are the ones who showed it working.

After the campaign

Late pledges on Kickstarter, then pre-orders on sovereignwebsite.ai, and the plain site on sale there throughout. Patreon later, for the people who want to support the work as it goes, and for the creators whose sites we build to be supported on their own pages (the launch plan in eleven turns).

What this does not claim

  • Nothing has been submitted to Kickstarter; its review may ask for changes, and its rules on AI and on services apply.
  • Hardware prices are examples from UK retailers on 19 November 2026; two tablet prices could not be confirmed.
  • Local AI on the Pro devices is to be tested; no website has been built on one yet, and local models will be slower than cloud ones.
  • The appliance's software, the editing, monitoring and vault backup on the device, is a plan; the Android build and the sites exist.
  • The goal amount is not yet set.
