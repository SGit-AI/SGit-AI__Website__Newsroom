A website with a team inside: a conductor and six agents in the owner's repository.

## In short

- **We are not delivering a website;** we are delivering a website with a team inside it.
- **Six or seven agents,** already set up and coordinated: conductor, designer, writer, developer, reviewer, translator, keeper.
- **The owner talks to one of them,** the conductor, in plain words.
- **Each has a brief and a behaviour policy,** in the repository; every change is a signed commit.
- **Nothing goes live until the owner says yes.**
- **The team is the owner's,** and moves with the site.

## Not a site, a team

When we describe a [Sovereign Agentic Website](sovereign-agentic-website.md), it is easy to make it sound like a better website builder: a site made by agents, handed over, owned. That is true, and it undersells it. What the owner gets is not only the site but the team that made it, still there, already set up and already coordinated, inside the site's own repository.

In [the website cell](the-website-cell.md) the hand-over was to the owner's own Claude, with a CLAUDE.md and a behaviour policy to keep it safe. That works: Yiheng's agent took over her site within an hour. But one general agent, starting from a blank conversation each time, is a lot to ask of a person who just wants a new page. A team with roles, briefs and a way of working is different. It is what we run ourselves, in this newsroom and in [the team's operating system](the-teams-operating-system.md) we used at Web Summit, and it is, I think, very powerful.

## The seven roles

- **Conductor.** The one the owner talks to. It turns a request into a plan, hands out the work, follows it, reports back, and asks before anything goes live.
- **Designer.** Layouts and images: the five directions at the start, and afterwards every new page in the style the owner chose.
- **Writer.** The words, in the owner's voice, from what they say and send: a CV, a menu, a price list, a voice note.
- **Developer.** Builds the change, keeps the checks green, and publishes once the owner approves.
- **Reviewer.** Reads every change before the owner sees it: are the prices right, is any claim untrue, is a customer named without consent, has anything private or secret slipped in.
- **Translator.** Keeps every page in each of the site's languages, so a second language is not a second job.
- **Keeper.** The weekly care nobody remembers: broken links, speed, accessibility, a certificate, the domain's renewal date.

A small site may need six of these, or five; a shop or a site in three languages may want all seven. The point is not the number. It is that each role is written down, so the owner does not have to know what to ask for.

## One request, through the team

One request through the team: a Mother's Day page, from the owner's words to live, with one yes.

Take a florist who writes, on a Monday: "A Mother's Day page, with the bouquets, by Friday." The conductor plans it: words, a banner, the Portuguese version, a check and a date. The writer and the designer work side by side on the page and the banner; the translator follows; the reviewer checks that the prices match the shop, that no customer is named and that every link works. The owner gets one preview link and says yes, or asks for a change. The developer publishes, the checks run, and the page is live. The keeper adds it to the weekly checks, and takes it down after the day if she asked for that.

She did not learn a tool, write a prompt or open an editor. She asked, looked once, and said yes. That is the difference between handing someone a website and handing them a team.

## How they are coordinated

None of this needs a platform of ours. It is all files in the owner's repository, the same way our own teams work:

- **A CLAUDE.md** that says who is in the team and how a request flows.
- **A brief per role,** what it does and what it hands to whom.
- **A behaviour policy per role:** the designer can change styles and images but not prices; the writer can draft but not publish; only the developer publishes, and only after the owner's yes; nobody can touch the domain, payments or the policy itself ([feature behaviour policies](feature-behaviour-policies.md)).
- **The work as files:** each request, its plan and its status, so the owner, or any other agent, can see what is happening.
- **Every change a signed commit,** and checks before anything is live, as this newsroom's are ([policies in practice](policies-in-practice.md)).

The team runs on the owner's own Claude account and wakes when there is a request or a weekly check, not all the time, so it costs what it is used for.

## Why it matters

- **For the owner,** it removes the hard part. Owning a site was always possible; looking after one was the work. Now the looking after comes with it.
- **For sovereignty,** the team is in the repository, so it belongs to the owner as the site does. Move the site to another host, and the team moves with it; stop paying us, and the team keeps working ([sovereignty you can move](sovereignty-you-can-move.md)).
- **For behaviour policies,** every one of these sites is a small team of agents working under written rules for a real person. A thousand sites is a thousand working examples, and a practical test of [RiskMandate](feature-behaviour-policies.md)'s ideas.
- **Against the platforms,** a website builder gives you tools; an agency gives you people at an hourly rate. This gives you a team, for one payment, that you own.

## What this does not claim

- **The sites we hand over today** carry a CLAUDE.md and a behaviour policy for one agent; the seven-role team in the repository is the next step, not yet built.
- **The florist is an illustration,** not a customer, and the flow is not a timed run.
- **The roles are a proposal** for the team building sovereignwebsite.ai to refine; which run on which sites will depend on the site.
- **The agents run on the owner's own AI account,** at its cost and within its limits.
