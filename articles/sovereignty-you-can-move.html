Sovereignty you can move: why where your data is hosted is not sovereignty, why being able to move the site, the data and the schema is, why that makes the website cell a direct competitor to Wix, Squarespace and Fliplet with a proper sovereign offer, European-aligned, multilingual and privacy-first, and the business that other companies can build on it

By Dinis Cruz · 2026-11-19 · article v1.0.1, 2 versions · newsroom v0.14.164 · sovereigntyagenticcellswebsitesprivacyeuropeopen-sourcepositioningarticle

Abstract: We should say plainly what we are: a direct competitor to the website platforms, Wix, Squarespace, Fliplet and the rest. And we should compete on the thing they cannot offer, which is sovereignty, properly understood. Most talk about digital sovereignty is about where the data is hosted: an EU data centre, a UK company. That is not sovereignty. You do not have sovereignty if you cannot move your data, your site and the shape of it, the schema, to somewhere else; if the provider's terms decide what you may do; or if the company can be bought and its terms and its jurisdiction change with it. Squarespace was taken private by an investment firm in 2024; a UK company today can have owners elsewhere tomorrow. The website cell gives the owner what sovereignty actually requires: the files, the schema and the history in their own repository, hostable anywhere, editable by their own Claude, under open-source code nobody can withdraw, with no cookies unless they choose them, in their language, and moving into their ownership within three months. That is a sovereign website, European-aligned and multilingual from day one. And because it is open, other businesses can build on it: partners, agencies, newsrooms, with RiskMandate's behaviour policies keeping the agents in line. There is a large business here on its own.

In short

  • We are a direct competitor to the website platforms, and should say so.
  • Hosting location is not sovereignty.
  • Sovereignty is being able to move: the data, the site and its schema.
  • Terms and owners change; a platform's sovereignty moves when it is bought.
  • The website cell passes the tests: your repository, any host, open code, yours in three months.
  • Privacy-first, European-aligned, multilingual.
  • Others can build on it, with RiskMandate's policies.

A direct competitor

We have been describing the website cell as something new. It is, in how it is made. But in what it replaces, it is plain: it is a website, and the people who would buy it today buy Wix, Squarespace, a WordPress host or, increasingly, an AI builder like Fliplet (rent or own). So we should position it as a direct competitor, and compete on the one thing those platforms cannot offer without giving up their business (no lock-in).

Where it is hosted is not sovereignty

Most of the talk about digital sovereignty is about location: data kept in an EU data centre, a supplier incorporated at home. That matters, and it is not enough. A site hosted in Dublin on a platform whose terms say you cannot take it with you is not yours, wherever the servers are. Fliplet keeps accounts in Dublin by default; its standard terms still say the service and what you build in it are Fliplet's, and that it offers no way to transfer or copy your applications.

What sovereignty requires

On our open source site I set it out in four steps (sovereignty requires open source):

  • Only open source makes independence possible: "if you suddenly get cut off from the service, at least you have a chance".
  • The schemas matter as much as the code: "the data schemas are as important as the code ... you need to be able to control it, to own it". An export in a format the vendor chose is access, not control.
  • Without ownership you are one SLA away from losing access, and whoever has jurisdiction over the supplier has it over you.
  • Company nationality is not sovereignty: "A European company gets acquired by a US company and the sovereignty moves, you lose it."

That last one is not abstract. Squarespace was taken private by an investment firm in 2024. A company that is British today can have owners, investors and a jurisdiction elsewhere tomorrow, and its terms with them.

The website cell passes the tests

  • Can you move it? Yes: static files in your own GitHub repository, with their history, hostable anywhere, from GitHub Pages for free to any web host (one commit, one site, one owner).
  • Do you own the schema? Yes: the structure of the site and its content are files in your repository, in open formats, changed by your own Claude.
  • Who sets the terms? You: no platform, no subscription, and open-source code that nobody can withdraw (governance from zero).
  • What if we are bought? Nothing changes for you: you already hold everything, and every site is expected to move into its owner's ownership within three months.

Privacy-first, European, multilingual

The rest follows from the same design. No cookies unless the owner adds them, and no tracking by us (static against the platforms). Built in Europe, on open standards, and deployable in any region the owner chooses. Multilingual from the first day: agenticcells.ai itself is in ten languages (you bring the business, we build the site). That is a proper sovereign website, not a sovereign label on a rented one.

A business others can build on

Because it is open, the website cell is also a base for other businesses: partners who sell and support it in their own country and language, agencies, newsrooms, councils (partners sell, we build). The agents that build and maintain these sites work under behaviour policies from RiskMandate, which is where the alignment with our other company lies: sovereign sites, built by agents whose reach is written down and checked. I think there is a large business here on its own.

What this does not claim

  • Sovereignty is a set of possibilities, not a guarantee: owning the files means you can move; it does not move them for you.
  • The platforms' positions are from their pages and standard terms; negotiated contracts may differ.
  • The three-month ownership expectation is a soft requirement for now.
