## Why the cell

[AgentiCCells](the-intersection.md) today explains itself by size: a ladder from one agent to a network of teams, the six setups of an agentic system, two modes of who runs the team ([six setups of an agentic system](six-setups-of-an-agentic-system.md)). That is right, and it is how a buyer understands the price. But it is not how a buyer thinks about the problem. Nobody wakes up wanting "a few sessions with lanes and a vault". They want help replying to email, keeping a website, following up after an event.

So the cell itself should be at the centre: the unit of what we build, what we sell, and how we work. We need to think hard about what a cell looks like, how it is sold, what the customer is buying, how it is set up, and how it actually works day to day.

## The anatomy of a cell

The anatomy of a cell: an objective, a deliverable and a behaviour policy always; other parts as the job needs.

Every cell has three things it cannot do without:

- **An objective:** the one job it exists for, said in a sentence the customer would use.
- **A deliverable:** what you have at the end of each day, week or run: drafted replies, a live site, a debrief, a list of what waits on you.
- **A behaviour policy:** what it may reach, what it was asked to do, and what stands in its way ([where assist becomes execute](where-assist-becomes-execute.md)).

And then as many parts as its job needs: a Claude session to do the work; a vault for its memory, files and record ([how a vault works](how-a-vault-works.md)); a repository and CI; a website on its own subdomain; lanes and keys to talk, sign and approve; a schedule of wakes; a connector to email, a calendar, Odoo or a CRM; and a person who owns it.

That range is the point. A cell can be as small as one Claude session replying to email under a policy, or as rich as a team keeping a website with a factory behind it ([the site factory works](the-site-factory-works.md)). Every website is a cell. Now that the factory creates repositories and changes AWS from a commit, cells can carry capabilities that were too painful to set up before.

## A page for every cell

A page for every cell: what it does, what you get, examples, use cases, its policy, and four ways to start.

Selling has to be as focused as the cell. Every cell gets its own page, built the same way:

- **What it does,** in one line, and **what you get,** concretely.
- **Examples:** real ones, from our own work and our customers', with links.
- **Use cases:** the situations where people reach for it.
- **Its behaviour policy,** in plain words: what it can reach, what it will never do.

And, on the side, four ways to start:

1. **Free:** the recipe. The brief, the policy and the steps to run it yourself, with your own Claude. Most people should start here, and many will stay here, which is fine: it drives usage.
2. **Our agent helps you:** a specialist agent reviews your setup and tells you what to fix ([specialist agents for every team](specialist-agents-for-every-team.md)).
3. **We help you:** two sessions with a person, on the ladder at £1,500.
4. **We set it up for you:** in your accounts or ours, from £500 ([atomic cells, end to end](atomic-cells-end-to-end.md)).

## A catalogue that grows one need at a time

The catalogue grows the simplest possible way. Someone says: I need help with this. I need help sending these emails. I need help keeping this page current. And we add a cell for it. That is the workflow now: find the use case, write the cell, give it a page.

Some cells are general: reply to your inbox, follow up after an event, a weekly digest, a website you steer, translate your site, debrief your meetings, a CRM of your contacts, a policy for your agent, a vault for a project. Many will be specific to one industry, and that is good. A clinic's cell drafts patient letters in plain words for a clinician to approve; a farm's takes orders by email and keeps a page of what is in season; a council's answers the same questions every week, with the sources. The cell is the same underneath; its page, its examples and its words belong to that industry, and it connects to that industry's systems ([integrate with whatever is on the other side](integrate-with-whatever-is-on-the-other-side.md)).

## Live in cells first

The best test of whether cells are easy to use is to use them ourselves, every day. Our team should start thinking in cells: what is this piece of work, what is its objective, its deliverable, its policy? And I should move my own Claude work into cells. Today much of it is sessions that come and go; as cells, each would come packaged with its brief, its policy, its vault and its record, and each would become an example on a page.

The same logic as the CRM applies: what we use ourselves gets better fastest, because the feedback loop is ours ([a CRM for us and for our agents](a-crm-for-us-and-our-agents.md)). Every cell we run becomes a page, a recipe and a proof.

## What to build next

1. **A cell format:** a folder with the objective, the deliverable, the behaviour policy, the recipe and the parts, the same for every cell.
2. **The page template** for agenticcells.ai, with the four ways to start, generated from that folder.
3. **The first ten cells,** from what we already do: reply to your inbox, follow up after an event, a website you steer, debrief your meetings, draft bills in Odoo, a policy for your agent, a vault for a project, a weekly digest, a page per place, CI behaviour policies.
4. **Our own work in cells:** each of us moves one recurring piece of work into a cell this week.
5. **A request form:** "I need help with...", into an agent's lane, which proposes the next cell.

## What this does not claim

- **The page and catalogue are mock-ups;** the cells exist today as pieces of our work, not yet as a catalogue.
- **Prices follow the AgentiCCells ladder,** which its site calls first proposals.
- **The industry cells are examples,** not offers with customers yet.
