No lock-in: why the one thing that most separates the website cell from Wix, Squarespace, Fliplet and the rest is that the customer can leave, what the platforms' own terms and export pages say, how large the market of people paying monthly for sites they cannot take with them is, and what a site that promises to move into its owner's hands should say on its front page

By Dinis Cruz · 2026-11-19 · article v1.0.1, 2 versions · newsroom v0.14.164 · agenticcellslock-inownershipwebsitesmarketpositioningarticle

Abstract: The more I compare the website cell with what is out there, the clearer it is that its biggest difference is not the AI, the designs or the price. It is that the customer can leave. Wix's own help page says its sites "need to be hosted and operated on Wix's servers". Squarespace exports only part of a site, in WordPress's format. Fliplet's standard terms say the service and what you build in it are Fliplet's, that it offers no way to transfer or copy your applications, and that when the terms end you must stop using it, with no right to a copy. And the market of people in that position is very large: Wix alone has 6.11 million paying subscriptions, Squarespace reported more than 5.2 million, and both raised their prices this year. Against that, the website cell is files in the owner's own repository, hostable anywhere, edited by their own Claude, paid for once; and, as agreed today, every site we build is expected to move into its owner's ownership within three months. No lock-in should not be a footnote. It should be the headline, and I have briefed the AgentiCCells team to make it one.

In short

  • The biggest difference is that the customer can leave.
  • Wix: sites must stay on Wix's servers.
  • Squarespace: a partial export, in WordPress's format.
  • Fliplet: no transfer, no code, no copy when the terms end.
  • The market in that position is millions of paying sites, with prices rising.
  • The website cell: files in the owner's repository, and ownership within three months.
  • No lock-in should be the headline.

The difference that matters most

When I set the website cell beside the alternatives, the AI is not the difference; everyone has it now (rent or own). The designs and the price matter, but they can be matched. What cannot be matched by a platform without changing its business is this: the customer can leave, with their site, whenever they want, and owes nobody anything.

What the platforms say

  • Wix: sites "need to be hosted and operated on Wix's servers" and cannot be moved to another host, its help page says (static against the platforms).
  • Squarespace: the only export is a WordPress-format file with pages, one blog and their text and images, leaving out the store, products, audio and video blocks, other page types and the styles.
  • Fliplet: its standard terms say "Fliplet and/or its licensor(s) are the sole owners of the Service", including what users build in it; that "Fliplet does not offer a facility for You to transfer or copy any Content or Applications" to another provider; and that on termination you must stop using it, with no right to a copy of any application.

None of this is hidden; it is the business model. A platform earns every month because you stay.

The size of the market

The people in that position are not a niche. Wix reported 6.11 million premium subscriptions at the end of 2025, and Squarespace more than 5.2 million unique subscriptions in 2024; WordPress runs 38.5% of all websites. Estimates of the website builder market run from about $3.6 billion to $6.4 billion in 2026. And this year Squarespace raised three of its four plans by 18% to 26%, and Wix raised its prices on new subscriptions and renewals. When you cannot leave, the price is whatever the platform says.

What the website cell does instead

  • Your repository: the site is files in the owner's own GitHub repository, with its history (the website cell).
  • Any host: static files serve from GitHub Pages, for free, or from any web host (one commit, one site, one owner).
  • Your Claude: the owner changes it by talking to their own Claude, not to our platform.
  • Paid once: no subscription from us.
  • Yours within three months: as agreed today, every site we build is expected to move into its owner's ownership within three months, sooner if they ask; a soft requirement for now, which may become a rule.
  • Open: the code and the policies are open source, so even we cannot lock anyone in (governance from zero).

Make it the headline

So I have briefed the AgentiCCells team to put no lock-in at the top of agenticcells.ai, not in the small print: the ability to leave as the first promise, the platforms' own words beside it, and the three-month ownership expectation in the terms. It fits the pitch we already have: "a site that looks better, that you own, that nobody can lock you into" (the pitch). Of the six promises, this is the one nobody else can copy without giving up their business.

What this does not claim

  • The platforms' positions are from their own pages and standard terms, read in November 2026; negotiated contracts may differ.
  • Market figures are estimates from different sources and definitions.
  • The three-month ownership promise is a soft requirement for now.
