Websites for the moment: a first calendar, and the weekly loop that keeps it going.

## In short

- **Topical sites, built fast,** around what people are looking for now.
- **Run continuously,** to see what an agent team does over time.
- **A loop:** five proposals a week, I pick, a day to build each, within a budget.
- **Every fact sourced,** nothing presented as official, each site with a sunset date.
- **A first calendar,** from GTA VI today to January.

## Why

Every experiment we run leaves something behind, an arrow in the quiver for later, and this one is designed to leave many. Today someone I met by chance heard me describe the sovereign website, liked the name and understood it straight away; that is good validation, and it is also the reason to show it working, over and over, in public. Websites for the moment do that. They drive traffic, they show the factory and the agents at work, and they give an agent team a long-running job, which is the best way to see what it does when it is left to work.

We have everything it needs: Claude, search, embeddings, the Google APIs, the [site factory](the-site-factory-works.md) that makes a site from a commit, and now a place to put the results.

## The loop

1. **Propose.** A trend agent reads search and the news and proposes five sites a week, each with who it is for, what it would show, its sources, and when its moment ends.
2. **Pick.** I choose, or say not now.
3. **Build in a day.** A small team, a researcher, a writer, a designer and a reviewer, makes the site through the factory, within a token budget set per site.
4. **Live for its moment.** Each site has a sunset date, after which it is archived but stays in its repository, as everything does.
5. **Count.** Visits, where they came from, and what people did, so the next proposals learn from the last.

The rules sit in the team's behaviour policy: every fact has its source; nothing pretends to be official or uses another brand's marks as its own; nothing is copied that a site's terms forbid; and anything a person would have to check, a date or a price, says where it came from and when it was read.

## A first calendar

- **Now.** GTA VI, which its publisher has set for release today, 19 November, on consoles: a sourced, unofficial guide to what is known. The Web Summit pieces, brought back as sites of their own: the [X-ray](the-teams-operating-system.md), the voice-note-to-deck demo, and the Galaxy. And [One Human, Nine Agents, Nineteen Hours](the-book-is-on-leanpub.md), with a site of its own.
- **December.** Christmas last posting dates, from each carrier's own page, in the UK and Portugal. An advent of tools: a new tool site every day to the 24th, from [the tools waves](every-tool-its-own-website.md). The Kickstarter, live: the total, the map of countries, and what has unlocked ([the appliance plan](launching-the-sovereign-website-appliance.md)).
- **Late December.** New Year in London and Lisbon, sourced. The year in AI models: releases and prices. [A sovereign website on a Raspberry Pi](a-sovereign-website-on-a-raspberry-pi.md), the experiment, as it runs.
- **January on.** What launched at CES, read for sovereignty. The next big sporting tournament when it comes, since those are where topical sites earn their keep. And whatever the trend agent finds next.

## What we expect to learn

How good an agent team's ideas are when it is left to propose them; how fast and how well it builds under a budget; which kinds of site bring people in and keep them; what goes wrong over weeks rather than hours; and how much of a site's value is in its timing. Each of those is a finding worth publishing, whichever way it goes.

## What this does not claim

- **None of these sites exists yet;** the team has picked the first topics.
- **GTA VI's date is its publisher's announced one,** as reported; we have not checked whether it shipped.
- **The Galaxy and the voice-note demo** are not described here; each deserves its own piece.
