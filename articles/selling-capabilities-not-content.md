## In short

- **Florent Daudens asks where the products are,** now that publishers have the protocols.
- **His answer, which I agree with:** sell capabilities, not content.
- **The Storm Charles site was a capability:** 2,618 pages in three days, every fact sourced and timed, made possible by months of work on how we run agents.
- **PrompTugal.news going local is a product:** a site per town, each a lens on one shared graph.
- **For access, permissions and payment,** we have answers: keys people own, read keys, licences, a meter, pages for agents.
- **This is our second reply to Florent,** after [would you pay to plug this newsroom in?](would-you-pay-to-plug-this-newsroom-in.md)

## His question

In [We have the protocols. Where are the products?](https://fdaudens.substack.com/p/we-have-the-protocols-now-what-are) (and [on LinkedIn](https://lnkd.in/p/eduvEsh7)), Florent Daudens, co-founder of Mizal, lists what has arrived in a few months: Reuters and Getty with MCP services, university presses piloting access through ChatGPT Edu and a shared gateway, the Internet Licensing Collective's first customer, and a telemetry standard from a coalition of large publishers. His point is that protocols alone cannot create a market: "We need to move beyond licensing content and start selling capabilities." The opportunity he is most excited about is connecting the subscriptions people already pay for to their personal agents, because "people already pay for journalism. But their AI agents don't have access to the content they're paying for."

I agree with all of it. The question is what a capability looks like, and we have two newsrooms that suggest an answer.

## A storm site is a capability

When Storm Charles reached Lisbon during Web Summit and the summit called off its second day, we had a new site for it the same evening, and three days later it had 2,618 pages and 1,434 versions, with desks for the weather, flights, transport and the cloud, and every fact carried its source and the time it was read ([Storm Charles, a newsroom built in a night](storm-charles-a-newsroom-built-in-a-night.md)).

It would be easy to tell that as one request to an AI, and it would be wrong. The site could be set up that quickly only because of the work that came before it: months of building the method our agents run on. Conductors that start and brief other agents, roles with written mandates, and hand-overs when a session gets long: the storm site alone passed roles to fresh sessions 106 times. State kept in files, not in conversations. A key and an inbox lane for every agent. Release gates that refuse a page that breaks a rule. And observability and visualisation, so we could see what more than a hundred sessions were doing that day and steer them. The speed was the dividend of that structure ([the team's operating system](the-teams-operating-system.md), [six setups of an agentic system](six-setups-of-an-agentic-system.md)).

What it produced is not content to license. It is a capability: a live, sourced picture of a situation, kept current, that a person can read and an agent can query, with the provenance to trust it. The value is not in any one article; it is in having the whole picture, now, with its sources. And the capability behind it, a method that can stand up a sourced newsroom on any subject in hours, is the real product.

## A newsroom by place is a product

[PrompTugal.news](promptugal-news-the-newsroom-we-ran.md) ran a newsroom of agent desks for Web Summit: 73 signed articles in ten days, a reviewer for every draft, every source opened at its own address. Now it is going local ([PrompTugal.news by place](promptugal-news-by-place.md)): one site for Portugal, one for the Algarve and one for every Algarve town, each on its own subdomain, each a lens on one shared semantic graph of stories, places, companies, institutions and events. A story about a company in Loulé leads on Loulé's site and is a small item on the national one.

That is the kind of product Florent is asking for. A town's site is a service for the people, businesses and agents of that town: what is happening in AI where they are, with every fact sourced, and the graph behind it to ask questions of. An agent working for a business in Faro does not need our articles; it needs that lens, and the graph, on demand.

## Answers to the open questions

Florent lists the questions Mizal is still working out: the user experience, authentication and permissions, what publishers will expose, and whether people want this at all. We have been working on the same ones, from the other side:

- **Access for agents:** every page of our newsrooms has a markdown twin and an `llms.txt` index, so an agent reads the page, not the chrome ([reading your way into an agentic team](reading-your-way-into-an-agentic-team.md)).
- **Authentication and permissions:** keys people own. Each person gets a key, and an agent acting for them can hold one; private material sits in encrypted vaults opened by a key, and public material by a published read key ([a key for every person](a-key-for-every-person.md)).
- **Conditions:** a licence on every page, and, for the agents themselves, Agent Behaviour Policies that say what each may reach and do ([what can your agent reach?](what-can-your-agent-reach.md)).
- **Payment and verification:** this newsroom charges for what is read, not what is scrolled, with the reader in control of the meter ([your reading, your controls](your-reading-your-controls.md)); articles and releases are signed; and our observability records who read what.

None of these is finished, and a shared standard for telemetry would help us as much as anyone. But they are working pieces, and they point at an answer to "do people want this": they want the capability, the town's picture or the storm's, delivered where they already are.

## What I would build next

With Florent's framing, I would build three things. A subscription that a person's agent can hold as a key, so the paywall is a permission, not a login page. A lens per place and per topic, sold as a capability, with its graph exposed to agents under a licence. And a decision service on top, a graph for one decision with every source ([a market for sweets](a-market-for-sweets.md)), which is where trusted journalism is worth most to an agent: at the moment its person has to decide.

## What this does not claim

- **The local sites are a strategy, not yet built;** the storm site and PrompTugal.news ran in November 2026.
- **The meter and keys are working pieces on our own sites,** not a product sold to publishers.
- **Florent's examples and quotes are from his post** of 15 November 2026, summarised here.
