## More than a presentation

For years my advice to anyone looking for a job has been to make a presentation about yourself, and to act as the person in the job you want ([act as the job you want](act-as-the-job-you-want.md)). These days a presentation is not enough. You should have a website: something that shows the depth of your work, not a line on a CV but the project, what you did and how; something that shows you in action; something that makes you more human, with a video of you talking; something that brings out the best of you as a candidate.

That is what the people's sites on [the hub](the-showroom.md) already do: an actor with her reels, a designer with her shelves of packaging, a musician with his film, a project manager with the story of the team of agents she ran ([a website that is her](a-website-that-is-her.md)). A CV says what you did. A site like this shows it.

## Four levels of involvement

What I find interesting is that this is not one product. It is a set of levels, and each person chooses how far to go:

1. **The site.** A better version of your CV: your own material, in your own words, with your photos credited and a video of you, at your own address, in designs you choose from. The simplest version is the £50 one ([launching the appliance](launching-the-sovereign-website-appliance.md)). Here is my website; you use it, and that is fine.
2. **Yours to change.** The site is handed over, and you change it with your own Claude or ChatGPT: a new project, a better photo, a version tuned for each role you apply for ([the first round trip](the-first-round-trip.md)).
3. **The team inside.** You start working with the agents in the site: ask one to research a company before an interview, draft a cover letter from your own material, prepare the questions you might be asked, and you review what they give you ([a website with a team inside](a-website-with-a-team-inside.md)).
4. **An agentic worker.** You make it your own: you delegate, you check, you learn from the agents and they learn from you, and the site's history shows how you work.

## Why the last levels matter

There is a lot of value now in being hired because you are an agentic worker: because you know how to work with agents, how to get real work out of them and how to keep them under control ([hired for the workflow](hired-for-the-workflow.md)). Companies are moving from pilots to products, and the people they need are the ones who can make agent teams operational, not only the ones who build models.

That is what we provide, almost as a side effect of making the site: a crash course in how to use agents, how to work with them, how to learn from them and how to be operational with them. We give it to people on a plate. They do not have to set anything up; the team, the repository and the policies are already there. And the evidence is built in: a site with a team inside, a history of every change, and the person's own requests and reviews in it, is something they can show an employer and talk about in an interview.

## Not for everyone, and that is fine

Some people will not need this, and some will not want it. They want a good site and they will use it, and the first level is a complete product on its own. But I think a lot of others will want to go further, especially those starting out, for whom this may be the first time they work with a team of agents ([a career website, not a CV](a-career-website-not-a-cv.md)). The service should make every level easy to reach and none of them compulsory.

## What we need to work out

- **What each level includes,** and how much of it is agents and how much is people.
- **Prices beyond the first level,** which are not set.
- **How the crash course is given:** inside the site, as tasks the team suggests, or with sessions alongside.
- **How a candidate shows the work to an employer** without exposing what should stay private.

## What this does not claim

- **The levels are a proposal;** the sites and the handover exist, the crash course does not yet.
- **A site does not guarantee a job,** and no example is claimed to have got one.
