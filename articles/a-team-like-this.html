A team like this, for sale: what we sell is not the platform, it is the agentic team that built it, its roles, its behaviour policies, its security passes and its speed, with the code open and the data public, and who I want to talk to now that PrompTugal.news is a working data platform and not an experiment

By Dinis Cruz · 2026-11-18 · article v1.0.0 · newsroom v0.14.158 · agenticcellspromptugalagent-teamsbehaviour-policiesgo-to-marketseriesarticle

Abstract: The last part of the series is the offer. Everything I have described, a newsroom that became a data platform in two weeks, a factory that turns a commit into a site and its owner's access, money flowing back to the sources, sites built on the data in minutes, a library at the heart of it, was built by an agentic team: conductors, a place agent for every town, data agents, a security agent reviewing every change before and after, a behaviour policy for every agent, a librarian. That team is what we sell. The code is open source and the data is public, so anyone can take it; what a customer buys is a team like this one, set up for them, with its policies, running their sources, their sites and their workflows, at the speed they have seen here. It is a live demo of what an agentic team does under agent behaviour policies. And now that PrompTugal.news is a working platform and not a small experiment, I want to talk to the people who could use it, integrate with it, fund it or feed it: councils and regional bodies, tourism, universities, newsrooms, the platforms we cannot read yet, and anyone who needs to know what is happening in a place. Part six of the series.

In short

  • We do not sell the platform; it is open.
  • We sell a team like the one that built it.
  • Roles, behaviour policies, security passes, and the speed.
  • A live demo of agentic teams under agent behaviour policies.
  • Now a platform, not an experiment.
  • Who I want to talk to: councils, tourism, universities, newsrooms, platforms, anyone who needs to know a place.

Part six of gaining speed.

What built it

Behind everything in this series is a team of agents, steered by a few people (ten days to a data platform):

  • Conductors that take a brief and hand out the work, one per account.
  • A place agent for every town, keeping each place's file and its sources.
  • Data agents that collect the public sources, as code, and keep the register.
  • A security agent that reviews every change before and after, sweeps every repository hourly, and keeps a public log.
  • A behaviour-policy agent that writes and tightens a policy for every agent, 72 of them.
  • A librarian with its own team, and outreach that drafts every request for a person to send.
  • Designers and developers that build each new site.

What we sell

The code is open source and the data is public, so the platform is not what we sell: anyone can take it (governance from zero). What we sell is a team like this one, set up for a customer, with its roles, its policies and its security passes, running their sources, their sites and their workflows. AgentiCCells already offers it, and shows our own team as the example: "what we are going to do here is exactly what we want to sell" (our own team, run as a cell). The smallest version is a website cell; the largest is a network of teams like PrompTugal.news's (the website cell).

And it is the most practical demonstration of agent behaviour policies I know: dozens of agents, moving fast, each with a written policy, every change reviewed, and a person who can stop it all (what can your agent reach?).

Who I want to talk to

PrompTugal.news is no longer a small publication; it is a data set about places, with its sources and licences, that grows every day. What it needs now is use: people trying it, integrating with it, and paying for what it does for them. The people I want to talk to:

  • Councils and regional bodies: their own data, published openly, and a picture of their place that joins it to everyone else's.
  • Tourism and hospitality: what is on, the weather and the sea, for visitors, the way the holiday-homes demo shows (sites on the data).
  • Universities and research centres: a platform for their data and their students, like the heritage centre in the Algarve (HeritageTech, together).
  • Newsrooms and their associations: stories with their evidence, and money back to the sources (paid by the read).
  • The platforms we cannot read yet: to show their public data with credit, and pay them for it (whose event is it?).
  • Librarians, and the people who fund them (the librarian).
  • Anyone who needs to know what is happening in a place, insurers, estate agents, civil protection, investors.

Real integrations bring real traffic, and real traffic brings real budget and revenue. That is the next stage.

What this does not claim

  • No customer has bought a team like this yet; our own team is the example.
  • The list of people to talk to is a plan; nobody on it has been approached for this.
  • Prices are on agenticcells.ai, most of them first proposals.
