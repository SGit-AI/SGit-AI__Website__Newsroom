Start with an interview: how someone goes from "I want to try it" to editing their own site with Claude, by being interviewed by Claude first, about what they want, the sections, their materials and the sites they like, then sending their material in whichever way suits them, getting five designs inspired by what they like, or a better version of the site they already have, and reaching the one milestone that matters

By Dinis Cruz · 2026-11-18 · article v1.0.0 · newsroom v0.14.151 · agenticcellsonboardinginterviewswebsitesclaudeproductarticle

Abstract: When someone says "cool, I want to try it", what happens next decides whether they ever get a website. Today the first step on agenticcells.ai is an email with our questions in it. I think we can do something much better, and show the power of Claude at the same time: start with an interview. Claude interviews the person about what they want the site to do, the sections they would like, the materials and documents they have, and, the question I like most, which websites they like and why. The answers become the brief. The material can come in whatever way suits them: a zip, an email, links to what is already public, or their own encrypted vault; and the interviewing Claude can even be the one that starts building. The five designs are then inspired by the sites they chose, which is what already happens with our best ones. Someone with a site already can start from it instead, and get a better version back. And all of it is aimed at one milestone: the moment the person changes their own site by talking to Claude. That is when they have what we sell.

In short

  • The first step decides everything: today it is an email with questions.
  • Start with an interview instead, by Claude: what you want, sections, materials, sites you like.
  • The answers are the brief.
  • Material in any way that suits: a zip, an email, links, or a vault.
  • Five designs inspired by the sites you like.
  • Or start from the site you have, and get a better one back.
  • One milestone: you change your own site by talking to Claude.

Where to start

The website cell is a strong offer (the pitch), and it is aimed at a big market: people who want a website, and people who have one and are tired of it (static against the platforms). Both kinds hit the same moment: they say "cool, I want to try it", and then they have to do something.

Today, on agenticcells.ai, that something is an email: a short description in your own words, photos, videos, and a person replies. It is honest and it works, but it asks the person to know what to write. Most people do not know what they want from a website until someone asks them the right questions.

Start with an interview

So the first step should be an interview, by Claude. It also shows them what they are about to get: the same kind of intelligence that will edit their site is the one asking them about it. An interview covers five things:

  1. What the site is for: who should read it, and what they should do after: hire you, book you, buy from you, listen to you.
  2. The sections: what you want on it, a story, work, services, prices, a CV, a contact.
  3. What you have: photos, videos, documents, a CV, a portfolio, an existing profile or site.
  4. The sites you like, and why: three sites, yours or anyone's, and what you like in each, the colours, the type, the feeling.
  5. What to avoid, and what must stay private.

The interview ends with a brief the person can read and correct: one page, in their words.

Sites you like

The fourth question is the one I like most. People find it hard to describe a design, and easy to point at one. "I like this site's photographs, that one's calm, and the way this one tells a story" is a better design brief than most designers get. And it is already how our best sites happened: the five designs for Yiheng and Silvio were five different directions, and each person knew immediately which one was theirs (a website that is her, a portfolio with a film first).

So the five designs are inspired by the sites they chose: not copies, but the qualities they named, in five different readings.

Material, in any way that suits

Once the brief exists, the material can come in whichever way suits the person:

  • A zip of photos, documents and videos.
  • An email, to a person, as today.
  • Links to what is already public: a profile, a portfolio, an existing site.
  • Their own encrypted vault, filled over days, its key sealed to the factory, so private material never travels in clear (your own agentic cell).
  • The interview itself: the Claude that interviewed them can take the files there and then, and start.

That last one is the most seamless: the interviewing Claude can be the one that builds the first versions.

Or start from the site you have

Someone who already has a site has already answered most of the questions. Their site is the material: the words, the photos, the structure. The interview then asks what they would change, and the five designs are better versions of what they have. For a WordPress or Squarespace site, its export can carry everything across (static against the platforms).

The milestone

All of this is aimed at one moment: the person changes their own site by talking to Claude. A sentence, a photo, a CV, and the site is different, checked and live. Until then they have a website; from then on they have what we sell. So the hand-over is not the end of onboarding, it is the middle: the last step is their first change, made with them, and confirmed live.

The interview, as a prompt

To show how little it takes, this is the shape of an interview anyone could paste into Claude today:

Interview me, one question at a time, to write the brief for my website.
Cover: what the site is for and who should read it; the sections I want;
the photos, videos and documents I have; three websites I like and what
I like about each; what to avoid and what must stay private.
Then write the brief on one page, in my words, and ask me to correct it.

The real one, on agenticcells.ai, will carry our checks: what may be public, consent, and where the material goes next.

What I would build first

  • A "where to start" page that opens with the interview, and keeps the email as the alternative.
  • The brief as a file the person keeps, and sends with their material.
  • "Sites you like" as a required question, feeding the five designs.
  • An existing-site route, starting from a link.
  • The first change, made together, as the last step of every hand-over.

What this does not claim

  • This is a design, not yet built; today's first step is an email.
  • The vault route is part of a plan that is not built end to end.
  • "Inspired by" means the qualities people name, never copying someone else's site.
