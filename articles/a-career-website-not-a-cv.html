A friend who has just graduated

Tonight I talked to a friend who has just graduated and is looking for a job. It is very hard to stand out when you are applying with a CV, one among hundreds, often read by software first. I showed her Yiheng Wang's site, and she reacted the way everyone does: that looks really good.

It made something obvious. Most of the sites we have made so far, for Yiheng, for Silvio Switha and for others, are career sites: built, in the end, to help someone get the next job. That is a market, and a large one.

Two things they get

  • A better chance. Not a CV rewritten by a chatbot, but a site that is theirs, from their own photos, words and work, at their own address, in five designs to choose from (the website cell). It stands out because it is real.
  • Their first agentic workflow. For most people looking for a first job, this will be the first time they work with a team of agents: interviewed, shown designs, their site handed over to their own AI to keep changing (a website with a team inside). Each of them has friends looking for jobs too. That is how a mass market starts.

The workflow

  1. Give. An onboarding page that says exactly what we need: their CV, a few photos, links to their work, a short voice note about themselves, and the jobs they want. What they give is what makes the site good.
  2. A first pass, cheaply. The first version can be fairly plain: a template, or a variation of a site we have already made, so that it costs little and arrives fast.
  3. An interview. A conductor or a journalist agent asks what the CV does not say: what they are proud of, what they want next, the photos behind the projects. This is where the site gets its life.
  4. Five designs. From their real photos and words; they pick one, or mix them.
  5. An expert's review. Fifteen minutes from someone who works in the field they want to enter, turned into changes by agents (fifteen minutes of an expert).
  6. Theirs. Handed over, at their own address, changed by their own AI, with a version tuned for each role they apply for.

The address

Every site can live at name.sovereignwebsite.site from the first day, which is already a good address to put on an application. Those who want their own domain, their name or their name with .cv, can have it as an upgrade, registered in their own name (your own registrar).

Templates and examples

We need a set of examples to start from, for the first pass and for showing people what is possible: the real ones, with their owners' consent, and synthetic ones for the fields we have no real site in yet, a graduate in law, in nursing, in engineering, in design. Each becomes a template.

People, as well as agents

Agents make it scale; people make it better. My Claude is not better than anyone else's, but a designer knows what to ask, and a careers adviser knows what an employer looks for. As more professionals take part, the questions in the interview, the templates and the reviews all get better, and every site after benefits.

What this does not claim

  • The onboarding page, the templates and the career workflow are not built yet; the sites we have made are.
  • Prices for career sites are not set; the launch price in the appliance plan is £50 for a site.
  • A site does not guarantee a job, and no example is claimed to have got one.
