## Why now

When I show people the sites our agents made, for [Yiheng Wang](a-website-that-is-her.md), for [Silvio Switha](a-portfolio-with-a-film-first.md), for Gabriel, the answer to "do you want one of these?" is always yes. The factory that makes them is automated: a commit becomes a repository, an address, a certificate and a site ([the site factory works](the-site-factory-works.md)). So the next question is the commercial one: what is the unit, what does it cost, and who buys it? The AgentiCCells team rebuilt agenticcells.ai around that question today. This is my map of it.

## What a website cell is

One unit, standalone, with nothing of the customer's kept by us:

- **A GitHub repository, fully configured:** GitHub Pages, a live address at `<name>.agenticcells.site` or the person's own domain, HTTPS enforced.
- **CI on every change:** an agent check, build, validation, a leak scan, deploy and a version tag, with the version in the footer.
- **A CLAUDE.md written for its owner:** what the site is, how to change words and photos, the checks, what not to touch.
- **An agent behaviour policy:** what any agent working on the site may and may not do ([where assist becomes execute](where-assist-becomes-execute.md)).
- **A copy in an encrypted sgit vault,** so the site and its history travel with a key.

Setting all of that up by hand is not for the faint of heart: GitHub Pages with a custom domain, DNS, certificates, CI, the policy. Our factory does it in minutes.

## Five designs, by a team

Then the part I enjoy most. A team of agents goes to work on the person's photos, videos and description: a designer, a developer, a writer, a reviewer, working together. They make five designs, each different, and the person picks one, or mixes them. For Yiheng, five design agents worked side by side for about eleven minutes. A site that looks professional, with five designs to choose from, is, I think, the best embodiment of what Claude can do today, and especially Claude Design.

## Yours, with your own Claude

The most beautiful part is that the site belongs to its owner. It is in their repository, and they change it with their own Claude. Yiheng gave Claude her CV as a PDF; Claude read it, took what was there, added her latest experience to the site, and the site now carries her name as it is on her CV and LinkedIn. Her own agent took over within an hour of the hand-over ([your own agentic cell](your-own-agentic-cell.md)).

And because it is Claude, the site can do whatever Claude can connect to: a booking flow, a feed from another system, a page per project. The CLAUDE.md and the behaviour policy keep the owner's agent from doing damage: every change is a commit, the checks run before anything goes live, and anything can be undone. A person who knows what they are doing can override it; most owners will never want to.

That is the explorer and the town planner ([explorer coding](explorer-coding.md)): we hand the explorer a working site, they tell us, by using it, what they want, and the town planners build that into the factory.

## What it costs

- **The anchor: £500, one-off.** No subscription from us, ever, and no support: our job ends at the hand-over.
- **A launch price, still to decide:** the options I have in mind are £250, 20% off, or a low impulse price; agenticcells.ai's team proposes £250, labelled as a launch price. The price lives in one data file, so a change is one line.
- **Running it is nearly free:** GitHub Pages serves a static site with HTTPS, a custom domain included, at no charge; the owner's Claude subscription does the editing.

The AgentiCCells team also ran made-up buyers through the pages today, an artist, a musician, an AI technologist, a consultant, a company owner and a would-be partner. It is a simulation, not evidence of demand, but it is useful: at £500 one said yes outright; at £250, four said yes; at £50, five did, and one suspected something was missing. The question they all asked: who runs it after the hand-over?

## What else is out there

A website cell against AI builders, vibe-coding tools and a freelancer: price, who owns it, who changes it.

- **AI website builders:** Wix, with its Harmony AI editor, from about £9 to £25 a month for most plans; Squarespace, with Blueprint AI, from about $19 a month; Framer from $10; Webflow from $15; Hostinger from about £2.59 a month on a four-year term; Durable free on its own subdomain. All hosted: the site lives in their platform, and the common complaints are lock-in, no export, renewal price jumps and sites that look like "templates wearing a new coat of paint".
- **Vibe-coding tools:** Lovable at about $25 a month plus usage, Bolt.new at $25, v0 at $30, Replit at $20. The code can go to GitHub, but each needs its own subscription and credits, and users report credits draining fast.
- **A person:** a UK freelancer charges roughly £800 to £3,000 for a small site, an agency £2,500 to £10,000 or more, and running costs add 30 to 50%; Fiverr gigs start at $30 to $200; a personal brand site for a director on Contra starts at $1,500.

Against those, the website cell is one payment, a repository the owner keeps, no platform to be locked into, and an agent with written rules to keep it alive. Nobody else I found hands over a site with its own CLAUDE.md and behaviour policy. The website builder market is estimated at between about $3.6 billion and $6.4 billion in 2026, depending on who is counting.

What to learn from them: a free first step on a subdomain works (Durable, Carrd, Wix); bundling a domain works (Squarespace, GoDaddy); and Wix's builder now runs inside Google's Gemini, which says where people will make websites next: inside their assistant.

## Who already sells something close

Who to partner with and where to sell: outplacement, formation agents, portfolio platforms, marketplaces.

Partners sell and own the relationship; we automate and build; the revenue from the sale is split 50/50, and the partner can charge for support, changes and hosting on top. The places where a website already sits next to something else:

- **Outplacement and career coaching:** LHH's programmes produce a CV and LinkedIn profile, paid by the employer, with no website. A site that is the person, updated by their own Claude from their CV, fits exactly ([show me the evidence](show-me-the-evidence.md)).
- **Company formation agents:** a UK formation package already bundles a domain, with no site on it.
- **Creative portfolio platforms and link pages:** Adobe Portfolio, Dribbble, Linktree and Beacons, for artists and musicians who want something of their own.
- **Chambers of commerce and business networks:** member discounts on web development already exist.
- **Web freelancers themselves:** the partner who does not want to build, and does want a site to sell next week.

## Where to sell

- **Our own sites and examples first:** agenticcells.ai leads with the website cell, and every example is a demo with consent.
- **Marketplaces:** Fiverr (20% seller fee), Upwork (0 to 15%), Contra (no commission for freelancers), and LinkedIn's free Services Page.
- **Digital stores:** Gumroad (10% plus 50 cents) or Lemon Squeezy (5% plus 50 cents), with a claim code for the order ([keys after payment](keys-after-payment.md)).
- **Where builders are:** Product Hunt, Show HN, Indie Hackers, and Anthropic's plugin directory for the factory's skills, once they are ready.

## Why I want many

The point is not the websites. Every website cell is a set of agents working under a behaviour policy, in the open, for a real person: the most practical example of agent behaviour policies there is ([governance from zero](governance-from-zero.md)). Ten of them are interesting; a thousand are a market. All of it is open source, so anyone can do it themselves. Most will not want to, and that is what we sell.
