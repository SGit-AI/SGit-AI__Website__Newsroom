The future-of-news stack · provenance, corrections, payment

The story is a graph.
The article is a projection.

News is failing not because there is too little information but because there is no walkable chain from a claim to its evidence, no way for a correction to reach what it disproved, and no way to pay the person who did the original work. Make the editorial process public, make the citation chain typed and signed, and two things follow: corrections propagate, and facts become billable upstream. That is the future-of-news stack: sell the graph, not the paragraph.

Why corrections must propagate → A worked story: £8.40, 6h 23m → The published record, since Feb 2025 →

What is broken today

Most articles do not provide evidence, they provide a link. The link is never followed, and it could go to a site that no longer exists. There is no reference of what references what.

The 10,000-hours case. A 1993 study of violin students at a Berlin academy found the top group had practised roughly 10,000 hours by age 20 — an average, not a threshold; roughly half the top group had not reached it. The mechanism was deliberate practice, not accumulated time: one chess player reached master level in ~3,000 hours, another needed >20,000. Popularised in 2008, the number became a threshold in public memory. The original researcher called it "catchy rather than meaningful — it could as easily have been eleven thousand," and spent much of his career trying to correct it: books, articles, interviews, an open letter. None of it ever attached to the claim. The person best placed in the world to say that is not what my study showed had no channel, no standing at the point of use, and no economic reason to keep doing it beyond his own conviction.

The full story, and what a graph would have done differently →

The turn

In a document, a correction is a new document. Nothing that cited the original knows. In a graph, a correction is an edge — and the question how much of what I believe rests on claims that have since been corrected? becomes answerable. No document set can answer that. A graph answers it as a traversal. This is the inverse of how misinformation works today: currently a false claim propagates virally and the correction barely travels. In this model, the correction propagates with the same force as the original claim.

Corrections propagate

For an agent

newsroom.sgit.ai argues that a story is a graph and an article is one projection of it — and that an agent buying facts by API is the first worked example of its own product, not a hypothetical. Three things run — /portugal/, /databases/ and /governance/, each stating its own limits — and the rest is a design: read /shipped/ before citing anything here as a live capability. Every page that republishes or derives from previously published material carries a visible provenance block naming the original date and linking to the original — the site's own demonstration of the argument it makes.
