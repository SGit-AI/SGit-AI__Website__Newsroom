## In short {#in-short}

- **I wrote Generation Z Developers in 2018.** On Leanpub at 60%, and on Amazon.
- **Much of what I write now is in it.** Mostly as outlines: 90 of its 138 files.
- **The outlines have since been written.** As articles on this site.
- **So it is time for a second edition.** For the people I want to help into development and into a job.
- **Keep the name.** They are still Generation Z, and they are all developers now.
- **Publish it the way books from articles proposed.** With my handwritten notes, and a launch in Portugal.

## The book {#book}

[Generation Z Developers](https://leanpub.com/generation-z), subtitled "Key concepts and ideas for the next generation of developers", is on Leanpub at 60% complete, 136 pages and 30,695 words, last updated on 8 October 2018, with 465 readers. It is also on Amazon, and its source is on GitHub, in [Book_Generation_Z_Developer](https://github.com/DinisCruz/Book_Generation_Z_Developer). I wrote it the way it told readers to work: in Markdown, in Git, in public, published early and often, with GitHub issues for feedback.

It was a decent book. It was also full of spelling mistakes, and a lot of it was never finished.

## What it said {#said}

It opened with why I was worried about Generation Z, "the generation born after 1996": not their values, which I liked, but that, having grown up with technology that simply works, many had missed the history of how it came to work. And it had one chapter that I still give, almost word for word, to people looking for a job:

> The best way to get a job is to have ONE competitive advantage. One activity or task that you can do better than the person or company hiring you.

For a lot of companies in the 1990s, it said, that was simply using a computer, and in the 2000s, using the internet. Then the advice: "Act like you are part of the company", by collaborating on its open-source projects, which is [act as the job you want](/articles/act-as-the-job-you-want.html), eight years early.

On AI, it said: "One of the most important areas that you need to gain a strong understanding in the next 5 years is Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence (AI)." ChatGPT arrived four years later.

## What held, and what did not {#then-and-now}

**What held.** The competitive-advantage argument is exactly [hired for the workflow](/articles/hired-for-the-workflow.html): today the one advantage is running agentic workflows. Act like you are part of the company. Publish, publish, publish. GitHub as your CV. Write for your future self. Pioneers, settlers and town planners, which became [software vendors as villagers](/articles/software-vendors-as-villagers.html) and [explorer coding](/articles/explorer-coding.html).

**What did not.** The book's list of technologies to learn, Jira, WallabyJs, AWS's machine-learning services, has dated, as such lists do. Its AI chapter pointed readers at cloud machine-learning tools to learn "how to program it"; the bigger shift turned out to be learning how to use it, and how to direct agents, which is a different skill from building models. And its view of who is a developer was narrower than it should have been: today anyone who can brief an agent to build something is one.

## The outlines have been written {#outlines}

The most interesting thing in the repository is what is not finished. Of its 138 files, 90 are outlines: a title, "Topics to cover and ideas", and a few bullet points. Life patterns: 21 of 27 are outlines. Concepts: 26 of 27. Security: all 8. Your CV: 8 of 9.

Reading them now, many are briefs that this newsroom has since written as articles, without my planning it. "Future self", whose outline says to create assets that will be useful for him or her, is [bringing my past work back](/articles/bringing-my-past-work-back.html). "Publish, publish, publish" is [your own newsroom](/articles/your-own-newsroom.html). "Graphs" is [the graphs books](/articles/the-graphs-books.html) and [search as navigation](/articles/search-as-navigation.html). "GitHub is your CV" is act as the job you want. Even chapters that were written have newer answers: the one on open source now has [the fork next door](/articles/the-fork-next-door.html). That is the material for a second edition, and it is why I feel I now have enough to have a go.

## The name {#name}

The first question is whether to call it Generation Z, or something like Generation AI Developers. I think Generation Z Developers is still right, for two reasons. The people I want to give it to, those coming out of university or looking for their first or next job, are still Generation Z: the oldest were 22 when I wrote it, and are 30 now. And the play on the name works better now than it did: they are all developers. With agents, everyone who can describe what they want built is one.

So: keep the title, and let the subtitle carry the change, something like *Generation Z Developers, second edition: how to get into development, and get a job, with agents*.

## The second edition {#edition}

- **Keep what held,** corrected and finished, and the spelling fixed.
- **Say what did not,** in a short "then and now" for each chapter that changed.
- **Fill each outline** from the newsroom articles that answer it, rewritten for this reader.
- **Add a part on working with agents:** [explorer coding](/articles/explorer-coding.html), your own newsroom, [the man behind the curtain](/articles/the-man-behind-the-curtain.html), hired for the workflow, act as the job you want.
- **Add my handwritten notes.** As with the other books, I have analogue notes for this one that I am happy to scan and publish alongside it.

## How, and when {#how}

The method is the one in [books from articles](/articles/books-from-articles.html): map the chapters to articles, write the missing ones here first, assemble, and publish on Amazon KDP. I already have a KDP account and have published there before, so the shorter review for accounts with a published book applies. And this may be the right book to launch in Portugal: it is the one I would most like to hand to people starting out, and I will be in Lisbon for Web Summit, 9 to 12 November.

It is the book I would give, literally, to the people I am trying to help: here is the manual for getting into development, and here is the manual for getting a job.

The build brief is a note for the agent that makes it: [Generation Z Developers, second edition](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/admin/notes/2026-10-18__generation-z-developers-second-edition.md). It is also on [the briefs page](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/briefs/index.html#generation-z-developers-2e).
