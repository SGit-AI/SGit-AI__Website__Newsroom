## In short {#in-short}

- **One sentence is the whole offer,** and it is six promises.
- **Each promise has a proof** we can already show.
- **Three designs,** each telling it in a different order and voice.
- **"Six promises":** the sentence as the headline, for anyone.
- **"Just say it":** a conversation with Claude, for technologists.
- **"Stop renting your website":** owning against renting, for people leaving a platform.
- **My recommendation:** the first on the home page, the other two for their audiences.

## The sentence {#sentence}

In [static against the platforms](/articles/static-against-the-platforms.html) I ended with this: "a site that looks better, that you own, that nobody can lock you into, that has almost nothing to attack, that costs once instead of every month, and that you change by talking to Claude." Reading it back, I think it is the pitch. It says what the website cell is ([the website cell](/articles/the-website-cell.html)) without a single word of our jargon, no cell, no vault, no policy, and each clause answers a real complaint people have about the site they have now.

## Six promises, and the proof {#promises}

1. **Looks better.** Five designs, made for you by a team of agents, a designer, a developer, a researcher, a security reviewer and a conductor; you pick one. The proof is the sites themselves.
2. **You own it.** It lives in your own GitHub repository, with its full history. Nothing of yours stays with us.
3. **Nobody can lock you in.** It is static files, so it moves to any host: GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, any web host. A Wix site cannot be moved off Wix, and Squarespace exports only part of a site.
4. **Almost nothing to attack.** No server code, no database, no plugins, no cookies unless you add them. The WordPress ecosystem had 11,334 new vulnerabilities in 2025, 91% of them in plugins, as counted by Patchstack, a security vendor.
5. **Costs once.** £250 at the launch price, usually £500, one-off; hosting on GitHub Pages is free; and we build a limited number of free evaluation sites every day. The platforms charge every month, and several raised their prices this year.
6. **You change it by talking to Claude.** "Here is my CV as a PDF, add my latest job", and the site is updated, the way Yiheng's was ([a website that is her](/articles/a-website-that-is-her.html)). With guard rails: a behaviour policy, checks before anything goes live, and every change undoable.

## Three ways to tell it {#designs}

I asked a designer agent for three sites built on the pitch and nothing else, each a different look and, more importantly, a different order. The order is the message: what you say first is what the reader believes the offer is.

### Six promises {#a}

!shot pch-a.webp | images/ | Six promises: the pitch sentence is the headline, one clause per line, the first promise in colour. | screenshot | medium

Light, clean and confident. The headline is the sentence itself, one clause per line. Below it, the six promises in the sentence's own order, each a card with its proof: the five designs, the repository in your name, the list of hosts against Wix and Squarespace, the vulnerability count, £250 against a monthly bill, and a short exchange with Claude. Nothing is reframed; it reads as a complete offer you can check. Best for first-time site owners and small businesses, and the safest default for a general audience. The button: "Apply for a free evaluation site."

### Just say it {#b}

!shot pch-b.webp | images/ | Just say it: the owner hands Claude a CV, Claude reports its checks, and a before and after shows the site changed. | screenshot | medium

Dark and technical. The headline is "Just say it. Your site changes.", and the hero is a conversation: the owner sends Claude a CV, Claude reports what it found and the checks it ran, the owner says publish, and a before and after shows the new job on the site. The order follows the questions that conversation raises: talking to Claude first, then the guard rails, because "an AI edits my site" makes people ask about safety; then the designs, the attack surface, lock-in, ownership, and the price last. Best for technologists, founders and anyone who hates page builders. The line for LinkedIn: "Here is my CV as a PDF, add my latest job. That is the whole edit."

### Stop renting your website {#c}

!shot pch-c.webp | images/ | Stop renting your website: owning against renting, with the pitch as a pull quote. | screenshot | medium

Editorial and warm, with serif headings. The headline is "Stop renting your website.", the pitch sits beside it as a quote, and the first section is a ledger: every month, forever, or once. Lock-in and the attack surface come next, as the hidden costs of renting; the designs and Claude come last, as what you get once you own it. It counts payments rather than inventing platform prices. Best for people leaving WordPress, Wix or Squarespace, and small businesses tired of subscriptions and plugin updates. The line: "Your website should not be a subscription."

## What I would do {#recommend}

- **"Six promises" on the home page:** it is the whole offer, for everyone, and it is the sentence.
- **"Just say it" for technologists:** the page I send to people in AI and software, and the one for LinkedIn.
- **"Stop renting your website" for people on a platform:** the page for anyone leaving WordPress, Wix or Squarespace, and the natural home for the converter, when it exists.
- **All three for partners:** a freelancer, a consultancy or a firm can send whichever fits their customer ([you bring the business, we build the site](/articles/you-bring-the-business.html)).

I have passed the three designs and their messaging to the agent team that runs agenticcells.ai; how and whether to use them is theirs and mine to decide there.

## What this does not claim {#limits}

- **These are mock-ups,** made by a designer agent today, not live pages.
- **The conversation in "Just say it" is an illustration,** with an invented job.
- **Nothing has been sold yet;** the examples are demos, named with consent.
- **The vulnerability count is a vendor's,** Patchstack's.
