## In short {#in-short}

- **Markus Franz's point:** not an agent, a desk; one front door; a person signs off.
- **His question:** who hands out the work, a person or another agent?
- **Our answer: both,** at different moments.
- **A person sets the work and owns it;** one conductor agent hands it out.
- **Specialists and a reviewer do it;** a person decides what goes out.
- **One door is easier to govern,** and it should also be able to close.

## The post {#post}

!shot mf-question.webp | images/ | Markus Franz of ippen.ai on Ippen Digital's editorial team of agents, LinkedIn, November 2026. | medium | aside

Markus Franz, who leads the incubator lab at ippen.ai, wrote: "We didn't build an agent. We built a desk." Editors at Ippen Digital brief one Editorial Team in a chat, which works like a duty editor: it takes the request, decides which specialists are needed, hands out the work and brings back one answer. Twelve specialists, eight of them in three small teams, for search, research, publishing, audience data and SEO. Before the team, every call to a specialist came from a person; from the week after launch, nine in ten came from the team. "An agent may draft, a person decides what goes out."

Two lessons in it I recognise exactly. Rules are easier to hold when most of the work comes in through one door, so they made the team the default way in. And they never closed the direct route: people still ask single specialists about as often as before, and the team's work came on top. He ends: "If you run more than one agent: who hands out the work, a person or another agent?"

## Our answer: both {#both}

For ten days around Web Summit, six of us (Dinis Cruz, Angelo Leggio, Vicky Wang, Silvio Switha, José Bota and Indi Gupte) ran around 250 named agents, more than 60 at once ([ten days at Web Summit](/articles/ten-days-at-web-summit.html)). PrompTugal.news, the newsroom we ran there, had desks of journalists, a reviewer and a curator ([PrompTugal.news, the newsroom we ran](/articles/promptugal-news-the-newsroom-we-ran.html)). This newsroom works the same way: my voice notes go to a desk of named agents, and every article passes gates before it is released.

The answer that emerged is that work is handed out twice:

1. **A person sets the work.** A voice note, a brief, a decision. The person owns the outcome and the direction.
2. **One conductor agent hands it out.** It is the one door: it reads the board, decides which specialists are needed, briefs them, and collects their reports ([how an agent team briefs its people](/articles/how-an-agent-team-briefs-its-people.html)).
3. **Specialists do the work,** each with a role, a brief and a behaviour policy that says what it may touch.
4. **A reviewer checks it,** a separate agent that checks every claim against its source.
5. **A person decides what goes out.** Agents draft; they never send or post.

## One door {#door}

We learned Markus's lesson too. The person on our team who ran one of the largest agent teams, with no technical background, put the rule in one line in her interview: start with one conductor and only talk to that one; let it hand out the work ([don't micromanage your agents](/articles/dont-micromanage-your-agents.html)). She stopped trying to read everything, and started to manage.

And, as at Ippen, the direct route stayed open. I still work directly with a specialist when the job is mine to steer, and the conductor's work comes on top.

## A door that can close {#close}

One thing we would add to a front door: a way to close it. When I needed to stop every agent across six people's accounts, the pausing took seconds, but each conductor only heard the order at its next wake, and some agents never read it at all. The fix was a file every agent reads first, before any work: a HOLD ([stopping a hundred agents](/articles/stopping-a-hundred-agents.html)). One door makes the rules easier to hold; a file at the door makes the stop easier to hold too.

## What this does not claim {#limits}

- **Ippen's figures and design are Markus's;** I quote only his post.
- **Our numbers are from our own records** of the ten days, published in the series.
- **Our conductors are agents with policies,** not a product; each person ran their own.
