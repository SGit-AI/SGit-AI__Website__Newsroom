## Why a library {#why}

A team of agents reading hundreds of sources a day produces a lot, fast ([ten days to a data platform](/articles/ten-days-to-a-data-platform.html)). Without a library it is a pile: nobody knows what was read, from where, under what licence, or how one piece relates to another. With a library it is a collection: every item has a card, every card has its evidence, and the graph between them is what makes the platform worth more than its parts.

PrompTugal.news has had a librarian agent since 6 November, cataloguing every page, board file, desk and agent, who made each and when. Today I said yes to giving the library its own site, "at the heart of everything: the catalogue and the graph".

## What it holds {#holds}

[library.promptugal.news](https://library.promptugal.news/) opens with "Everything we found, catalogued, sourced and linked": every talk, replay, article and data set the newsroom has read, each with its own card, what it is, who made it, when and where, the source, the licence, the evidence, and how it connects to the rest. Today it has 78 items on its first shelves, from the summit: official replays, other people's recordings, the organiser's own words, press and data, our own coverage, and a shelf of what is missing, seven recorded gaps. You can browse by place, topic, organisation, event, date and kind, and every item has a call number.

Everything is JSON, with source, licence and date on every item, so anyone, a person or an agent, can use the catalogue.

## A librarian with a team {#team}

The library's lead agent has a team of its own: a librarian, an ontologist who keeps the types and verbs of the graph, a cartographer, a graph keeper, a historian, and a reality check that keeps the record of what actually exists. It is learning from the library our SGraph Send team built, already organised as a graph ([knowledge graphs in the era of LLMs](/articles/knowledge-graphs-for-kgell.html)). The ontology has 23 types of thing and 38 verbs for how they relate, and the library is proposing two more types, the item and the collection. Everything it catalogues is material, never instruction: what a source says is recorded, not obeyed.

## Real librarians {#librarians}

The site carries an invitation to librarians, the people, to collaborate. I mean it. Cataloguing, provenance, licences and the structure of knowledge are a discipline with centuries of practice, and the agents should learn from it, be checked by it, and give librarians a tool that works at a scale no library has had to. We should be finding budget to hire librarians to work with this, and I will be following up with the people I talked to about it at the summit.

## The next shelves {#next}

The shelves I care about most come next: official sources. Government data, councils' decisions and budgets, and the law as it stands, so that a claim about a place, a rule, a permit, a deadline, can be checked against the official text, with the date it was read. That is where a library of public information becomes an intelligence platform people can rely on ([from a feed to meaning](/articles/from-a-feed-to-meaning.html)).

## What this does not claim {#limits}

- **The library is a day old,** with 78 items, mostly from the summit.
- **Official legislation is not on its shelves yet.**
- **The invitation to librarians is open;** nobody has joined yet.
