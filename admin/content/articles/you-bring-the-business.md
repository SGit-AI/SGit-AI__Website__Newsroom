## In short {#in-short}

- **The partner programme is live** on agenticcells.ai, built overnight by its agent team.
- **A tagline:** "you bring the business, we build the site".
- **One page per kind of partner,** each standing alone, one link per person.
- **Four figures on each:** the fit, the flow, who does what, the money.
- **£250 launch price,** against £500, and free evaluation sites every day.
- **Partner terms published,** not a draft.
- **Five designs on every page, ten languages** from day one.

## From article to site in a night {#night}

Yesterday evening I wrote the partner programme here ([partners sell, we build](/articles/partners-sell-we-build.html)) and the case for the website cell ([the website cell](/articles/the-website-cell.html)), and sent both to the conductor of the AgentiCCells team, the agents that run agenticcells.ai. That is the workflow I want: the newsroom thinks it through in public, and the team that owns the site implements it.

Within minutes the conductor had routed it to its writer and ambassador agents. By 22:09 there was a partners page; by 22:59, one page per kind of partner; by 23:08, four figures on each, drawn by the designer agent; by 23:16, partners in the main navigation, with a section on the home page. Along the way I added three things by voice: the launch price, ten languages, and one page per partner.

## You bring the business, we build the site {#tagline}

The team wrote a line on the partners page that I liked so much I asked for it on the home page: "Partners: you bring the business, we build the site." It is the whole programme. The partner knows people who need a website; we have an agent team that builds one from photos, videos and a short description, and hands it over in a GitHub repository the customer owns.

## One page per partner {#pages}

Each kind of partner has its own page, which stands alone, so I can send a freelancer one link and a firm another:

- **[Freelancer](https://agenticcells.ai/partners/freelancer/):** "sell a website, an agentic email and a workflow", one kind of customer, one flow, more of it.
- **[Consultancy](https://agenticcells.ai/partners/consultancy/):** "one cell for each client, the same team every time".
- **[Firm](https://agenticcells.ai/partners/firm/):** "bigger projects, with our orchestration and a network of experts", case by case.

Each page carries a worked example, from the first conversation to the hand-over and after, labelled as an example, because no deal has run yet.

## Four figures on every page {#figures}

!shot pab-fit.webp | images/ | How the parts fit: the partner, the factory's agent team under behaviour policies, the customer's own GitHub and Claude. | diagram | medium

The first figure shows how it fits together: the partner brings the customer and the material; the factory's agents, a conductor, a researcher, a designer, a developer and a security reviewer, work under behaviour policies from RiskMandate; and the site lands in the customer's own accounts, the repository in their GitHub with its history, and Claude to manage it afterwards.

!shot pab-lanes.webp | images/ | Who does what, in four lanes: the partner, AgentiCCells, the agent team under policies, and the customer. | diagram | medium

The second is the deal in six steps; the third, who does what in four lanes, the partner, AgentiCCells, the agent team and the customer, with the note that each agent signs and logs its own work.

!shot pab-money.webp | images/ | The money: whoever has the customer invoices; 50/50 of the sale; hosting, support and retainers stay with the partner. | diagram | medium

And the fourth is the money: the customer pays for the site, whoever has the customer invoices, the sale is split 50/50 when we are involved, and hosting, support, retainers, subscriptions, changes and installation are the partner's, entirely, at their own prices.

## £250, and free evaluations {#price}

The anchor stays at £500. The launch price is £250, labelled as a launch price that may change. And because the best way to see this is to have one, we also build a limited number of sites every day, free, for evaluation: anyone can apply by email. It is the escape valve for the person who wants to see before they pay, and for the partner who wants to show a customer something real.

## Partner terms, not a draft {#terms}

I asked for the terms to be published as terms. They say what I wrote: 50/50 of the sale when we orchestrate; larger projects case by case; whoever has the customer invoices; the customer owns the site and the material; the partner owns the relationship; no subscriptions and no support from us; our code and policies are open source, and partners may keep their own work private. The payment timing stays a proposal, agreed for each deal.

## Five designs and ten languages {#designs}

Two more things I asked for last night. Every page now has the same design layer as the sites we make for people, with a dark top bar and a "choose a design" button: Gallery, Calm, Editorial, Console and Proof, the same words in each. And the whole site is in ten languages: English, Portuguese for Portugal and for Brazil, French for France and for Canada, Spanish for Spain and for Mexico, German, Simplified Chinese and Japanese. A partner programme that works in any country should be readable in that country's language from the first day, and agents make that cheap.

## What happens now {#now}

This week I start sending the links, to the freelancers and companies I already have in mind. Each will get the page for their kind of partner. The first ones will shape the terms with me.

## What this does not claim {#limits}

- **Nothing has been sold and no partner has signed,** as every page says.
- **The translations are agent-made** and not yet reviewed by native speakers.
- **The worked examples are examples,** not records of deals.
