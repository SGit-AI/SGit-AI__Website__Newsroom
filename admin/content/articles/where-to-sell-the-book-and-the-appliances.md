## In short {#in-short}

- **The printed book:** early December; [the ebook is on Leanpub](https://leanpub.com/one-human-nine-agents-nineteen-hours) now.
- **The appliances:** Kickstarter from 1 to 31 December, general sale from 1 January 2027.
- **For print pre-orders, IngramSpark,** not KDP, which has none for print.
- **For the appliances, our own shop first,** then late pledges, eBay, Amazon and OnBuy.
- **Selling hardware under our own name** makes us its manufacturer in law.

## The two products and their dates {#dates}

The book is [One Human, Nine Agents, Nineteen Hours](/articles/the-book-is-on-leanpub.html), on Leanpub as an ebook since 14 November; the print edition should be ready in about two weeks, once the final touches are done. The appliances are the ones in [the Kickstarter plan](/articles/launching-the-sovereign-website-appliance.html): a sovereign website on a Raspberry Pi or a tablet.

A one-month Kickstarter, 1 to 31 December, is within the rules: a campaign can run from one to 60 days, Kickstarter recommends 30 or fewer, and the end date and time can be set exactly ([project duration](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005128434-What-is-the-maximum-project-duration)). The payout begins 14 days after the deadline, so about 14 January for a 31 December end ([receiving funds](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010120934-If-my-project-is-successfully-funded-how-do-I-receive-my-funds)). General sale from 1 January follows.

## The printed book {#book}

- **Leanpub sells ebooks only,** but can export a print-ready PDF for print-on-demand services ([Leanpub on print books](https://help.leanpub.com/en/articles/110755-does-leanpub-sell-print-books), [print-ready PDFs](https://help.leanpub.com/en/articles/110525-can-i-use-leanpub-to-create-print-ready-pdfs-for-third-party-print-on-demand-sites-like-lulu-and-amazon)).
- **Amazon, through Kindle Direct Publishing,** does not offer pre-orders for paperbacks or hardcovers; a new print book can be given a release date up to 90 days ahead, and its page stays hidden until then ([KDP on pre-orders](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GDTMGBCWHAY35NK4)). On Amazon.co.uk the royalty is 60% of a list price of £7.99 or more, less the cost of printing ([KDP print royalties](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330)).
- **IngramSpark** can set a publication date up to a year ahead, which opens pre-order pages at retailers including Amazon and Bookshop.org ([on-sale dates](https://www.ingramspark.com/blog/on-sale-date-for-book-marketing)), and distributes in the UK to Amazon.co.uk, Blackwell's, Foyles, Gardners and Waterstones ([IngramSpark distribution](https://www.ingramspark.com/how-it-works/distribute)). Setup is free.
- **An ISBN of our own** is needed for that route: from Nielsen, £93 for one, £174 for ten ([Nielsen ISBN store](https://www.nielsenisbnstore.com/Home/Isbn)).
- **Signed copies** can be sold from our own shop.

One caution: KDP advises against using its scheduled release while another service runs a pre-order for the same book, so it is one route or the other for each edition.

## The appliances, during the campaign {#campaign}

Nowhere but Kickstarter. Kickstarter does not accept "offering something that's already available somewhere else" as a project's end goal, and its rules also exclude rewards the creator did not make ([what Kickstarter can fund](https://help.kickstarter.com/en-us/articles/16236681-can-kickstarter-be-used-to-fund-anything)). The second rule matters here: the Pi kits and tablets are bought in, so the campaign has to present what is new, the setup, the site, the agents and the local model, as the work, with a working prototype on video and no photorealistic renders ([hardware rules](https://help.kickstarter.com/en-us/articles/16236428-what-are-the-rules-for-hardware-and-product-design-projects)). If the product reaches the public before backers receive theirs, the creator has to say so openly ([offering rewards elsewhere](https://help.kickstarter.com/en-us/articles/16236648-the-creator-is-offering-rewards-elsewhere-before-shipping-to-backers)).

## The appliances, from 1 January {#after}

Best fit first:

1. **Our own shop,** on sovereignwebsite.ai: no marketplace rules, and Stripe at 1.5% plus 20p on UK cards ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/gb/pricing)). It is also the sovereign answer.
2. **Kickstarter's late pledges,** which have to be switched on before the campaign ends ([late pledges](https://updates.kickstarter.com/understanding-late-pledges-a-comprehensive-guide-for-kickstarter-creators/)).
3. **eBay UK:** for business sellers, 9.9% in computers, tablets and networking, 6.9% on tablets up to £1,000, plus 40p an order ([eBay business fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/business-selling-fees?id=4809)).
4. **Amazon UK:** a Professional plan at £25 a month, and a 7% referral fee in computers and consumer electronics ([Amazon UK pricing](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing)). A bundle needs its own barcode, and Brand Registry needs a trademark ([Brand Registry](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/brand-registry/)). Amazon in the US has announced that from 11 January 2027 most bundles must be packaged by the brand itself; whether the UK follows is not confirmed, so plan to sell it as our own branded product.
5. **OnBuy:** 7% on consumer electronics, with plans from £25 a month ([OnBuy fees](https://www.onbuy.com/gb/sell/selling-fees/)).

Not a fit: Etsy, whose rules exclude resold electronics ([what can I sell on Etsy](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360024112614-What-Can-I-Sell-on-Etsy)); and Not On The High Street, which is built for gifts. Tindie, a marketplace for maker hardware, is a good audience, but limits resold items and takes no pre-orders, from what we could read. The Pi Hut and Pimoroni do not list other sellers' products; they are conversations for later, as partners.

## What selling hardware brings with it {#law}

- **We become the manufacturer.** Selling a product under our own name, or changing one in a way that affects its compliance, makes us its manufacturer in law, responsible for its CE or UKCA marking; Great Britain still accepts CE marking ([using the UKCA marking](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/using-the-ukca-marking)).
- **WEEE:** a producer placing under five tonnes of electrical equipment a year registers directly, by 31 January or within 28 days of the first sale ([EEE producer responsibility](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/electrical-and-electronic-equipment-eee-producer-responsibility)), and an online seller can meet its take-back duty by joining the Distributor Takeback Scheme ([the scheme](https://www.gov.uk/electricalwaste-producer-supplier-responsibilities/join-the-distributor-takeback-scheme)).
- **The Raspberry Pi name:** it can describe the genuine part inside, but cannot be in our product's name without a licence, must be less prominent than our own brand, and every use needs the line "Raspberry Pi is a trademark of Raspberry Pi Ltd" ([trademark rules](https://www.raspberrypi.com/trademark-rules/)). Its "Powered by Raspberry Pi" programme is the route to apply for.

## The EU, next {#eu}

From the UK, appliances over €150 travel with a full customs declaration, and import VAT and any duty are collected on arrival unless sold duty-paid ([EU guidance on low-value consignments](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2022-01/guidance_on_import_and_export_of_low_value_consignments_en.pdf)). Under the EU's General Product Safety Regulation, a product needs a responsible person established in the EU, named on every listing ([the regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj/eng)); industry guidance says the same applies to printed books. That is a step for after the UK launch.
