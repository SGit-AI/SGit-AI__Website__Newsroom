## In short {#in-short}

- **The cell is the unit:** one focused job, with an objective, a deliverable and a behaviour policy.
- **As many parts as the job needs:** a Claude session, a vault, a repository, a website, lanes, a schedule, a connector, a person.
- **Every website is a cell,** and so is Claude replying to email.
- **Every cell gets a page** that sells it: what it does, examples, use cases, its policy, and four ways to start.
- **The first way is free:** the recipe. Then our agent helps, we help, or we set it up.
- **The catalogue grows one need at a time,** often in one industry's own words.
- **We live in cells first:** the team, and my own Claude work.

## Why the cell {#why}

[AgentiCCells](/articles/the-intersection.html) today explains itself by size: a ladder from one agent to a network of teams, the six setups of an agentic system, two modes of who runs the team ([six setups of an agentic system](/articles/six-setups-of-an-agentic-system.html)). That is right, and it is how a buyer understands the price. But it is not how a buyer thinks about the problem. Nobody wakes up wanting "a few sessions with lanes and a vault". They want help replying to email, keeping a website, following up after an event.

So the cell itself should be at the centre: the unit of what we build, what we sell, and how we work. We need to think hard about what a cell looks like, how it is sold, what the customer is buying, how it is set up, and how it actually works day to day.

## The anatomy of a cell {#anatomy}

!shot cel-anatomy.webp | images/ | The anatomy of a cell: an objective, a deliverable and a behaviour policy always; other parts as the job needs. | concept

Every cell has three things it cannot do without:

- **An objective:** the one job it exists for, said in a sentence the customer would use.
- **A deliverable:** what you have at the end of each day, week or run: drafted replies, a live site, a debrief, a list of what waits on you.
- **A behaviour policy:** what it may reach, what it was asked to do, and what stands in its way ([where assist becomes execute](/articles/where-assist-becomes-execute.html)).

And then as many parts as its job needs: a Claude session to do the work; a vault for its memory, files and record ([how a vault works](/articles/how-a-vault-works.html)); a repository and CI; a website on its own subdomain; lanes and keys to talk, sign and approve; a schedule of wakes; a connector to email, a calendar, Odoo or a CRM; and a person who owns it.

That range is the point. A cell can be as small as one Claude session replying to email under a policy, or as rich as a team keeping a website with a factory behind it ([the site factory works](/articles/the-site-factory-works.html)). Every website is a cell. Now that the factory creates repositories and changes AWS from a commit, cells can carry capabilities that were too painful to set up before.

## A page for every cell {#page}

!shot cel-page.webp | images/ | A page for every cell: what it does, what you get, examples, use cases, its policy, and four ways to start. | screenshot

Selling has to be as focused as the cell. Every cell gets its own page, built the same way:

- **What it does,** in one line, and **what you get,** concretely.
- **Examples:** real ones, from our own work and our customers', with links.
- **Use cases:** the situations where people reach for it.
- **Its behaviour policy,** in plain words: what it can reach, what it will never do.

And, on the side, four ways to start:

1. **Free:** the recipe. The brief, the policy and the steps to run it yourself, with your own Claude. Most people should start here, and many will stay here, which is fine: it drives usage.
2. **Our agent helps you:** a specialist agent reviews your setup and tells you what to fix ([specialist agents for every team](/articles/specialist-agents-for-every-team.html)).
3. **We help you:** two sessions with a person, on the ladder at £1,500.
4. **We set it up for you:** in your accounts or ours, from £500 ([atomic cells, end to end](/articles/atomic-cells-end-to-end.html)).

## A catalogue that grows one need at a time {#catalogue}

The catalogue grows the simplest possible way. Someone says: I need help with this. I need help sending these emails. I need help keeping this page current. And we add a cell for it. That is the workflow now: find the use case, write the cell, give it a page.

Some cells are general: reply to your inbox, follow up after an event, a weekly digest, a website you steer, translate your site, debrief your meetings, a CRM of your contacts, a policy for your agent, a vault for a project. Many will be specific to one industry, and that is good. A clinic's cell drafts patient letters in plain words for a clinician to approve; a farm's takes orders by email and keeps a page of what is in season; a council's answers the same questions every week, with the sources. The cell is the same underneath; its page, its examples and its words belong to that industry, and it connects to that industry's systems ([integrate with whatever is on the other side](/articles/integrate-with-whatever-is-on-the-other-side.html)).

## Live in cells first {#ourselves}

The best test of whether cells are easy to use is to use them ourselves, every day. Our team should start thinking in cells: what is this piece of work, what is its objective, its deliverable, its policy? And I should move my own Claude work into cells. Today much of it is sessions that come and go; as cells, each would come packaged with its brief, its policy, its vault and its record, and each would become an example on a page.

The same logic as the CRM applies: what we use ourselves gets better fastest, because the feedback loop is ours ([a CRM for us and for our agents](/articles/a-crm-for-us-and-our-agents.html)). Every cell we run becomes a page, a recipe and a proof.

## What to build next {#next}

1. **A cell format:** a folder with the objective, the deliverable, the behaviour policy, the recipe and the parts, the same for every cell.
2. **The page template** for agenticcells.ai, with the four ways to start, generated from that folder.
3. **The first ten cells,** from what we already do: reply to your inbox, follow up after an event, a website you steer, debrief your meetings, draft bills in Odoo, a policy for your agent, a vault for a project, a weekly digest, a page per place, CI behaviour policies.
4. **Our own work in cells:** each of us moves one recurring piece of work into a cell this week.
5. **A request form:** "I need help with...", into an agent's lane, which proposes the next cell.

## What this does not claim {#limits}

- **The page and catalogue are mock-ups;** the cells exist today as pieces of our work, not yet as a catalogue.
- **Prices follow the AgentiCCells ladder,** which its site calls first proposals.
- **The industry cells are examples,** not offers with customers yet.
