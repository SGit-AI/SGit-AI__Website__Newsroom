## The suggestion {#suggestion}

After the [Vibathon Algarve 2026](/articles/vibathon-algarve-2026.html), someone in the event's group chat suggested that it would be nice to have a quick pitch deck, or presentation, for each of the projects people worked on during the day. I agreed, and replied with a link to the presentation of my own talk: "we could do one of those presentations (with dynamic infographics) for each of those projects and MVPs delivered during the event".

## We already have every piece {#pieces}

Two days earlier, on 29 October, I gave [the ultimate insider](https://the-ultimate-insider.presentation.sgit.ai/) at hack::soho. Its presentation was [built in a day](/articles/the-ultimate-insider-case-study.html): from an empty repository at 00:16 to the stage at 19:00, by nine agents and one editor, with its own site, its own navigation and infographics that move. Overnight, two more agent teams worked on [the book of the talk, with a companion for reviewers](/articles/the-book-of-the-talk-and-its-companion.html), and that book, which we are still writing, already explains much of how the workflow runs.

Everything a project presentation needs is there: a repository to hold the material, a team of agents with roles, a researcher that checks every claim, a designer that draws infographics, a writer, a review step, and a site per presentation.

## What one prompt already does {#prompt}

!shot pd-reading-map.webp | images/ | ChatGPT's reading map of the same six articles, with each article's address. | small

This afternoon I asked ChatGPT for infographics of the six articles we wrote today about Algarve Evolution, and it made the two in this article: one shows the six as a connected ecosystem, the other is a reading map with each article's address. They are not perfect, a word is misspelt in the small print and the people are illustrations, but they are a good picture of what even one prompt can do with good material. A project from a build day is good material.

## A presentation for every project {#workflow}

!shot pd-flow.webp | images/ | A presentation for every project: from what a team built on the day to a site that explains it. | process

The workflow, applied to a build day:

1. **The project:** what a team built on the day, the app, its notes, and a short voice memo of the pitch.
2. **A repository** that holds the app and its record together, public, with the team's permission.
3. **An agent team:** a researcher that checks what the project claims, a designer that draws the infographics, a writer that drafts the story, and a review before anything is shown.
4. **The presentation:** a site of its own that tells the story, explains it with dynamic infographics, embeds the app, and sources every number.
5. **Shared:** a link for the show and tell, an article for the newsroom, an infographic to print.

Done during the afternoon, every project can be presented at the show and tell; done overnight, every team wakes up to a presentation of its own.

## It shows the power of agentic workflows {#power}

A good demonstration of agentic workflows is not a talk about them. It is a presentation, made by agents, of what the participants themselves built that day. The participants see their own work explained back to them, and they see, in the result, how the workflow did it.

## And it is a service {#service}

There is a business here too. Making a presentation of someone's project, a site, infographics, a short article, is a service that providers in a region can offer, priced per project, finite and handed over, like the [villager services](/articles/villager-services-for-explorer-coders.html) we have described. Founders need to explain what they built to customers, investors and partners, and most never have the time to.

## How we recommend running build days {#recommend}

It fits how we recommend running these days, which I wrote up in [running an AI build event](/articles/running-an-ai-build-event.html): everyone ships something someone else can use. A presentation is how they show it, and how it reaches the people who were not in the room.

## What this does not claim {#limits}

- **It is a proposal.** No project from the day has a presentation yet.
- **The infographics were made by ChatGPT,** at my request, from our articles; they are illustrations, not photographs, and have small errors.
- **The talk's timing is from its own records,** as the case study sets out.
- **The participant who suggested this is not named;** the group chat is private.
- **My interest:** I would like to offer this, and I build the tools it uses.
