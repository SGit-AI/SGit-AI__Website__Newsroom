## In short {#in-short}

- **A brief for the agenticcells.ai team,** in public.
- **The site has good material** and the wrong first message.
- **Sovereignty is the message:** own it, control it, change it, run it anywhere, keep it.
- **The competitors cannot say it,** by their own terms.
- **Show the deliverables plainly,** with infographics.
- **Five homepage designs** to choose from.
- **A launch offer: £50 or €50,** against a platform's monthly rent.
- **A European launch,** in more languages.
- **Partners and examples stay,** under one idea.

## What the site does well today {#today}

agenticcells.ai, at version 0.1.269 tonight, already has most of what a buyer needs, built in two days by its agents ([you bring the business, we build the site](/articles/you-bring-the-business.html)):

- **Real examples,** named with consent: [Yiheng Wang](/articles/a-website-that-is-her.html), [Silvio Switha](/articles/a-portfolio-with-a-film-first.html), José Bota, Gabriel, and demos on the data, from IPMA's weather to an invented holiday-homes company ([sites on the data](/articles/sites-on-the-data.html)).
- **An honest offer:** £250 launch price, usually £500, one-off, no subscription, free evaluation sites every day, "nothing has been sold yet" stated plainly.
- **The partner programme:** a page each for freelancers, consultancies and firms, with four figures and published terms ([partners sell, we build](/articles/partners-sell-we-build.html)).
- **Ten languages and five designs** on every page, and a trust page.

## What is missing {#missing}

- **The first screen** still leads with a team photo and the phrase "a website like these"; its own simulated buyers kept asking what a "cell" is and who runs the site after hand-over.
- **Sovereignty is absent,** and it is our strongest argument ([sovereignty you can move](/articles/sovereignty-you-can-move.html)).
- **No lock-in is in the small print** ([no lock-in](/articles/no-lock-in.html)).
- **The deliverables are described in sentences,** not shown: what arrives, where, and what the owner can do with it.
- **The competitors are not named,** so a buyer cannot see why this is different from Wix or an AI builder ([rent or own](/articles/fliplet-and-the-website-cell.html)).

## The message {#message}

One idea, on every design: **a website you own, control and can take anywhere.** Under it, the six promises from [the pitch](/articles/the-pitch.html), led by sovereignty:

1. **You own it:** your GitHub repository, with its full history; yours within three months at the latest.
2. **You control it:** no platform, no subscription, no terms but yours; open code nobody can withdraw.
3. **You change it:** by talking to your own Claude, with guard rails, every change undoable.
4. **You can run it anywhere:** static files on any host, in any region; GitHub Pages for free.
5. **It looks better:** five designs by a team of agents, you choose.
6. **Almost nothing to attack, and it costs once:** no server code, no database, no cookies unless you add them ([static against the platforms](/articles/static-against-the-platforms.html)).

And the line that sets it apart: where it is hosted is not sovereignty; being able to move it is.

## The deliverables, shown {#deliverables}

!shot piv-deliver.webp | images/ | What arrives at hand-over: the repository, the live site, the designs, the CLAUDE.md, the policy, the ownership. | infographic

The site should show, not describe, what the owner receives:

- **A GitHub repository in your name,** with the site's files and every change since the first.
- **A live site on its address,** with HTTPS, on the owner's domain if they have one.
- **Five designs,** the chosen one live and the others kept.
- **A CLAUDE.md written for the owner,** so their Claude knows how to change the site safely.
- **A behaviour policy** for any agent that works on it, and checks that run before anything goes live.
- **Ownership:** the repository transferred to the owner within three months, sooner if they ask.

- **The tenets of owning a website,** as in the figure above: hold, control, change, run anywhere, keep.
- **Rent or own:** a platform account against a repository you hold ([rent or own](/articles/fliplet-and-the-website-cell.html)).
- **Can you leave?:** Wix, Squarespace and Fliplet in their own words, against the website cell ([no lock-in](/articles/no-lock-in.html)).
- **From brief to your own Claude:** the interview, the material, five designs, hand-over, and the milestone, the owner's first change ([start with an interview](/articles/start-with-an-interview.html)).

## Five homepage designs {#designs}

!shot piv-designs.webp | images/ | Five homepage directions for the sovereign website, each telling the same message in a different order and voice. | comparison

We make five designs for every site; agenticcells.ai should do the same for its own home page, with the same words and data in each, as its design switcher already allows:

- **Sovereign:** the headline "A website you own, control and can take anywhere", the five tenets as the first section, for anyone.
- **Six promises:** the pitch sentence set large, the promises as proof cards ([the pitch](/articles/the-pitch.html)), the safest default.
- **Stop renting your website:** the ledger of paying every month forever against paying once, for people leaving a platform.
- **Just say it:** a conversation with Claude changing the site as the hero, for technologists.
- **Proof:** a wall of the real sites, each linked and labelled, with the promises underneath, for people who believe what they can see.

Each should be professional enough to go to market, which means plain words, no internal terms on the first screen, and the offer and price visible without scrolling.

## A launch offer: £50, or €50 {#launch}

!shot piv-price.webp | images/ | A year of a platform against the website cell at its launch offer: monthly rent against one payment you keep. | comparison

To go to market, I want a launch offer that is impossible to ignore: **£250, at a promotional launch price of £50**, and **€50** on the Portuguese and other European sites. In the team's simulated buyers, five of six said yes at £50, four at £250 and one at £500; a simulation, not a survey, but it points the same way.

And the comparison sells itself. A year of Wix costs about £108 to £300 on its main plans; Squarespace from about $19 a month, Fliplet from $25 a month for publishing, every month, for a site you cannot take with you ([static against the platforms](/articles/static-against-the-platforms.html), [rent or own](/articles/fliplet-and-the-website-cell.html)). The website cell at £50 is less than a few months of rent, once, for a site that is yours on the first day; even at £250 it is about a year of a platform, paid once and kept forever.

Two honest notes belong beside it. The owner changes the site with their own Claude, so they need a Claude plan to edit it themselves, or a partner to do it for them; the platforms bundle their editor. And at £50 a partner's half is small, so partners set their own price for the build and earn on the care around it, as their terms already allow.

The arithmetic of the launch: a hundred sites at £50 is £5,000; a thousand is £50,000, and each one is a real customer, a real example and a real site running under behaviour policies. A hundred would already be a success story.

## A European launch {#europe}

The site is already in ten languages, Portuguese, French and Spanish each in two variants, plus German, Chinese and Japanese. For a European launch, add the other large European languages, Italian, Dutch and Polish first, with prices in euros on every European site, and the sovereignty message in each language ([sovereignty you can move](/articles/sovereignty-you-can-move.html)).

- **The partner programme,** unchanged, with the sovereignty line added to each page: the customer owns the repository, the partner keeps the relationship.
- **The free evaluations,** beside the new launch offer.
- **The examples,** with demos labelled as demos.

## What I am asking the team {#ask}

Build the next version of agenticcells.ai around this brief: the message, the deliverables, the infographics, five homepage designs, and the competitors named in their own words. Keep every claim linked to its evidence, as the site already does. When it is ready, I will take it to market, with the partners.

## What this does not claim {#limits}

- **This is a brief;** none of it is built yet, and the team's runs resume when I restart them.
- **The ownership promise is a soft requirement** for now.
- **The competitors are described from their own pages and standard terms.**
