## In short {#in-short}

- **Web Summit validated the technology, the direction and the need,** and the price points.
- **What was missing was a place to start:** something to buy now and use tomorrow.
- **RiskMandate is learnt by using it,** as Vicky Wang ran a newsroom without writing code.
- **Sovereign websites are where to start:** one-off, cheap, no lock-in, made by cells under policies.
- **Nobody deploys agents they cannot see and stop;** a site lets people touch them first.
- **B to C to B:** the personal customer is the way to the corporate one.

## What the summit validated {#validated}

At Web Summit we had validation of the technology, the path and the direction. Validation that there is a need. Validation that people understand the concept and the principle: they saw it in action, in [the X-ray](/articles/the-riskmandate-x-ray.html) and [the Galaxy](/articles/a-galaxy-of-agents.html), and they talked to people who had experienced it. The energy was positive. Even the price points held: no one recoiled, and even the main offerings, up to the £20,000 tier, drew good interest ([partners sell, we build](/articles/partners-sell-we-build.html)).

## What it showed was missing {#missing}

That is also an important signal. The people we met did not come to us and say: great, I will buy one of these now. Not because they did not see the value, or not mainly. They did not see where to start. There was no thing you could buy and use right now, no "I spend this many pounds, I get this, and I have it tomorrow". The larger offerings have a sales cycle, and that is fine, but we need a fast sale and a fast deployment as well.

## Why using it is the point {#using}

So much of RiskMandate is learnt by using it: you start, you execute, you get your head around it. The summit team had its momentum not only because of what we made, which was spectacular, but because of how it was made, how fast, and how deep. We built a full news publication ([PrompTugal.news](/articles/promptugal-news-the-newsroom-we-ran.html)): 73 articles in ten days, every one signed. And that gave Vicky the experience of running a newsroom without ever writing a line of code, as a project manager. She did not need to code. She delegated, to a development team that was controlled by behaviour policies.

That is the part that matters. It was possible because every agent was kept under control. I do a great deal of work this way now, and because the agents are limited in what they can do, I can delegate far more important tasks to them. Claude is an extraordinarily capable tool: at the same time a bicycle, a car, a Formula One car, a tank, a boat and probably a plane. You have to use the right capability for the job. By zooming in on the role, the function and the behaviour, we have a strong way of controlling what agents do ([feature behaviour policies](/articles/feature-behaviour-policies.html)). The hard part is how to start.

## From cells to websites {#cells}

That is why we started selling agentic teams: [AgentiCCells](/articles/starting-agenticcells.html), a cell of agents run under RiskMandate's policies. Then the question was what these cells can do. One of the things they can do is make our websites. And once I had wired in the automation, the GitHub repository, the address in AWS Route 53 and the rest of the settings, I realised we had a new product: websites made easily, with the identity that connects to them ([the website cell](/articles/the-website-cell.html)). There are still many details to sort out, as [the first round trip](/articles/the-first-round-trip.html) showed, but for the first time we have something people can try now and buy now.

## Every technology meets here {#intersection}

When I mapped its properties, I realised how strong this offering is. It ticks the boxes I always talk about for a startup: a one-off service, cheap to make and cheap to run ([sovereign websites, the investment case](/articles/sovereign-websites-the-investment-case.html)). And it is where everything I have been building meets:

- **No lock-in,** and the owner keeps ownership of the data and the systems ([sovereignty you can move](/articles/sovereignty-you-can-move.html)).
- **Version control,** which most websites do not have, and provenance on every change.
- **Claude or ChatGPT as the editor:** the content management system is now the AI that builds the site.
- **Agentic cells under behaviour policies** make it, run it and change it.
- **Others can build on top of it,** with the owner still in control.

It also puts me right in the conversation I have been having for years: sovereignty, determinism, provenance.

## Nobody deploys what they have not touched {#touch}

To buy a product, people have to trust it, and to trust it they have to try it. Nobody deploys technology in their organisation that they do not understand, do not control and cannot see. That is one of the problems with agents: they are powerful, but if you cannot see what they do, you will not deploy them, even if they are fine. You need the stop button, and you need to see it. What we had at the summit that was special was exactly that: start, stop, control, delegate, visualise and map ([an agentic console](/articles/an-agentic-console.html)).

So the hypothesis is simple. Someone will not buy or use this until they have experienced it, tried it and gained trust in it, until they say: yes, this works, I like it, I am going to use it for other things. A sovereign website is where that happens. The owner talks to the agents, sees their work, and sees what they are and are not allowed to do.

## B to C to B {#b2c2b}

Every person needs their own website and their own brand. You do not wait until you are looking for a job to build your brand. Everyone needs their own newsroom, to share what they know, and this is the way to do it. Which means that anyone who could be a corporate customer of RiskMandate can first be a personal customer of a sovereign website. B to C to B: the consumer product that people try now and buy now, which is critical for every startup, and the path from it into the company. A profitable way for people to learn, try and experience RiskMandate's policies, on something they all need.

## What this does not claim {#limits}

- **It is a hypothesis,** to be tested by how many site owners go on to use cells and policies at work.
- **The summit interest was conversations,** not orders, as this article says.
- **The handover still has friction,** as the first round trip recorded.
