## In short {#in-short}

- **RiskMandate has a song:** [Know the Reach, on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/track/1J6zgXwUBtbuUkqUMi6wI6), about three and a half minutes long.
- **Its music video is under way:** the first part is 53 seconds, made by Silvio Switha with AI, and you can [watch it on YouTube](https://youtu.be/BjN-K-C2fsw).
- **The record is complete enough to count:** 43 generated images, 16 motion clips and 21 edited exports.
- **The human part is the direction:** about 2,300 words in the available chats, shaping story, accuracy, sound, type and explanation.
- **Paid generation stayed small:** 1,236 Runway credits, with existing images reused and one eight-second test first.
- **This is part one.** The rest of the song is still to be filmed.

## The song {#song}

Know the Reach is RiskMandate's song, released in 2026 and [on Spotify now](https://open.spotify.com/track/1J6zgXwUBtbuUkqUMi6wI6). RiskMandate is about three words: what an AI agent can **reach**, what it was asked to do, its **mandate**, and the **gap** between the two. The song puts those words to music, and the video puts them on a screen.

## The first part of the video {#video}

The first part runs for 53.4 seconds, cut from 14 new clips to the original song. It follows one protagonist through Lisbon, from night to morning. The software agent is shown as golden light, working on a specific email, specific tasks and specific resources, and there is a human reviewer who is clearly a different person from the agent. The opening instrumental is used to explain the product in English, and small labels then name what is on screen without competing with the sung subtitles.

[Watch the first part on YouTube](https://youtu.be/BjN-K-C2fsw), and listen to the whole song [on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/track/1J6zgXwUBtbuUkqUMi6wI6).

## How it was made {#made}

Silvio made it with an AI assistant, an image generator and Runway for motion, and kept every output. The production audit of 14 November counts what the saved project holds:

- **43 generated images:** 34 options for the base images, 3 clearer images of the agent, 5 corridor variants, and 1 clean plate for compositing.
- **16 generated motion clips,** 108.7 seconds in all: the 14 clips of part one, an eight-second pilot and a five-second motion test.
- **21 edited exports,** 24 minutes 44 seconds of running time, across seven families: silent, with sound, previews, font tests, technical and editorial alternatives. Footage is reused across versions, so these are not 21 films.
- **About 2,300 words of human input** in 78 messages, and about 7,650 words of replies from the assistant.

Paid generation was kept small. 49 existing production images were moved into Runway instead of being generated again; one eight-second clip was tested before any more were made; part one had a budget per shot, and came to 1,140 credits on top of the pilot's 96. Every change to fonts, captions and labels after that was made locally, with no new generations.

## Eight decisions {#decisions}

!shot ktr-decisions.webp | images/ | The eight decisions the production audit records, from real sources to controlled spend. | concept

What strikes me reading the audit is how much of the work was judgement, not generation. Three of the decisions are ones I would want from any team:

- **Accurate claims.** The video shows reach, mandate and the gap, and does not claim that RiskMandate blocks attacks or enforces permissions automatically. A product film that overclaims is worse than none.
- **The song leads.** The original song stays the timing reference, and its existing caption timings were recovered, instead of changing the music to fit the pictures.
- **Readable on a phone.** Four font families were compared, at 44, 42 and 40 pixels in 720p, and Inter SemiBold won, at the equivalent of 40 pixels in 720p and 60 in 1080p.

## What this does not claim {#limits}

- **The figures cover the saved project and the three main chats** up to 14 November; earlier sessions are not counted.
- **The 2,300 words are the creative direction,** including pasted briefs and quoted material, not the whole original brief, the music, or every earlier conversation.
- **The 21 exports are versions,** and the 16 clips are the only newly generated motion; the audit's 464 gallery entries are references, snapshots and thumbnails, not new images.
- **Runway credits are counted for the pilot and part one only.**
