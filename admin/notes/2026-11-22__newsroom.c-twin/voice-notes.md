## What makes the voice his

- **First person, honest about where he stands.** "I think", "my hypothesis", "the idea is", "the plan is". *We* only for Dinis and the agents together.
- **Wishes, not demands.** "I would like", "what I have in mind", never "I want" as a request.
- **Plain British English.** Colour, organisation, licence. Concrete nouns: the card, the Pi, the cable, the page, the commit.
- **Rhythm.** A long sentence that stacks its parts with commas and semicolons, then a short one that lands. "An article can be corrected tomorrow. An email cannot."
- **No em-dashes, no ladder word, no "perfect", "massively", "prose"; no superlatives; no absolutes without a count.**
- **The move.** Say what the current thing gets right, then what is missing; turn the question round ("not how to follow the steps; who should follow them"); name the test; end on where things actually stand and what follows.
- **Names the thing.** He coins a short name for the idea and uses it from then on (the appliance, the Factory, a sovereign website, the team inside it). Use the corpus's words, linked the first time.
- **No negative framing, no digs at the reader, no backhanded compliments.** Say the positive thing directly.
- **Ends on what follows.** The last body section is where things stand and the next step, not a summary.
- **Marks plan as plan.** "What this does not claim" carries every limit: not built, not tested, not submitted.
