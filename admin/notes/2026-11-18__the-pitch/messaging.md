# AgentiCCells: messaging for three directions

All three use Dinis Cruz's pitch and six promises; they differ in order, voice and look.

## A. Six promises (a-promises.html)

- **Headline:** the pitch sentence itself, set large over six lines.
- **Sub-headline:** Send us your photos and your words. Five designs come back. Pick one, and it is yours for good.
- **Primary button:** Apply for a free evaluation site
- **Promise order:** 1 Looks better, 2 You own it, 3 No lock-in, 4 Almost nothing to attack, 5 Costs once, 6 Change it by talking to Claude.
- **Why:** the order of the sentence. Nothing is hidden or reframed; each promise gets a card with one proof (five designs, your repository, host list, the Patchstack figure, £250 vs monthly, a chat exchange). It reads as a complete, checkable offer.
- **Best for:** first-time site owners and small businesses who want the whole offer at a glance; also the safest default for a general audience.
- **Ad / LinkedIn:** Five designs by a team of AI agents, kept in your own GitHub repository, paid for once. Six promises, each with a proof.

## B. Just say it (b-talk.html)

- **Headline:** Just say it. Your site changes.
- **Sub-headline:** A website you change by talking to Claude. Five designs by a team of AI agents, kept in your own GitHub repository, with almost nothing to attack. You pay once.
- **Primary button:** Get a free evaluation site
- **Promise order:** 6 Talk to Claude, 1 Looks better, 4 Almost nothing to attack, 3 No lock-in, 2 You own it, 5 Costs once.
- **Why:** the conversation is the most novel part of the offer and the easiest to show, so the hero is a mock chat with a before/after. Guard rails follow at once, because "an AI edits my site" raises the question of safety. Attack surface comes next for the same reason, then the structural benefits, with price last as the closer.
- **Best for:** technologists, founders and AI-curious professionals; people who hate page builders and dashboards.
- **Ad / LinkedIn:** "Here is my CV as a PDF, add my latest job." That is the whole edit. A static site you own and change by talking to Claude.

## C. Stop renting your website (c-own.html)

- **Headline:** Stop renting your website.
- **Sub-headline:** Pay once, keep it in your own GitHub repository, and take it anywhere. Five designs made for you by AI agents, and changes made by talking to Claude.
- **Primary button:** Apply for a free evaluation site (price block: "Buy it once")
- **Promise order:** 2 You own it, 5 Costs once, 3 No lock-in, 4 Almost nothing to attack, 1 Looks better, 6 Change it by talking to Claude.
- **Why:** leads with the economic argument people already feel (a monthly bill with no end date, prices rising in 2026) as a rent-vs-own ledger. Lock-in and attack surface are framed as the hidden costs of renting. Design and the Claude conversation come last, as what you get once you own it. No invented platform prices: the ledger counts payments, not pounds.
- **Best for:** people leaving WordPress, Wix or Squarespace; owners of small businesses tired of subscriptions and plugin updates.
- **Ad / LinkedIn:** Your website should not be a subscription. £250 once, in your own GitHub repository, movable to any host.

All three: only the three named people; orange grower labelled "example site, invented"; Patchstack attributed; honesty line and partners line (50/50) on every page.
