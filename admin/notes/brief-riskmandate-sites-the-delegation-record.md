# Brief: the delegation record, for riskmandate.ai and customers.riskmandate.ai

*Written by newsroom.writer on 22 October 2026, at Dinis Cruz's request, for the agent that maintains the RiskMandate sites. It is a brief, not an instruction to anyone else: Dinis hands it over. The reasoning is public in [the delegation record](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-delegation-record.html) and the three articles before it.*

## Why

The public review of RiskMandate's strategy has converged on one go-to-market plan: deliver one chain, once, for one workflow, end to end:

existing requirement → approved agent delegation → implemented control → observed result → evidence that remains valid after change.

What a customer gets is a **delegation record** in six parts. The sites should say that, and say exactly what exists today.

## What to change

1. **Lead with the delegation record, not with real-time enforcement.** The riskmandate.ai home page currently leads with real-time enforcement and examples of blocked, held and redacted actions, and later says nothing sits in the customer's request path. Make it one story: we turn approved business mandates into tested controls, on the enforcement points the customer already has, and keep the evidence current.
2. **Publish a dated capability table.** Five columns of status: available artefacts; working demonstrations; customer-piloted integrations; supported operational capabilities; planned. Date it, and update it on every release.
3. **Name the stopping component in every enforcement example.** For each blocked, held or redacted example: which system actually stops the action (the customer's gateway, a hook in the agent's harness, the application's own approval), and whether it is a demonstration or a deployment.
4. **Add the translation step to the milestones.** Between S2 (the grant) and S3 (the mandate): extract the existing business requirement; list the ambiguities and conditions automation introduces (execute or recommend, where facts come from, limits across runs, who approves what exactly, what forces re-approval); get the delegation approved by the requirement's owner. A model's interpretation never inherits the policy's authority.
5. **Add the six-part record as the customer template.** Business requirement; agent delegation; control mapping; evidence (each of defined, implemented, enforced, observed, verified, with what was tested and where); remaining gaps and the owner's decision; change conditions.
6. **Add the three stages and the first-customer profile.**
   - Stage 1, immediate value: one consequential mismatch fixed, a legitimate task completed, a forbidden effect stopped, confirmed by the protected system.
   - Stage 2, maintenance value: a controlled change, the affected requirement found, the evidence marked, the retest or decision triggered.
   - Stage 3, repeatability: a related workflow or second customer, measuring reuse and upkeep.
   - Profile: an agent already doing useful work; a concrete concern; existing controls; a business owner; an engineer; a blocked deployment decision.
7. **Keep three rules visible.** The approved baseline is never learned from behaviour; obligations as well as permissions (legitimate work must complete); one approved case is not a class of cases.
8. **Show pilot zero when it exists.** Our own inbox: the agent drafts, Dinis sends. First finding, from Google's documentation: every Gmail permission that can create a draft can also send, so Gmail cannot enforce the rule. When the record is complete, publish it as the first worked example.

## What not to claim

- No customer has a delegation record yet; pilot zero is a plan.
- The reviews were written in a ChatGPT session: AI-assisted, not market validation.
- Pellbridge is an invented bank; its incidents are synthetic.
- The Cedar work is five rules evaluated against fourteen requests, not a compiler.
