# Notes: launching-the-sovereign-website-appliance (19 November 2026)

Dinis, in writer-10's session: Android first; his Web Summit tablet as the prototype; launch alone and reach out to companies later; tiers of about £500 and £1,000 where "we spend roughly 250 quid on the tablet" and "about 500 quid", the best available at the time, with examples; a European tablet; a £50 supporter tier for the site without a tablet; no T-shirt; Raspberry Pi as an option, chosen at order time ("we spend 250 quid on hardware in one option, we spend 500 quid on hardware in the other one"). Prices read by a research agent on 19 November from John Lewis, OnePlus, Murena and The Pi Hut (Argos, Currys, Amazon, mi.com and lenovo.com refused); the Yoga Tab and OnePlus Pad 3 current prices unconfirmed. The earlier cost-plus-50% idea was replaced at his request.

## Revision, 19 November, evening

Dinis, with two Amazon UK screenshots of iRasptek's Raspberry Pi 5 starter kit (8GB/128GB £229.99, 16GB/128GB £380.99, 16GB/256GB £397.99): every device should run AI models, and websites should be created within the device itself; separate the prices into four hardware products (Pi and tablet, each standard and more expensive) with about a £500 margin; "some people might even just want to buy this just for the setup"; "we need proper sovereignty". Tiers first revised to £750/£750/£900/£1,000; Dinis then preferred the earlier shape, so the final tiers are £50, £400 Pi, £500 tablet, £900 Pi Pro, £1,000 tablet Pro. Then: local LLMs only on the Pro tiers ("I don't think it would be realistic to run models on" the others); on the £400 and £500 tiers the website is built for them, on the Pro tiers on the device itself. Then: the appliances launch officially once the Kickstarter finishes. The Volla tablet moved to a European alternative at the Pro price without the local model, since its chip is slow for AI. The delivery address shown in his screenshot is not reproduced. AI on a Pi is marked as to be tested.
