# Memo: selling at the right altitude (4 November 2026)

Dinis Cruz, to the newsroom writer, voice message, lightly cleaned.

An article about a couple of key concepts, the main one being selling at the right altitude. A cybersecurity
company of one to five people that wants to provide local services. Create a vault with a bad, a medium and a
good example, starting with bad. The bad example is a website that tries to sell all sorts of security
services: assessments, SOC assessments, a SOC, risk management. Very grandiose, the typical AI-developed
website with massive claims, very technical, built for a cybersecurity service provider. The more grandiose
it is, the more you know it is a small company; bigger companies feel different.

The tragic symmetry: the customer who reads the site and might give them credibility has no idea what
cybersecurity is, does not understand half the terms, has different problems, will never buy a SOC, does not
know what a SOC or PCI is; at best knows ransomware. And the more security-savvy, tech-savvy or bigger the
company, the less likely they are to buy from this firm rather than an established one with a bigger team and
a pedigree. So challenge every product on the site: have you actually sold it, and how realistic is it? Not
because you did it before, could do it, or ChatGPT or Claude wrote a nice page about it. Synthetic users show
this: "I have no idea what this is."

The good website names the individuals, the team, photos, what we do, the region we live in, how we operate,
the size of the team, why we are trustworthy, and focuses on services the customer cares about. If you are
small, your audience is SMEs, and you can now scale with agents doing continuous checks. Sell the
fundamentals: backups, 2FA, Google Workspace and Office 365 set up correctly, EDR, no passwords lying around,
data managed properly, not sharing confidential data with half the planet. A business proposition: you can
operate through ransomware; the fundamentals; the basics of GDPR; your AI agents controlled, with agent and
system behaviour policies. Infographics to explain it well.
