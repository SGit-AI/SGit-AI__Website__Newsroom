# Memo: the narrator's voice in the book (4 November 2026)

Dinis Cruz, to the newsroom writer, after reading RC2 of One Human, Nine Agents, Nineteen Hours (the full
draft, 111 pages). Transcribed and lightly cleaned.

---

First message: can you see on this book how my narrator voice is not as good as what we have here, in the
newsroom? Start with my voice; maybe later the other agents. Don't make the full comments yet; let's have
a conversation first.

The newsroom writer's reading (agreed by Dinis as "exactly the analysis"): the narrator reads the record
instead of thinking out loud; its rhythm is an agent's (short paired sentences, "That is the point", triads);
it hands conclusions to the historian; it speaks the team's vocabulary; the energy was removed. Cause: the
narrator was built from the repository record, with the rule that every line rests on a source, so the
evidence drives and the voice became a caption. To be fair, that agent did not have as many examples of my
voice as the newsroom has.

Second message:

Yes, that is exactly what happened, and the example rewrite is the right direction. Review the first set of
pages, the first section, and create a markdown file I can give to the book team.

We are going to add a page to explain the logic: I am the narrator of this story, but we need an
introduction that explains this. I was trying to create a book that is also creative. I have all this
technology, all these workflows, all these agents. And the agentic workflow that is building the book is
pretty spectacular; we need to really show that off. After this, maybe a pass on infographics or examples to
add to the book in specific places, because we are trying to be creative with the way we build it and tell
the story.

My voice is me looking back, with opinions on what I learned. It is not a documentary. I am narrating the
story from now, from hindsight, and connecting the dots: to my past work, to the other presentations, to the
fact that a lot of this was first an experiment in the Black Hat presentation, then the other
presentations, then the ruv.sgit presentation. Then we hand over to the agents, who speak more in the
format the book has now.

We might want to make changes to the agents later, but first focus on my voice. Start from the beginning:
an introduction and one or two chapters. I want to nail the first part of the book.

---

Third message (feedback on the first pass): almost there, one detail. "Because it is easy to miss" is a
phrase I have objected to several times: it passes judgement. It either offends the people who missed it
(you make me feel slow) or the people who did not (yes, I knew). I try not to say it, so it should be one of
the guidelines: the narrator does not say it. Done: removed from the introduction, "underrated" removed for
the same reason, and rule 11 added to the style sheet.
