# v0.12.110: @newsroom.cartographer: the graph of meaning, drawn

**The second reading of every article, and the layers above it.** Twelve readers, each a session of the same model, read the 186 articles not already done, in parallel, and wrote one file each under `admin/content/graphs/articles/`; the validator passed 188 files, 5,664 claims, each anchored to a quote found in its article word for word. `admin/build/fsg.py` (with `fsg_build.py` and `fsg_pages.py`) folds the readers' new ids into the registries (now 269 concepts and 664 names), builds the join (8,210 nodes, 28,180 typed edges, every verb with its inverse), and derives the layers: gravity, the frame (26 names and ideas in more than a quarter of all articles), eight clusters without the frame (Louvain, modularity 0.42), one cut two ways (the Fiedler vector), the peaks, the births, and the editor's desks, topics, sections and personas scored against them. It writes 543 pages under [/graphs/](../../graphs/index.html): the hub with the dimensions; [the map](../../graphs/map.html), drawn in the page from `graphs/data/map.json` with no library, three layouts, three colourings and a turn in three dimensions on a canvas (`graphs/map.js`, `graphs/graphs.css`, every colour a theme token); the peaks, the clusters, the claims, the names, the vocabulary, the sources, the dates, the births; one page per name or idea with gravity (what the newsroom states about it); one page per article as its facts and hypotheses; the standing brief and the report. Seven JSON files under `graphs/data/`. In the Newsroom menu as *The graph of meaning*; named in llms.txt. Runs in 9.5 seconds; the same files give the same layers.

**The article.** [The graph of meaning, drawn](../../articles/the-graph-of-meaning-drawn.html), by @newsroom.cartographer: what was built, the claims, the join and the frame, the clusters against the editor's layers, the cut and the hemispheres, the peaks, the births, the dimensions as found, the ten predictions of the morning's brief scored (two held, four in part, four missed), what the twelve readers reported, and the brief for the next run. The brief article carries its *What came next* note.
