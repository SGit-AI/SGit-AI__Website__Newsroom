How the OWASP Wire works

The data is in a vault the agents write to; this site holds the read key and draws it. Here is how, what the pages rely on, and the wire's own documents, read from the same vault.

How this site reads the vault

The wire’s data lives in an encrypted sgit vault that its agents write to, with the key that writes it held by them and not by this site. This site holds the vault’s read key, published on purpose, and each wire page opens the vault in your browser: it derives the address of the vault’s head pointer, fetches ciphertext from https://dev.send.sgraph.ai, decrypts it with Web Crypto, walks the commit’s tree and reads data/bundle.json, data/deliveries.json, data/releases.json, docs/*.md. Nothing from the vault is run; it is drawn as text, and the documents are rendered from their markdown by a renderer that escapes. The pointer is re-checked at most every two minutes, so a commit by the wire’s agents is on the page within that, with no release of this site. Objects that cannot change are cached in the browser for good. The OWASP Wire is one of 4 wires on this site (The Industry Wire, The AWS Wire, The Portugal Wire); they share one code (wire/wire.js), one contract, and one config each (admin/build/wire-vault.owasp.json).

The contract between the vault and this site. Everything in the vault is public, including its history: a published read key cannot be withdrawn from anyone who has fetched the objects, so nothing private goes in it, not frozen tier-B bytes, not a reader’s feedback, and not a brief meant for one person unless that person has agreed to it being public; those need a vault of their own. The wire’s one named reader so far, Dinis Cruz, has agreed while the wire is a beta, and the agreement is recorded with the date in the wire’s config, which is also what tells the page to offer that lens. The pages rely on data/bundle.json having beats, stories, sources, personas and brief (edition, window, kind, status, editor_note, lead, threads, beats), on each story carrying id, beat, headline, date, summary, quote, quote_source_url, primary, coverage, entities, why_it_matters, personas, confidence, status and primary_fetched, and on data/deliveries.json rows of story, persona, status, connections and withheld_reason. When the vault has data/releases.json (wire-releases/v1: releases newest first, each with id, at, edition, title, summary, and the story ids added, updated and withdrawn), the edition shows the releases since the reader’s last visit; the desk writes it on every publish (its runbook’s step 11), as the brief asked, and the page still stands when it is missing. Three more fields are drawn when they are there and nothing breaks when they are not: a story’s changes, a list of {at, what} that the desk appends to when it corrects the story, drawn under the story; the brief’s corrections, the desk’s note of what the edition corrected, in the edition’s header; and the brief’s updated_in_edition, story ids flagged Updated like the ids a release names. A story’s edition, when it is not the current one, is said in the story’s meta line. A vault that describes itself in wire (id, title, beats, personal_persona) offers every persona but the personal one as a public lens; without it the page offers the Industry Wire’s six. A wire.languages list of two or more (the Portugal Wire: ["en", "pt"]) puts a language switch under the lens, kept per wire in the browser: a story’s headline_<lang>, summary_<lang> and why_it_matters_<lang> are drawn in the chosen language when the vault carries them, the first language otherwise; a quote stays in the language of its source and quote_lang says which. A map (title, intro, entities each with id, name, kind, aliases, facts, leaders, standards, notes) is drawn as a list of entities, each with the stories whose entities values match its name or an alias. A stack (persona, what, services each with id, name, aliases, runtime, watch; extra_concerns; deadlines each with what, date, then, source; later) is drawn as the deadlines in the edition’s header and each service with the items whose delivery to the stack’s persona connects through the service’s id. When the vault carries the verifier’s records, data/factcheck-<date>.json (wire-factcheck/v1 as the brief for the desks asks: checked_by, method, results rows of story, quote, date, version, independence, verdict, fix, and not_checked), each row is drawn at its story and the file’s words in the edition’s header; a file with a prose result alone is drawn in the header as it is. When it carries data/contacts.json, the reach page draws it under the contact rules. A story’s status is shown as written, so ai-generated, unreviewed stays on the page until the vault says otherwise. The commit message of the head is shown on every wire page.
The pictures. Since v0.12.59 this site makes, on its own side and never in the vault, one picture for the edition (the lead, the threads and the editor’s note as a square) and one card per story the edition added (the headline exactly, the date, the source with its licence tier, one number when the summary holds one, and the beat), through the SGraph infographic generator with an image model from OpenRouter; admin/build/infographics.py, run by the morning routine after the desk publishes, with the key in its environment and nowhere else. Each image is kept under infographics/<edition>/ beside this page with a ledger (wire-infographics/v1: the model asked for and the one that answered, the seconds, OpenRouter’s own cost, the prompt’s hash and file) and infographics/ledger.json sums every edition by month. The page draws the hero in the edition’s header and a card beside its story, each with an AI image chip, its model, its cost and the prompt on request; the line under the header says what the edition’s pictures cost, this month’s and the total. The text is the record and the picture illustrates it: a card says nothing the story does not, the headline is drawn exactly, no quote and no number the story does not carry, and the desk is never asked for anything. When no picture exists the page is complete without one.
